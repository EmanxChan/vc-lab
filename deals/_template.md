## 1. The one-liner
What they do, in the founder's own words. If you can't write it in one sentence, you don't
understand it yet.

## 2. Why now
What changed in the world that makes this possible today and not three years ago. A company with
no "why now" is usually a feature waiting for a platform shift that already happened.

## 3. Team
Why *these* people. Do they have founder-market fit, or did they pick this market? Have they
worked together before — 68.6% of multi-founder unicorns had co-founders who previously worked or
studied together.

## 4. Market
Size, and more importantly shape. Who is the buyer, what do they currently spend on this, and how
hard is it to reach them.

## 5. Thesis fit
Does this actually rebuild human trust — bridges, community bonds, belonging — or am I stretching
because I like the founder? Be strict. This is the section that stops the portfolio drifting.

## 6. The bet
The single thing that has to be true for this to work. One sentence. If there are three, the
company hasn't focused yet.

## 7. Terms and ownership
Instrument, cap or price, check size, resulting ownership, and whether pro rata is available.

## 8. The anti-memo
The strongest possible case against, written honestly, as if I were paid to kill it. What would a
smart skeptic say? What would make me wrong? If I can't write this, I have enthusiasm rather than
conviction.
