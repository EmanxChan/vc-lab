## Why it matters

Early-stage companies have no brand equity, and taglines only work when the reader already knows
who you are. A famous company can afford three abstract words. A company nobody has heard of has
one sentence to explain itself before the tab closes.

It's a small thing that reveals a bigger one — a founder who leads with a mood usually hasn't
decided what the specific claim is.

## What it looks like in practice

The value proposition should name the outcome, ideally with a number, and be specific enough
that a competitor couldn't put it on their own homepage. If it works equally well for three
other companies, it isn't saying anything.

The tagline can come later, once enough people know the company that a short phrase has
something to attach to.

## Where it goes wrong

- Leading with an abstract tagline before anyone knows what you sell.
- Writing a value proposition that describes the product rather than the outcome.
- Using industry language the buyer doesn't actually use.
- Trying to cover every use case in one sentence, which produces a sentence about nothing.
