## Why it matters

DPI is distributions to paid-in — cash actually returned divided by cash actually contributed.
It's the number experienced LPs care about most, because it can't be marked up.

A DPI of 1.0 means LPs have their money back. Everything above that is real profit, and
everything below it is still a promise.

## What it looks like in practice

DPI takes years to become meaningful. A fund four years in with a DPI near zero is normal; the
same fund at year nine is a problem.

The pairing with TVPI tells the story. A high TVPI with a low DPI means the value is all on
paper — which may be fine, and may be a portfolio carrying stale marks from a different market.

## Where it goes wrong

- Ignoring DPI because TVPI looks good.
- Comparing DPI across funds without adjusting for age.
- Treating recallable distributions as permanent DPI.
- Assuming a fund with strong marks will convert them into distributions.
