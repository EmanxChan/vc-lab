## Why it matters

Traction is whatever evidence exists that people want this, and what counts changes entirely by
stage. At pre-seed it might be three design partners; at Series A it's retained revenue growing
through a repeatable channel.

The word does a lot of concealing. "Traction" in a deck can mean signed contracts or a waitlist,
and the difference is the whole investment decision.

## What it looks like in practice

The useful hierarchy runs from cheapest signal to most expensive: interest, signups, usage,
payment, retention, referral. Each step costs the customer more, which is exactly why each one
carries more information.

Absolute numbers beat percentages at this stage. "Grew 300%" from four customers to twelve is a
different claim from the same percentage at scale, and the percentage is usually chosen to
obscure that.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting percentages without the absolute numbers underneath.
- Counting unpaid pilots, waitlists, or LOIs as traction.
- Presenting cumulative totals, which can only ever go up.
- Showing acquisition without retention, which is the number that actually matters.
