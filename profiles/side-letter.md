## Why it matters

Side letters are how individual investors get terms the rest of the round doesn't. They're
normal and often reasonable — a large investor asking for enhanced reporting, a strategic one
asking for a specific right.

The risk is accumulation. A stack of side letters granting different rights to different parties
creates obligations nobody tracks, and one MFN clause can propagate every concession to
everyone.

## What it looks like in practice

Common contents are enhanced information rights, guaranteed pro rata, MFN protection, and
specific approval rights. In fund formation the same mechanism appears between GPs and
individual LPs, often on fees.

The discipline is a single register of what has been granted to whom. Companies discover during
diligence that they made commitments they no longer remember.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting side letters without keeping a central record of them.
- Including MFN, so every later concession propagates backwards.
- Giving governance rights in a side letter that contradict the main documents.
- Assuming side letters expire at the next round unless they say so.
