## Why it matters

In markets where being wrong is expensive — healthcare, finance, anything regulated — buyers
don't trust vendors, they trust peers. A reference from someone in the same seat at a similar
organisation removes the risk that no demo can.

It's also why beachheads work. Concentration in one segment means every new sale has a credible
reference available, and the sales cycle shortens with each one.

## What it looks like in practice

Good references are built deliberately: pick customers who got a measurable result, ask while
the result is fresh, make it easy, and don't overuse them. A reference who has taken nine calls
this quarter starts declining.

The strongest version isn't a call at all — it's a customer who brings you up unprompted in
their own network. That happens when the result was genuinely notable, not when you asked
nicely.

## Where it goes wrong

- Asking a customer to be a reference before they've seen a result.
- Burning out one enthusiastic reference on every deal.
- Only having references in a segment you're leaving.
- Treating a logo on a website as a reference. It isn't — a person on a call is.
