## Why it matters

An odds ratio compares the likelihood of an outcome in one group against another — how much more
likely a founder from a given background is to build a successful company. It's how the research
on founder backgrounds is usually expressed.

It matters because it forces a distinction between a signal that is real and one that is merely
visible. Many things correlate with success weakly and are treated as though they were decisive.

## What it looks like in practice

The interpretation trap is base rates. A background with three times the odds of producing a
successful founder still produces very few of them in absolute terms, because the base rate is
tiny.

It's also correlation. Selective institutions and companies select for people who were already
likely to succeed, which means the ratio measures the selection as much as the experience.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reading an odds ratio as a probability.
- Ignoring base rates, which makes small ratios look decisive.
- Treating correlation in the data as causation in the individual.
- Using population statistics to make a judgment about a specific founder in front of you.
