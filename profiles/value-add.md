## Why it matters

Value add is what an investor contributes beyond capital. It's the most claimed and least
verified thing in venture — every fund says it, and founders can tell within a quarter whether
it's real.

The honest version is narrow. Most investors are genuinely useful at two or three specific
things and unhelpful at everything else, and knowing which is more valuable than claiming
breadth.

## What it looks like in practice

The contributions that founders actually rate are hiring, customer introductions, help with the
next round, and being available when something is going wrong. Strategic advice ranks lower than
investors think.

The test is what portfolio founders say when asked by another founder, unprompted. That
reference is the only real measure, and it's the one that produces future dealflow.

## Where it goes wrong

- Claiming broad value add rather than being specific about two or three things.
- Offering advice when the founder needed an introduction.
- Being available during diligence and unreachable afterwards.
- Measuring value add by activity rather than by what founders say to each other.
