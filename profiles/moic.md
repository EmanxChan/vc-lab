## Why it matters

MOIC is the simplest performance measure: what a position is worth divided by what was paid for
it. No time adjustment, no fee adjustment — just the multiple.

Its simplicity is both the strength and the weakness. A 3x MOIC over four years and a 3x over
twelve are the same number and completely different investments, which is why MOIC and IRR are
always read together.

## What it looks like in practice

MOIC is usually quoted per position or across a portfolio at the gross level — before fees and
carry. TVPI is the LP-level equivalent, calculated after fees on contributed capital.

The gap between gross MOIC and net TVPI is the cost of the fund structure, and it's substantial.
A gross 3x can be a net 2.2x once fees and carry are taken.

## Where it goes wrong

- Comparing gross MOIC to net TVPI, which flatters the fund.
- Quoting MOIC without the holding period.
- Calculating it on the initial cheque rather than total invested including follow-ons.
- Reading an unrealised MOIC as though the value were established.
