## Why it matters

Every revenue projection rests on this number, and it's the number founders are most confidently
wrong about. Stated willingness to pay runs far above actual, because agreeing in a conversation
costs nothing.

It's also the fastest way to test whether a problem is real. Someone who pays before the product
is finished has told you more than a hundred interviews.

## What it looks like in practice

Real signal comes from commitments with a cost attached: a deposit, a signed LOI, a pilot with a
purchase order, a card on file. Even a small payment sorts genuine interest from courtesy.

The other reliable source is what they pay today — for the incumbent, for the contractor, for
the internal headcount doing it manually. That budget already exists and doesn't need to be
created, which makes it the easiest money in the market.

## Where it goes wrong

- Asking directly. The answer is a social response, not an economic one.
- Testing price with existing enthusiasts, who are the least representative buyers you have.
- Anchoring on competitor pricing when your product does a different job.
- Setting price low to reduce friction, then discovering low price attracted customers who
  churn.
