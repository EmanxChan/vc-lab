## Why it matters

It's a marketplace with the hardest possible constraint: liquidity has to exist *right now*, in
one place. A restaurant marketplace can tolerate a slow match. A delivery one can't — the
customer leaves in four minutes.

That constraint drives the economics. Guaranteeing availability means paying for idle supply,
and idle supply is pure cost. Most on-demand businesses spend years trying to get utilisation
high enough to stop subsidising it.

## What it looks like in practice

The levers are density, prediction, and pricing. Density means enough demand in a small enough
area that workers stay busy. Prediction means knowing Friday needs triple supply before Friday.
Surge pricing is the blunt instrument that clears the market when the first two fail.

Worker classification is a live legal and cost question in most jurisdictions, and a
reclassification changes the unit economics of the entire business, not just its compliance
posture.

## Where it goes wrong

- Expanding to a new city before utilisation works in the first one.
- Subsidising both sides indefinitely and calling the resulting growth product-market fit.
- Treating worker classification as a legal footnote rather than a model risk.
- Measuring average utilisation, which hides that mornings are dead and evenings are short.
