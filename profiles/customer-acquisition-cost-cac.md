## Why it matters

CAC is half of the central question in any growth business: does a customer generate more than
they cost. It's also the number most often understated, because the honest version includes
salaries and the flattering version doesn't.

The trend matters more than the level. CAC that rises as you scale means you've exhausted the
cheap channel and are buying progressively less interested customers — which is the normal path,
and the thing to know about before raising a round to accelerate it.

## What it looks like in practice

Fully loaded means all sales and marketing costs: salaries, commissions, tooling, content,
events, and ad spend, over the period that produced the customers. Blended CAC mixes organic and
paid; paid CAC isolates what you actually control.

Segment it. One channel at $400 and another at $6,000 average to something meaningless, and the
decision you want to make is about the channels, not the average.

## Where it goes wrong

- Counting only ad spend.
- Using blended CAC to justify scaling paid acquisition.
- Attributing customers to the period the spend happened rather than the period they closed.
- Comparing your CAC to a benchmark from a company with a different ACV.
