## Why it matters

Board composition is the most consequential governance term in a financing, and this is the
document that fixes it. It converts an annual political question into a settled structure.

It usually also carries the drag-along, which means it's the document that can compel a
shareholder to sell in an acquisition they oppose. Two very different powers living in the same
agreement is a common surprise.

## What it looks like in practice

The typical early structure is a small board with defined seats — founders, lead investor, one
independent. The independent seat matters more than founders expect, because it decides
deadlocks.

Board seats are sticky. An investor seat granted at seed usually persists through later rounds,
and a fund that becomes a small holder can still occupy a seat years later unless the agreement
ties the seat to continued ownership.

## Where it goes wrong

- Growing the board faster than the company, so every decision needs a large meeting.
- Granting a permanent seat rather than tying it to an ownership threshold.
- Not noticing the drag-along inside the same document.
- Leaving the independent seat unfilled, so deadlocks have no resolution.
