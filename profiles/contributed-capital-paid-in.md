## Why it matters

Contributed capital is the money LPs have actually paid in, and it's the denominator for the
metrics that matter. DPI and TVPI are both calculated against it.

It includes management fees, which is the part that surprises people. Fees are drawn from LP
capital, so a fund that has called 30% of commitments has typically invested less than that into
companies.

## What it looks like in practice

Paid-in capital equals capital called for investments plus capital called for fees and expenses.
The gap between paid-in and invested is the fee drag, and over a fund's life it's substantial —
a 2% annual fee over ten years is 20% of committed capital.

That's why a fund needs to return meaningfully more than 1x invested capital just to return 1x
to LPs.

## Where it goes wrong

- Calculating TVPI against invested capital rather than paid-in.
- Forgetting that fees are drawn from LP capital and reduce what's available to invest.
- Comparing funds at different points in their lives without adjusting for how much is paid in.
- Assuming paid-in equals cost basis of the portfolio. It doesn't — fees sit in between.
