## Why it matters

The LPA is the constitution of the fund. It sets the fee, the carry, the waterfall, the
investment restrictions, the key person provisions, and the terms on which LPs can act if things
go wrong.

It is also close to unamendable in practice — changing it requires a supermajority of LPs —
which means terms negotiated during fundraising govern for a decade regardless of how
circumstances change.

## What it looks like in practice

The terms that matter most are the economics (fee, carry, hurdle, waterfall type), the
constraints (concentration limits, geography, stage, follow-on reserves), and the protections
(key person, no-fault divorce, LPAC consent rights).

ILPA publishes principles that many institutional LPs use as a reference, which has narrowed the
range of what's considered market on several of these terms.

## Where it goes wrong

- Negotiating the fee and carry and skimming the rest.
- Agreeing investment restrictions that the actual strategy will breach.
- Assuming the LPA can be amended easily when the market changes.
- Not reading the waterfall carefully enough to model when carry is actually received.
