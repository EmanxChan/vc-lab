## Why it matters

A lifestyle business is one that can be profitable and durable without ever being large enough
to return a venture fund. It's not a criticism of the business — it's a statement about fit.

Under the power law a fund needs outcomes large enough that one can return everything. A company
that will comfortably reach $8M of revenue and stay there is a good business and a bad venture
investment, and conflating those two judgments serves nobody.

## What it looks like in practice

The honest version of the conversation happens early. A founder who wants a sustainable,
profitable, independent company should not raise venture money, because the structure will force
decisions they don't want.

The tell is usually in the ambition of the market definition combined with the founder's stated
goals. Both are legitimate; they just have to match the funding structure.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating it as a pejorative. Most good businesses are lifestyle businesses.
- Funding one with venture capital and then pressuring it to behave otherwise.
- Assuming a founder who says they want independence will change their mind after a round.
- Confusing current size with ceiling. The question is the ceiling.
