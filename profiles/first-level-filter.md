## Why it matters

The first filter is a cheap, fast check against the thesis: stage, sector, geography, and
whether this is a venture-scale outcome at all. It exists to protect attention.

Most deals should die here, and quickly. A filter that passes half of what arrives isn't
filtering — it's deferring the decision to a stage that costs much more time.

## What it looks like in practice

Good first filters are mechanical and stated in advance, which makes them fast and makes the
passes honest. They should be answerable from a deck and a five-minute look.

The cost of a tight filter is false negatives, and that cost is real. The mitigation is a
written record, so patterns in what you rejected can be examined later.

## Where it goes wrong

- Making the first filter about quality rather than fit. Quality assessment is expensive.
- Applying it inconsistently depending on who made the introduction.
- Not writing down the reason, which makes false negatives invisible.
- Setting it so loosely that everything reaches the second level.
