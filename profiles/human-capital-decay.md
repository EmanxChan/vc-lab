## Why it matters

Teams degrade over time in ways that don't show up in the metrics until late. Founders
disengage, key people leave, and the density of talent that made the early product good quietly
thins.

For an investor it's a risk that's hard to observe from outside and easy to observe from a board
seat, which is part of what board access is actually for.

## What it looks like in practice

The observable signals are turnover among the strongest people, founders spending time on things
that aren't the hard problem, and a hiring bar that has visibly moved.

Vesting and equity refresh are the structural mitigations. A team fully vested with no refresh
has weak retention incentives at exactly the point where their experience is most valuable.

## Where it goes wrong

- Watching headcount rather than watching who left.
- Ignoring full-vest cliffs for the early team, which is a predictable retention risk.
- Assuming a strong founding team stays strong without renewal.
- Reading growth as evidence that the team is intact.
