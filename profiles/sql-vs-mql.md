## Why it matters

The gap between these two numbers is where marketing and sales usually stop trusting each other,
and where founders overestimate their own traction. Interest is cheap and abundant.
Qualification is expensive and scarce.

The conversion rate between them is a direct measure of whether the top of the funnel is
attracting the right people at all. A very low MQL-to-SQL rate means the content is reaching an
audience that will never buy.

## What it looks like in practice

Qualification usually comes down to a few checks: do they match the ICP, is there a real
problem, is there budget, and is this person able to move it forward. Written down and applied
consistently, that filter is what makes a pipeline number mean something.

At pre-seed this machinery is mostly overkill — but the underlying discipline isn't. Even a
founder tracking twenty conversations should know which ones are real.

## Where it goes wrong

- Counting MQLs as pipeline in an investor update.
- Qualifying on job title alone rather than on problem and budget.
- Setting the bar so high that sales rejects everything and marketing stops trying.
- Never measuring the conversion rate between the two, which is the only number that says
  whether the top of the funnel works.
