## Why it matters

Committed capital is the fund's headline size and the base most fees are calculated on during
the investment period. It's a legal commitment, not cash in hand.

The distinction from contributed capital matters for every performance metric. TVPI and DPI are
calculated on what was actually drawn, not on what was promised, which is why a fund early in
its life can show odd-looking numbers.

## What it looks like in practice

During the investment period management fees are usually charged on committed capital;
afterwards they typically step down to invested or net invested capital, which reduces the fee
as the fund winds down.

Reserves are held against committed capital too. A fund that deploys all its committed capital
into initial cheques has nothing left to defend its winners, which is a portfolio construction
error rather than a cash management one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting performance against committed rather than contributed capital.
- Deploying the full commitment into initial investments with no reserves.
- Forgetting that fees on committed capital accrue whether or not money is deployed.
- Assuming the full commitment will ever be called. Many funds don't call all of it.
