## Why it matters

Runway is cash divided by net burn — the number of months before the company is out of money.
It's the single most important operating number at an early company because it bounds every
other decision.

The trap is that runway is usually calculated on today's burn, and burn rises. A plan that shows
fourteen months at current spend often shows nine at planned spend, and the planned figure is
the real one.

## What it looks like in practice

Raising should start when there are nine to twelve months left, because the process takes three
to six and the terms get worse as the buffer shrinks. Investors can see the position and it
prices in.

The useful discipline is running two numbers: runway at current burn, and runway under the
hiring plan. The gap between them is the decision that hasn't been made explicitly yet.

## Where it goes wrong

- Calculating on current burn when the plan increases spending.
- Starting a raise with under six months, which visibly weakens the position.
- Assuming a bridge will be available if the raise takes longer.
- Reporting runway without saying which burn assumption it uses.
