## Why it matters

At pre-seed there is nothing to look at except the team and the evidence they've gathered.
Signed design partners are the strongest evidence available — they mean someone with a budget
and a reputation agreed to spend time on this.

The risk is the opposite failure: building a bespoke product for three customers and calling it
a company. The point of a design partner is to find the shared shape of the problem, not to take
orders.

## What it looks like in practice

A real design-partner agreement has obligations on both sides written down — a cadence, named
people, access to real data or real users, and an end date with a decision at it. The weak
version is a friendly logo and a monthly call that gets rescheduled.

The signal to watch is whether they use it when you're not in the room. A design partner who
only opens the product before the weekly call is giving you feedback, not usage.

## Where it goes wrong

- Signing partners who are unusual, and building for the exception rather than the pattern.
- Giving away too much — perpetual free access, exclusivity, or roadmap veto — for a logo.
- Letting the relationship stay free forever. The conversion to paid is the actual test.
- Treating their feature requests as a roadmap instead of as evidence about the underlying job.
