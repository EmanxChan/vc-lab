## Why it matters

Convertible preferred is the standard venture instrument because it does two things at once:
downside protection through the liquidation preference, and full upside through the right to
convert to common.

That asymmetry is the entire structure of a venture investment. The investor takes the better of
two outcomes at every exit value, which is what makes a portfolio of mostly-failures workable.

## What it looks like in practice

The preferred rights ride along with it: the preference, anti-dilution, protective provisions,
information rights, and usually a board seat. Conversion strips all of them, which is why
conversion only happens when the economics justify it.

Each round issues its own series with its own terms, which is how the stack accumulates and why
later rounds can have priority over earlier ones.

## Where it goes wrong

- Modelling an exit as though preferred converts, without checking whether it would.
- Forgetting that conversion strips the protective provisions too, not just the preference.
- Treating all series of preferred as one block when they may vote separately.
- Ignoring accrued dividends, which increase the amount owed before common.
