## Why it matters

Participation is the difference between a preference that expires when things go well and one
that never expires. Non-participating preferred forces a choice: take the preference or convert
and take your percentage. Participating takes both.

That choice is what makes non-participating fair in a good outcome. Remove it and the investor's
return improves at every exit value, with the difference coming entirely out of common.

## What it looks like in practice

In a $30M exit on $20M invested, 1x non-participating pays investors $20M. Participating pays
$20M plus their percentage of the remaining $10M. The gap widens with the size of the preference
and narrows as the exit grows.

Caps are the common compromise — participation until the investor has received some multiple of
their money, then it stops. A 2x cap means participation ends once they've made twice their
investment.

## Where it goes wrong

- Accepting participation to preserve a headline valuation. It usually costs more than the price
  difference.
- Not modelling participating versus non-participating across a range of exit values.
- Ignoring the cap, which changes the arithmetic substantially at higher exits.
- Assuming participation is market. In most healthy seed and Series A rounds it isn't.
