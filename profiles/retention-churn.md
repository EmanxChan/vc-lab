## Why it matters

Retention is the closest thing to a single measure of whether a product is any good. Acquisition
can be bought; retention has to be earned, which is why it's the number that predicts everything
downstream.

Churn compounds exactly as hard as growth. A business losing 5% of customers monthly has an
average customer life of about twenty months and has to run hard just to stand still.

## What it looks like in practice

Logo churn and revenue churn can diverge instructively. Losing many small accounts while
retaining large ones is a very different business from the reverse, and a blended number hides
which one you have.

The most useful view is by cohort, plotted against age. A curve that flattens means a durable
base; one that keeps declining means the company has to keep replacing everyone forever.

## Where it goes wrong

- Tracking only logo churn while revenue concentrates in a few fragile accounts.
- Averaging across segments, which hides that one is fine and another is bleeding.
- Treating churn as a customer-success problem when it's usually a product-fit problem.
- Not asking departing customers why. It's the cheapest research available and the most avoided.
