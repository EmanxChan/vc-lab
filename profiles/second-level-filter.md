## Why it matters

The second filter is where judgment starts. Having established the deal fits the thesis, the
question becomes whether this specific team, in this specific market, at this specific moment,
is worth the work of diligence.

It's the most expensive stage per deal before diligence proper, which is why the first filter
has to do its job.

## What it looks like in practice

Typically a founder meeting, a look at whatever evidence exists, and a view on the market and
the timing. The output is a decision to diligence or a written pass.

The useful discipline is to name, before the meeting, what would have to be true for this to be
interesting. Deciding the criteria after hearing the pitch is how enthusiasm becomes analysis.

## Where it goes wrong

- Deciding what matters after the meeting rather than before it.
- Advancing deals because the founder was compelling rather than because the criteria were met.
- Spending diligence-level effort at this stage on deals that will fail anyway.
- Not writing the pass reason, which is the most useful artifact this stage produces.
