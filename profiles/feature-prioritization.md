## Why it matters

At pre-seed the constraint isn't ideas, it's time, and every wrong build is a month the company
doesn't get back. Prioritisation is where a small team either compounds or diffuses.

It's also where founder discipline shows. Building what the loudest customer asked for is the
path of least resistance, and it reliably produces a product that serves one account well and
the market badly.

## What it looks like in practice

Most frameworks — RICE, ICE, value versus effort — do the same job: force an explicit comparison
instead of an implicit one. The framework matters less than having any stated basis, because the
alternative is deciding by whoever asked most recently.

The underlying question is which assumption you're testing. Early on, prioritising by learning
value beats prioritising by revenue, because the revenue estimates are guesses anyway.

## Where it goes wrong

- Building whatever the largest customer asked for, and becoming a consultancy.
- Prioritising by effort alone, so easy things get built and hard important things never do.
- Maintaining a backlog nobody ever deletes from, which makes it a graveyard rather than a plan.
- Confusing a prioritisation framework with a decision. The numbers are inputs.
