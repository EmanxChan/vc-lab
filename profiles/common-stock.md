## Why it matters

Common stock is what founders and employees hold, and it sits at the bottom of the payout stack.
Everything preferred is owed gets paid first, which is why common can be worth nothing in an
exit that looks like a success.

It's also why the 409A price for common is so much lower than the preferred price. Common
carries none of the protections and none of the rights, and the market values that difference
honestly.

## What it looks like in practice

Common holders vote, but their voting power is constrained by protective provisions that require
preferred approval for the decisions that matter most.

The number that determines whether common is worth anything is the total preference stack
against the exit value. Below the stack, common gets nothing regardless of percentage.

## Where it goes wrong

- Valuing common at the preferred price when estimating what a stake is worth.
- Assuming a majority of common means control of a sale decision.
- Ignoring how the preference stack grows with each round.
- Treating option grants as equivalent to the preferred price investors paid.
