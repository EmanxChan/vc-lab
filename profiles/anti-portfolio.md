## Why it matters

The anti-portfolio is the record of what you passed on and why. Kept honestly it's the only
systematic way to audit your own judgment, because the successes you backed tell you far less
than the successes you declined.

Under the power law, missing one outlier costs more than making several bad investments. That
asymmetry makes the reasons for passing worth studying carefully.

## What it looks like in practice

The value is entirely in the reasons, not the list. "Passed because we thought the market was
too small" is a testable claim you can check three years later. "Passed, not a fit" teaches
nothing.

Reviewing it periodically surfaces patterns — a systematic blind spot about a sector, a founder
profile, or a business model that keeps being underestimated.

## Where it goes wrong

- Keeping the list without the reasons.
- Only recording the misses that became famous, which biases the sample.
- Treating it as embarrassment rather than as data.
- Never reviewing it, which is the same as not keeping it.
