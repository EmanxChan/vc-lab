## Why it matters

Top-down sizing produces a number nobody believes, including the person presenting it. Bottom-up
produces a smaller number that survives scrutiny — and surviving scrutiny is the entire purpose
of putting a market size in a deck.

It also forces the founder to know their customer. You cannot build a bottom-up estimate without
a concrete ICP and a defensible price, which means the exercise itself surfaces whether those
exist.

## What it looks like in practice

Start from a countable population — companies of a given size in a given industry, or people in
a defined situation — narrow it by the criteria in the ICP, and multiply by realistic annual
contract value based on what people currently pay for the alternative.

Showing the sources for each input is what converts it from a claim into an argument. An
investor who disagrees with one assumption can substitute their own, which is a far better
conversation than disagreeing with the total.

## Where it goes wrong

- Using a list price nobody actually pays as the ACV input.
- Counting the entire industry rather than the segment matching the ICP.
- Presenting the result without the inputs, which returns it to being an unverifiable number.
- Assuming 100% of the countable population is reachable through your channel.
