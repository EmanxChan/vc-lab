## Why it matters

Section 1061 requires carried interest to be held for more than three years to receive long-term
capital gains treatment. Below that, it's taxed as short-term.

For venture the rule is rarely binding, because holding periods are typically far longer. It
matters more for strategies with faster turnover, and it's the current form of a policy argument
that has run for over a decade.

## What it looks like in practice

The holding period applies to the underlying assets, not to the fund interest, which is the
detail that trips people up. An early exit inside three years produces short-term treatment on
that carry even in an otherwise long-lived fund.

The rule was introduced by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, extending the previous one-year
requirement.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming the holding period runs from fund formation rather than from the asset.
- Ignoring it for a fast acquisition inside three years.
- Treating the current rule as settled policy. It has been repeatedly proposed for change.
- Applying the rule without checking how it interacts with the specific fund structure.
