## Why it matters

First close is when a fund has enough commitments to start investing. It matters because it
converts a fundraise from a pitch into a live vehicle — and because the LPs who come in at first
close take the most risk.

For an emerging manager it's the hardest milestone in the whole process. Everyone wants to see
who else is in, and someone has to go first.

## What it looks like in practice

Funds usually hold multiple closes over twelve to eighteen months. Later LPs typically pay an
equalisation amount — interest on the capital earlier LPs had at risk — so everyone ends up in
the same economic position.

Anchor investors at first close often negotiate better terms: fee discounts, co-investment
rights, or an LPAC seat. That's the price of going first.

## Where it goes wrong

- Waiting for a full fund before starting to invest, and missing the deals that justified the
  strategy.
- Closing at a size too small to execute the stated strategy.
- Granting anchor terms so favourable they distort the fund's economics.
- Not handling equalisation properly, which creates inconsistency between LP cohorts.
