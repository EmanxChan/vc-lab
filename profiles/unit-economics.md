## Why it matters

Unit economics is where growth either compounds or accelerates the collapse. A company losing
money per customer that raises money to acquire more customers is buying its own failure faster,
and the revenue chart looks great the whole time.

For a pre-seed investor the numbers are unreliable — too few customers, too short a history —
but the founder's grip on them isn't. Someone who knows their real cost to serve, including
support and infrastructure, is running a business rather than a product.

## What it looks like in practice

The honest calculation uses fully loaded costs: all sales and marketing spend divided by
customers acquired, not just ad spend; cost to serve including support, hosting, and inference;
and a retention estimate grounded in actual cohorts rather than an assumption.

For AI products the cost side is genuinely different from classic SaaS, because serving costs
scale with usage. Gross margins of 40–60% are common where SaaS assumed 80%, and every model
built on the old assumption is wrong.

## Where it goes wrong

- Excluding salaries from CAC, which understates it by a lot.
- Using an LTV built on an assumed retention curve rather than a measured one.
- Ignoring inference and infrastructure cost in cost-to-serve.
- Scaling acquisition before the unit is profitable, on the theory that scale will fix it.
