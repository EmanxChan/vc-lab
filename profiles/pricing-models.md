## Why it matters

The structure decides how revenue grows once the customer is in. Per-seat pricing grows when the
customer hires. Usage pricing grows when they use it more. Flat pricing doesn't grow at all
without a renegotiation.

That's why the structure drives net revenue retention, and NRR is what separates a good SaaS
business from an ordinary one. Choosing the axis is a strategic decision, not a finance one.

## What it looks like in practice

The right axis is the one that tracks the value the customer receives, so their bill grows when
they're getting more and never grows when they're not. Per-seat works when value scales with
people. Usage works when it scales with volume — but it also makes revenue less predictable and
gives customers a reason to ration usage.

For AI products usage pricing has a second job: it passes through inference cost. Flat pricing
on a variable-cost product is how gross margin quietly disappears.

## Where it goes wrong

- Pricing per seat on a product where one power user does all the work.
- Flat pricing on top of a variable cost base.
- Changing the pricing axis on existing customers without grandfathering, which reads as a bait
  and switch.
- Copying a competitor's structure when your cost base is different.
