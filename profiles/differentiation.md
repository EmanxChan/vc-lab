## Why it matters

Undifferentiated products compete on price, and price competition at pre-seed is a way to lose
slowly. Differentiation is what lets a small company win a deal against a bigger one with a
better brand and more references.

The test is whether the claim survives a competitor's marketing page. If your competitor says
the same thing, it isn't differentiation — it's category table stakes described
enthusiastically.

## What it looks like in practice

Real differentiation is usually narrow and slightly uncomfortable, because it implies the things
you're deliberately worse at. A product that's genuinely the best at one workflow is usually
mediocre at three others, and saying so out loud is what makes the claim credible.

It also has to matter to the buyer, not just be true. Plenty of products are differentiated on
dimensions nobody is choosing on.

## Where it goes wrong

- Differentiating on adjectives every competitor also uses.
- Being different in a way the buyer doesn't care about.
- Listing ten differentiators, which reads as none.
- Claiming differentiation that disappears the moment a competitor ships one feature.
