## Why it matters

Wind-down is the end of a fund's life: remaining positions are sold, distributed in kind, or
written off, final distributions are made, and the clawback is settled.

It's where the residual problems surface. Positions that can't be sold, companies that are
neither dead nor liquid, and LPs who want their money back regardless are all wind-down
problems.

## What it looks like in practice

GPs usually seek extensions rather than force bad exits, typically one year at a time with LP or
LPAC consent. Beyond that the options are secondary sales of the remaining portfolio,
distribution in kind, or a continuation vehicle.

The final accounting includes settling the clawback, releasing escrow, and closing the
partnership — which can take a further year after the last position is resolved.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming extensions will be granted indefinitely.
- Forcing exits at bad prices to meet a deadline rather than negotiating an extension.
- Distributing illiquid securities to LPs who have no ability to hold or sell them.
- Not planning for the clawback settlement, which is the last thing to close.
