## Why it matters

The management company is the operating business: it employs the team, pays the rent, and
receives the management fee. It persists across funds, which is what makes a firm a firm rather
than a series of one-off vehicles.

That persistence is the point. Fund I ends, Fund II begins, and the ManCo carries the people,
the brand, and the infrastructure across the boundary.

## What it looks like in practice

The ManCo's revenue is management fees from every active fund, which is why fee income stacks as
a firm raises successive vehicles. Its costs are salaries, premises, and operations.

The economics matter for an emerging manager: a small first fund generates a management fee that
often can't support a team, which is why most first-time managers are underpaid or subsidising
the firm personally.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming management fee is profit. It funds the entire operating business.
- Sizing a first fund without checking whether the fee supports the team you need.
- Confusing ManCo ownership with carry allocation.
- Forgetting that fees step down after the investment period, which shrinks the budget.
