## Why it matters

A cram down is a financing structured so punitively that existing holders are effectively wiped
out — a very low price, a heavy preference, and often pay-to-play attached. It happens when a
company has no alternative.

The reason it matters as vocabulary is that it's the endpoint of the terms negotiated years
earlier. Anti-dilution, preference stacking, and pay-to-play all become instruments in the same
transaction.

## What it looks like in practice

Typically the new money comes in senior to everything else, prior preferences are reduced or
converted, and non-participating investors lose their protections. What was a meaningful
position becomes a rounding error.

For a fund the practical question is whether to defend the position with more capital or accept
the write-down. That decision is what reserves exist for, and it's usually made with poor
information under time pressure.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating a cram down as a normal down round. The mechanics are different and far more severe.
- Having no reserves, which removes the option to defend a position.
- Ignoring the possibility when agreeing anti-dilution and pay-to-play terms years earlier.
- Assuming existing relationships prevent it. Insolvency changes what people do.
