## Why it matters

The no-shop is the binding half of a non-binding term sheet. Once signed, the company can't talk
to other investors, which removes the competitive tension that produced the terms in the first
place.

That's a reasonable ask — an investor about to spend money on diligence doesn't want to be a
stalking horse — but the length matters enormously, because every day of exclusivity is a day of
lost leverage.

## What it looks like in practice

Thirty to sixty days is typical for a financing. Shorter is better for the company, and a hard
outside date rather than an open-ended period is worth insisting on.

The same mechanism appears in acquisitions as exclusivity in the LOI, where the stakes are
higher because a failed process after months of exclusivity leaves the company visibly unsold.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing to a long no-shop with no closing deadline attached.
- Signing a term sheet before other conversations have reached a comparable stage.
- Assuming non-binding covers the whole document. Exclusivity binds.
- Not clarifying what happens if the investor walks — whether the period ends immediately.
