## Why it matters

Fully diluted is the only honest denominator. Ownership calculated on issued shares alone
ignores the option pool, the warrants, and every convertible instrument — all of which will
become shares.

It's also where quiet errors live. A percentage quoted on the wrong basis can be materially
wrong, and it's the kind of error that survives until an acquisition makes it expensive.

## What it looks like in practice

The count includes issued common and preferred, all options granted, the unallocated option
pool, warrants, and convertible notes and SAFEs modelled at their conversion terms.

The unallocated pool is the item most often left out, and it's usually the largest. Excluding it
makes every ownership percentage look better than it is.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting ownership on issued shares only.
- Excluding the unallocated option pool from the count.
- Ignoring SAFEs and notes because they haven't converted yet.
- Using different bases in different documents, so the numbers never reconcile.
