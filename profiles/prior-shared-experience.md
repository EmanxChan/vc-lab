## Why it matters

Founding teams who have worked together before have already tested the thing that most often
breaks companies: whether they can disagree productively under pressure. That's not observable
in a pitch, and it's not learnable quickly.

The research on founding team composition consistently finds prior shared experience predictive,
which makes it one of the few team signals with evidence behind it.

## What it looks like in practice

What matters is whether they've worked together through something difficult, not whether they've
known each other a long time. Friends who have never built anything together are a different
proposition from colleagues who shipped under pressure.

The diligence question is direct: tell me about the biggest disagreement you two have had, and
how it resolved. Teams who have been through it answer easily.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating long friendship as equivalent to having worked together.
- Ignoring that co-founders who met recently can still be excellent — it's a prior, not a rule.
- Not asking how they handle disagreement, which is the thing being tested.
- Assuming prior shared experience removes the need for vesting.
