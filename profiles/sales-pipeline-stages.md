## Why it matters

Stages defined by buyer behaviour give you a forecast. Stages defined by seller optimism give
you a story. The difference shows up when a founder projects revenue and misses by 70% — usually
because "verbal yes" was counted as a stage.

For an investor it's a quick read on operational discipline. A founder who can tell you
conversion rate between two specific stages is running a process; one who reports a single
pipeline total is running a hope.

## What it looks like in practice

Each stage should have an objective exit criterion tied to something the buyer did — they shared
their current spend, they introduced you to procurement, they sent redlines. Things you did
don't count.

Once the stages are real, the conversion rates between them tell you where the problem is. A
pipeline that fills at the top and dies at security review is a different problem from one that
never gets second meetings.

## Where it goes wrong

- Defining stages by how the salesperson feels rather than what the buyer did.
- Letting deals sit in a stage indefinitely instead of aging out.
- Skipping stages for a promising deal, then being surprised at the end.
- Reporting total pipeline value without stage-weighted probability.
