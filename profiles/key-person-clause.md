## Why it matters

LPs invest in specific people, and the key person clause is what protects that. If the named
individuals stop devoting substantially all their time to the fund, the investment period
suspends automatically.

It's the main structural protection LPs have during a fund's life. Beyond it they have very
little ability to change anything, which is why the clause gets negotiated carefully.

## What it looks like in practice

Triggering usually suspends new investments until LPs vote to resume, replace the key person, or
terminate the investment period. Follow-on investments in existing portfolio companies are
typically still permitted.

For a solo GP the clause is absolute — there's no one else named. That concentration is one of
the reasons institutional LPs are cautious about single-partner funds.

## Where it goes wrong

- Naming too many key persons, which weakens the protection.
- Not defining what "substantially all time" means, leaving it arguable.
- Ignoring the clause when a partner takes on outside commitments.
- Assuming a trigger is recoverable. It requires an LP vote, and votes can fail.
