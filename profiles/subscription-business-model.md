## Why it matters

It is the default model for software because it turns a sales problem into a retention problem,
and retention compounds. It's also the reason SaaS gets valued on revenue multiples: recurring
revenue is predictable in a way transaction revenue isn't.

The catch is that recurring cuts both ways. Churn compounds exactly as hard as growth does, and
a business losing 4% of revenue a month has to run very fast to stand still.

## What it looks like in practice

The health of a subscription business lives in three numbers: what it costs to acquire a
customer, how long they stay, and whether existing accounts grow. Net revenue retention above
100% means the base grows without any new logos — that's the quiet engine behind most great SaaS
companies.

Annual contracts improve cash and hide problems. Monthly contracts show you churn in real time
and starve you of working capital. Most companies end up with both, aimed at different segments.

## Where it goes wrong

- Discounting annual plans so aggressively that a year passes before you learn people aren't
  using it.
- Reporting ARR that includes one-off implementation fees.
- Treating churn as a customer-success problem when it's a product-fit problem.
- Adding seats-based pricing to a product whose value doesn't scale with seats.
