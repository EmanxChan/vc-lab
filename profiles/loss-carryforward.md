## Why it matters

A loss carryforward provision requires realised losses to be netted against realised gains
before carry is calculated. Without it, a deal-by-deal waterfall would pay carry on every winner
while ignoring every loser.

It's one of the main protections that makes an American waterfall tolerable to LPs, and its
absence is a red flag in an LPA.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanism carries forward realised losses and offsets them against subsequent gains, so
carry is only paid on net profits rather than gross ones.

Variations matter: whether it covers only realised losses or also writes down unrealised
positions, and whether it applies across the whole fund or only within a period.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing to a deal-by-deal waterfall without a loss carryforward.
- Applying it only to realised losses while ignoring obviously impaired positions.
- Assuming it's standard. It has to be in the document.
- Modelling carry on gross gains rather than net of carried-forward losses.
