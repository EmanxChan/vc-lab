## Why it matters

The management fee funds the firm — salaries, premises, everything. It's not profit, and for a
small fund it's frequently not enough. A $20M fund at 2% generates $400,000 a year, which does
not support a team.

It's also drawn from LP capital, so it reduces what's available to invest. Over a ten-year fund
a 2% fee consumes roughly 20% of committed capital, which is why the portfolio has to return
meaningfully more than 1x before LPs see their money back.

## What it looks like in practice

The standard structure charges on committed capital during the investment period, then steps
down to invested or net invested capital afterwards. That step-down is significant for the
management company's budget and is often underplanned.

Fee offsets are worth checking: where a GP receives board fees or transaction fees from
portfolio companies, LPs typically expect those to offset the management fee.

## Where it goes wrong

- Sizing a fund without checking whether the fee supports the necessary team.
- Treating the fee as compensation rather than as the firm's operating budget.
- Not planning for the post-investment-period step-down.
- Failing to offset portfolio company fees against the management fee, which LPs will notice.
