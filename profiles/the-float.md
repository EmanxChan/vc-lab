## Why it matters

The float is the value of holding money you may later have to return. Under a deal-by-deal
waterfall a GP can receive carry in year four that isn't finally earned until year ten — and
having that money for six years is worth something real.

It's the economic reason GPs prefer American waterfalls even when the total carry is identical,
and the reason LPs push back.

## What it looks like in practice

Escrow arrangements exist to reduce it: holding back a portion of carry until the fund's outcome
is clearer means the GP doesn't have use of that money in the meantime.

It's rarely quantified explicitly in negotiations, but it's a meaningful part of what the
waterfall type is actually worth to each side.

## Where it goes wrong

- Ignoring the float when comparing waterfall structures.
- Spending floated carry, which makes a clawback obligation genuinely painful.
- Assuming escrow removes it entirely. Escrow reduces it; it doesn't eliminate the timing
  benefit.
- Treating it as a technicality rather than as real economic value.
