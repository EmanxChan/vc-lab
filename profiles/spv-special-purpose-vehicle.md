## Why it matters

An SPV is a single-purpose vehicle holding one investment. It lets a group invest together
through one entity, which keeps the company's cap table clean and gives the organiser a defined
role and usually carry.

For a fund, SPVs are how you take more of a winner than reserves allow. When a portfolio company
raises a large round, an SPV lets the GP bring LPs and others into a position the fund alone
couldn't fund.

## What it looks like in practice

Costs matter more than they look: formation, administration, tax filings, and audit all recur
annually for a vehicle holding one asset. On a small SPV that overhead is a meaningful drag on
return.

Economics vary widely. Some SPVs carry no management fee and 10–20% carry; others charge both.
The structure is worth reading before participating.

## Where it goes wrong

- Underestimating the annual administrative cost relative to the size of the investment.
- Stacking SPV carry on top of fund carry without disclosing it clearly to LPs.
- Using an SPV to concentrate into a position without the diligence a new investment would get.
- Forgetting the SPV has its own life and its own wind-down obligations.
