## Why it matters

Recycling lets a fund reinvest early proceeds rather than distributing them, so more than 100%
of committed capital ends up deployed. It's how a fund offsets the drag of management fees on
invested capital.

Without recycling, a fund charging 2% for ten years invests only about 80% of what LPs
committed. Recycling closes that gap.

## What it looks like in practice

The LPA sets limits: usually a cap on the total that can be recycled, often 110–125% of
commitments, and a time limit tied to the investment period.

It's generally LP-favourable because it puts more capital to work, but it also delays
distributions, which affects IRR. The trade-off is between more invested capital and slower
return of cash.

## Where it goes wrong

- Recycling beyond what the LPA permits.
- Recycling late in the fund's life, when there isn't time for the investment to mature.
- Modelling deployment at 100% of commitments when fees mean it's lower without recycling.
- Ignoring the effect on IRR of delaying distributions.
