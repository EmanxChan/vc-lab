## Why it matters

Concentration is the tension at the centre of portfolio construction. Under a power law you need
a big enough position in the winner for it to matter — and you don't know in advance which one
that is.

Too concentrated and missing the outlier is fatal. Too diversified and catching it doesn't help
enough. Every fund resolves this differently and the resolution defines the strategy.

## What it looks like in practice

The usual approach is broad at entry and concentrated through follow-ons: many initial
positions, reserves deployed disproportionately into the ones showing signal.

LPAs frequently cap concentration — no more than some percentage of the fund in a single company
— which constrains how far the strategy can go even when conviction is high.

## Where it goes wrong

- Concentrating at entry, before there's information to concentrate on.
- Spreading reserves evenly, which is diversification disguised as follow-on strategy.
- Breaching LPA concentration limits, which is a real covenant.
- Modelling concentration on expected value rather than on the tail outcome.
