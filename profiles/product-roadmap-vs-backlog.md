## Why it matters

Conflating them is how roadmaps become commitment lists, and commitment lists are how product
teams lose the ability to change their minds. A roadmap of outcomes can survive new information;
a roadmap of features can only be broken.

It also affects what you can show people. Outcomes can be shared with customers and investors
safely. A dated feature list shared externally becomes a contract nobody agreed to.

## What it looks like in practice

A useful roadmap has themes and rough time horizons — now, next, later — with confidence
decreasing as it goes out. The backlog underneath it is where implementation detail lives, and
it should be pruned aggressively rather than accumulated.

The honest version admits that the far end is a guess. A roadmap with the same precision at
eighteen months as at six weeks is fiction in both places.

## Where it goes wrong

- Publishing a dated feature roadmap externally.
- Letting the backlog grow forever, so nobody can find anything in it.
- Writing the roadmap as features, which makes every change look like a failure.
- Treating the roadmap as fixed for the year when the company is still finding fit.
