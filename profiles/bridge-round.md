## Why it matters

A bridge is a bet by existing investors that the company is close. That makes it one of the
clearest signals available — insiders with the most information choosing to put more money in,
or choosing not to.

The distinction that matters is whether it's a bridge or a pier. A bridge reaches something: a
defined milestone that unlocks the next round. A pier extends into open water and runs out of
money slightly later.

## What it looks like in practice

Bridges are usually done on a SAFE or note rather than a priced round, because pricing the
company mid-crisis is both hard and unhelpful. Terms often include a discount to the next round
or warrant coverage to compensate for the risk.

The right time to raise one is well before it's needed. A bridge negotiated with six months of
runway gets reasonable terms; the same conversation at two months does not.

## Where it goes wrong

- Raising a bridge with no defined milestone, which just moves the problem two quarters out.
- Waiting until the position is desperate, when the terms reflect it.
- Reading insider participation as a formality. If existing investors won't bridge, that's the
  signal.
- Stacking multiple bridges, each with its own discount, until the next round's cap table is
  unworkable.
