## Why it matters

Acqui-hires are how a large share of venture-backed companies actually end, and the economics
are very different from a real acquisition. Most of the value goes to the team as compensation
rather than to shareholders as proceeds, which is a structural conflict.

For a fund it's a soft landing rather than a return — capital back, occasionally a small
multiple, never a fund-returner. Under the power law it barely moves the outcome, which is why
funds are generally relaxed about them.

## What it looks like in practice

The negotiation is about how consideration splits between purchase price and retention
compensation. Acquirers prefer retention because it buys what they're actually after; investors
prefer purchase price because that's what pays the preference stack.

This is where founder and investor interests genuinely diverge, and where the protective
provisions and drag-along matter — an acqui-hire that pays founders well and shareholders poorly
usually needs approval from the people being paid poorly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating it as a success in an update. Investors can read the numbers.
- Negotiating retention packages without regard for the preference stack, then needing consent.
- Assuming the product survives. It usually doesn't, and customers should hear it from you.
- Forgetting that a team without acceleration may have little incentive to accept.
