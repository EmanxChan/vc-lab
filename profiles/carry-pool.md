## Why it matters

The carry pool is how carried interest is divided among the partnership. It's the compensation
structure that actually matters at a venture firm, and it's usually invisible from outside.

Allocation drives behaviour. A junior partner with a small carry allocation on a fund that may
not pay for eight years has weak long-term incentives, which is one reason venture firms have
retention problems at that level.

## What it looks like in practice

Allocations are typically set per fund rather than firm-wide, which means each new fund is an
opportunity to reallocate. Points are usually granted with vesting attached.

A portion is often reserved for future hires, so a fund raised today can accommodate a partner
joining in year three without renegotiating everyone else's share.

## Where it goes wrong

- Allocating the full pool at fund formation with nothing reserved for future hires.
- Setting allocations without vesting, so a departing partner keeps everything.
- Assuming firm equity and carry allocation track each other. They usually don't.
- Treating carry as a substitute for salary when it may not pay for a decade.
