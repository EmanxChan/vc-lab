## Why it matters

Some companies produce a disproportionate number of founders — the alumni networks of certain
firms show up repeatedly in successful cap tables. Those organisations function as talent
factories.

For sourcing this is a practical filter. Knowing which organisations in your sector reliably
produce founders tells you where to build relationships years before anyone starts a company.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanism is a combination of selection, training, and network. These companies hire well,
expose people to hard problems at scale, and create a peer group where starting something is
normal.

The signal decays with distance. Someone who spent four years in a core team is a different
proposition from someone who spent nine months in an adjacent function, and the logo doesn't
distinguish them.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating the logo as the signal rather than what the person actually did there.
- Over-indexing on a small number of well-known companies and missing sector-specific ones.
- Assuming the factory effect transfers to a completely different domain.
- Using it as a filter rather than as a sourcing map.
