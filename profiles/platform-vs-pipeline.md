## Why it matters

The distinction predicts how the business scales and where it's vulnerable. Pipelines scale by
adding cost — more output means more input. Platforms scale by adding participants, which is why
they can grow revenue far faster than headcount.

It also predicts quality control. Pipelines own quality and can guarantee it. Platforms inherit
whatever their participants produce, and moderating that becomes a permanent, expensive
obligation.

## What it looks like in practice

Most real companies are hybrids and get there deliberately: start as a pipeline to guarantee
quality and prove demand, then open up once there's enough demand to attract third-party supply.
Going the other way is much harder.

The tell in a pitch is what happens when demand doubles. If the answer involves hiring
proportionally, it's a pipeline — which is fine, but it should be valued and funded like one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Calling a pipeline a platform because it has an API.
- Opening a platform before demand exists, and getting supply with nobody to serve.
- Underestimating what moderation and quality control cost at scale.
- Assuming platform economics apply before network effects actually kick in.
