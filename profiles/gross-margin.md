## Why it matters

Gross margin decides what kind of company you are, and it's the number that drives valuation
multiples more than growth rate does at the margin. An 85% gross margin business and a 35% one
at the same revenue are not comparable assets.

It's also the constraint on everything downstream. Low margin means less room to acquire
customers, less room to hire, and a much higher revenue bar before the company can fund itself.

## What it looks like in practice

The judgment call is what counts as cost of revenue. Hosting, third-party APIs, inference,
payment processing, and customer support attributable to delivery all belong in it. Sales,
marketing, and R&D don't.

AI-native companies are the live case. Where classic SaaS ran at 75–85%, products with heavy
inference frequently run at 40–60% — which is a real business, just not a SaaS one, and it
should be modelled and valued accordingly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Excluding inference or infrastructure costs to make margins look like SaaS.
- Putting customer success entirely in operating expense when much of it is delivery.
- Reporting margin before payment processing and refunds.
- Assuming margin improves with scale without knowing which cost actually falls.
