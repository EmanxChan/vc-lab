## Why it matters

Buyers prefer asset sales because they leave unknown liabilities behind and often get better tax
treatment. Sellers prefer stock sales because they're cleaner and usually taxed more favourably.
The structure is one of the most consequential terms after price.

For shareholders it can matter more than the headline number. An asset sale can trigger
different tax treatment and can complicate QSBS eligibility, which for founders holding
qualifying stock is a very large difference on the same nominal price.

## What it looks like in practice

Asset sales require transferring contracts individually, and many contracts have change-of-
control or anti-assignment clauses that give counterparties a veto. That makes them slower and
gives customers leverage at exactly the wrong moment.

The structure is usually settled in the LOI, which is another reason the LOI matters more than
its non-binding label suggests.

## Where it goes wrong

- Negotiating price without pricing in the tax consequences of the structure.
- Assuming contracts transfer automatically in an asset sale. Many don't.
- Overlooking the QSBS implications for founders and early employees.
- Leaving structure to the definitive documents rather than settling it in the LOI.
