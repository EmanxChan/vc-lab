## Why it matters

The question is whether a company reaches profitability on current trajectory before it runs out
of money. Default alive means yes on existing growth and burn; default dead means no, and the
company is dependent on raising again.

It reframes fundraising from a milestone into a dependency. A default-dead company has handed
its survival to a market it doesn't control, and markets close.

## What it looks like in practice

The calculation projects current revenue growth against current burn and asks whether the lines
cross before the cash runs out. It's a rough number and its value is in forcing the question
rather than in precision.

Most early companies are default dead and should be — the whole point of venture funding is to
spend ahead of revenue. What matters is knowing it, and knowing what it would take to change.

## Where it goes wrong

- Not running the calculation, which is the most common failure.
- Assuming growth continues at the current rate when projecting.
- Treating default dead as inherently bad rather than as a dependency to be managed.
- Discovering the answer when the runway is already short enough to remove the options.

## Where the term comes from

Paul Graham introduced the framing in a 2015 essay, arguing that founders should know which
state they're in at all times, and that most don't.
