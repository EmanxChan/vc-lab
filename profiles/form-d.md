## Why it matters

Almost every sourcing channel depends on somebody choosing to tell you something — a founder
sends a deck, an alum makes an intro, an accelerator publishes a batch. Form D doesn't. It's a
legal obligation, filed with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale of securities in a private
offering, and it lands in a free public database whether or not the company wants attention.

For an angel without a fund's inbound volume, that asymmetry is the point. Form D is the one
channel where being early is a matter of reading a filing before anyone else bothers to.

## What it looks like in practice

The filing itself is short. It names the company and its state, lists officers and directors,
gives the exemption being claimed, states the **total offering amount**, and states **how much
has already sold**. The gap between those last two numbers is the whole signal.

A search on EDGAR filtered to a state and the last 90 days is a list of companies that closed
money recently. Filtered further to small offerings — under $3M, say — it's a list of pre-seed
and seed rounds in progress. That's a sourcing query, not a research project.

It also works backwards. When a company you already know files, the filing tells you the round
is real and roughly how big, which is more reliable than anything you'll get from the founder's
own framing during a raise.

## Where it goes wrong

- **It's a lagging indicator.** The clock starts at the *first sale*, so by the time you see the
  filing the round has already begun and the lead is usually set. It finds you rounds still
  filling, not rounds not yet started.
- **Not everyone files on time, and some never do.** Treat an absence as no information rather
  than as evidence a company isn't raising.
- **The addresses are public, which makes the channel easy to abuse.** A cold email that opens
  with "I saw your Form D" reads as surveillance rather than interest. The filing is a reason to
  find a warm path in, not a substitute for one.
- **The amount sold can be stale.** It reflects the moment of filing, and companies aren't
  required to amend as the round fills.
