## Why it matters

Angels are the only source of capital available before there is anything to diligence. That
makes them structurally important — most companies that later raise institutional money were
funded first by someone writing a personal cheque on judgment alone.

It also means the discipline is different. An angel has no LPs, no committee, and no mandate, so
the decision is entirely their own. That is freedom and it is also the reason angel portfolios
are so unevenly constructed.

## What it looks like in practice

The realistic path is a portfolio, not a bet. Under the power law, a handful of cheques will
produce almost all the return, which means concentration into two or three names is a good way
to get nothing. Twenty to thirty positions is the number most experienced angels converge on.

The other lever is access. Angels who invest where they have genuine domain expertise or a real
network see better deals earlier, and at pre-seed being early is most of the edge.

## Where it goes wrong

- Investing in three companies and calling it a portfolio. The power law requires more shots.
- Writing cheques into rounds you didn't source, on terms someone else negotiated, without
  reading them.
- Investing money that is needed within ten years. This asset class is illiquid for longer than
  people plan for.
- Confusing being helpful with being informed — enthusiasm for a founder is not diligence.
