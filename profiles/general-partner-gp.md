## Why it matters

The GP is the entity that manages the fund and bears unlimited liability for it, which is why it
exists as a separate vehicle rather than as the individuals themselves. It's also the entity
that receives carried interest.

The separation between the GP entity, the management company, and the fund itself is not
bureaucratic decoration — it isolates liability, separates fee income from carry, and allows the
economics to be split differently across the two.

## What it looks like in practice

In practice the GP entity holds the carry and the management company employs the people and
receives the management fee. Individual partners have interests in both, usually in different
proportions.

The GP commit — the partners' own money in the fund — is what LPs look at to confirm alignment.
A GP with no meaningful capital at risk is being paid to take risk with other people's money.

## Where it goes wrong

- Collapsing GP, ManCo, and fund in your mental model. They do different jobs.
- Assuming carry splits match management company ownership. They usually don't.
- Ignoring the unlimited liability the GP entity carries.
- Underestimating how much LPs weigh the size and source of the GP commit.
