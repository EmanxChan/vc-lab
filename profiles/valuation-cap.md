## Why it matters

The cap is what rewards the investor for going early. Without it, money that arrived eighteen
months before the priced round converts at the same price as money that arrived the week before
— which is not a deal anyone would take.

It's also the term most often mistaken for a valuation. A $8M cap is not a claim that the
company is worth $8M; it's a ceiling on the price at which this particular money converts.

## What it looks like in practice

At conversion the investor gets the better of the cap price or the round price. If the round
prices below the cap, the cap is irrelevant and everyone converts at the round price.

Where a cap and a discount both exist, the standard is that the investor takes whichever
produces more shares — not both. Reading which applies is worth doing before signing rather than
at conversion.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting the cap as the company's valuation.
- Setting a cap so high it provides no real protection to the early investor.
- Setting it so low that the priced round starts with heavy dilution already committed.
- Assuming cap and discount stack. They usually don't.
