## Why it matters

Projections are assumptions arranged in a spreadsheet. Evidence is something that already
happened. At pre-seed there's little of the second and unlimited quantities of the first, which
makes distinguishing them the core analytical skill.

A hockey-stick forecast contains no information — every deck has one. Three customers who
renewed contains a great deal.

## What it looks like in practice

The evidence that counts is behavioural: someone paid, someone kept using it, someone referred a
colleague, someone signed before the product was finished. Each of those is a costly signal.

The useful question when shown a projection is what has to be true for it to happen, and then
which of those things has already been demonstrated. Usually the answer is none of them, which
is fine at pre-seed as long as everyone knows it.

## Where it goes wrong

- Diligencing the model rather than the assumptions underneath it.
- Accepting stated intent — surveys, LOIs without money — as evidence.
- Dismissing a company for having no projections when it has real usage.
- Confusing a large pipeline with demonstrated demand.
