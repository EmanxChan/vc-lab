## Why it matters

TVPI is total value to paid-in: distributions plus remaining value, divided by what LPs have
contributed. It's the headline number for a fund's performance and the one most vulnerable to
optimistic marks.

TVPI equals DPI plus RVPI, which is the useful decomposition. A 3.0x TVPI made of 2.5x DPI is a
very different fund from a 3.0x made of 0.2x DPI.

## What it looks like in practice

Because it uses paid-in capital as the denominator, TVPI accounts for fees — LPs contributed
that money too. That makes it a fairer measure than gross multiples.

Early in a fund's life TVPI is dominated by unrealised marks and is close to meaningless. The
J-curve means it typically sits below 1.0x for the first several years as fees are drawn before
value is created.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting TVPI without DPI, which hides how much is realised.
- Comparing TVPI across funds of different vintages and ages.
- Treating early-life TVPI as predictive. It mostly reflects marks and fee drag.
- Calculating it against committed rather than paid-in capital.
