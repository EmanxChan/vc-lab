## Why it matters

Carry vesting determines what a partner keeps if they leave before the fund pays out. Given that
carry can take eight years to arrive, this is the term that actually governs partner retention.

It also matters to LPs, because a partnership where senior people can leave with full carry has
weak continuity — and continuity is much of what LPs are buying.

## What it looks like in practice

Typical schedules run four to five years, sometimes tied to the investment period rather than to
calendar time. Good-leaver and bad-leaver provisions determine whether vested carry is kept or
forfeited depending on the circumstances of departure.

The distinction between vested and unvested carry at departure is one of the more contested
points in partnership agreements, and it's worth settling explicitly rather than by reference to
general principles.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting carry with no vesting, which removes the retention it's meant to create.
- Leaving good-leaver and bad-leaver definitions vague.
- Vesting on calendar time when the work is concentrated in the investment period.
- Not reallocating forfeited carry, so it sits unassigned.
