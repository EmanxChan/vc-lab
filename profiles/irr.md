## Why it matters

IRR is the annualised rate of return accounting for the timing of every cash flow. It's the
measure that captures what MOIC can't — that money returned early is worth more than money
returned late.

It's also the most manipulable venture metric. Because early cash flows dominate the
calculation, a quick early exit or a subscription credit line can produce an IRR that flatters a
mediocre fund.

## What it looks like in practice

The distortions worth knowing: credit lines that delay capital calls raise IRR without changing
the return; early distributions have outsized effect; and IRR is unstable early in a fund's life
when the cash flows are few.

This is why ILPA pushes for reporting both with and without subscription lines, and why IRR
should always be read alongside a multiple.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reading IRR without a multiple alongside it.
- Comparing IRRs computed with and without subscription credit lines.
- Taking early-life IRR seriously. It's dominated by fee outflows and noise.
- Assuming a high IRR means large absolute returns. A fast small win produces both.
