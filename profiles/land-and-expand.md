## Why it matters

It converts an impossible first sale into a possible one. A large organisation will not buy a
platform from a two-year-old company, but one director with a budget and a problem might. The
expansion then happens against a track record instead of a promise.

It's also the engine behind net revenue retention above 100%, which is the number that makes a
SaaS business compound without new logos.

## What it looks like in practice

It only works if the pricing structure lets accounts grow — per seat, per usage, per site — and
if the product produces visible internal evidence. The expansion is usually driven by the
original champion showing results to a peer, so instrumentation that makes results visible is a
growth feature, not a reporting one.

The number to watch is expansion revenue as a share of total new revenue. A healthy land-and-
expand business generates a large fraction of growth from accounts it already has.

## Where it goes wrong

- Landing so small the deal isn't worth the implementation effort on either side.
- Pricing flat, so a successful deployment produces no additional revenue.
- Landing in a team that has no adjacency to the rest of the organisation.
- Assuming expansion happens on its own. It needs a trigger and someone to notice.
