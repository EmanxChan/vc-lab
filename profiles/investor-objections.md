## Why it matters

Objections are the highest-quality free feedback a founder gets, and most of it is wasted
because the instinct is to defend rather than record. The same objection appearing repeatedly is
a defect in the story or in the business, and the two require different responses.

How a founder handles pushback is also being evaluated directly. Defensiveness in a pitch
predicts defensiveness in a board meeting, and investors are underwriting a decade-long
relationship.

## What it looks like in practice

Track every objection and count them. The ones that recur are real; the one-offs are usually a
specific fund's mandate. Then decide which are story problems, fixable by explaining better, and
which are business problems that need evidence you don't yet have.

The strongest move is pre-empting the obvious one. Raising the incumbent question yourself, with
an answer, converts your biggest weakness into a demonstration that you've thought about it
harder than they have.

## Where it goes wrong

- Arguing with the objection instead of writing it down.
- Treating every no as a fund-specific mandate issue.
- Changing the pitch after every meeting, so nothing gets tested twice.
- Never asking, at the end of a pass, what the actual reason was.
