## Why it matters

Raising too little is the more common and more dangerous error. A company that runs out before
hitting its milestones raises from a position of weakness, and that shows up as a down round, a
bridge on bad terms, or no round at all.

Raising too much is a real cost too — dilution now, and a valuation that sets a bar the next
round has to clear. But the asymmetry is stark: excess dilution is survivable, running out
isn't.

## What it looks like in practice

Work backwards from the next round's expectations, convert those into a plan and a burn rate,
and add buffer — eighteen to twenty-four months is the usual target, because the milestones slip
and the raise itself takes months.

The dilution math is the counterweight. A typical seed sells 15–25%, and stacking oversized
rounds early leaves founders with too little by Series B to stay motivated, which later
investors notice and price.

## Where it goes wrong

- Sizing to what investors offer rather than to the milestone plan.
- Planning exactly to the milestone with no buffer for slippage.
- Raising a large round at a valuation the next round can't clear.
- Ignoring that the raise itself consumes three to six months of runway.
