## Why it matters

Total addressable market is the ceiling on how big this could ever get, and its real function
for a fund is checking whether the outcome could be large enough to matter under the power law.

It's also the most inflated number in any deck, which means an inflated one costs credibility
rather than buying it. A defensible $400M market beats an indefensible $90B one.

## What it looks like in practice

The credible construction is bottom-up: countable customers times realistic contract value. That
produces a smaller number and a much better conversation, because every input can be examined
and argued with.

TAM also isn't static. Markets expand when a product makes something possible that wasn't
before, and the strongest version of the argument explains that expansion rather than claiming a
share of an existing total.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting an analyst report's industry figure.
- The 1% fallacy, which describes no plan at all.
- Defining the market to include people who will never buy this.
- Presenting TAM alone without the serviceable and obtainable slices.
