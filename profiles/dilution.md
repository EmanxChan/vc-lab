## Why it matters

Dilution is not a problem to be avoided — it's the cost of the capital that makes the company
worth more. A smaller slice of a much bigger thing is the entire mechanism of venture. The
failure is dilution without corresponding progress.

For an investor, dilution is what stands between an initial ownership percentage and the one
that exists at exit. Without pro rata and reserves, that gap is large enough to change the
fund's outcome.

## What it looks like in practice

Sources compound: each priced round, each option pool top-up, each SAFE converting, each warrant
exercised. A founding team at 100% commonly holds 10–20% by Series B, and that's a normal path
rather than a bad one.

The lever an investor has is pro rata rights plus reserved capital. The lever a founder has is
raising the right amount at the right time rather than the most available.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating dilution as something to minimise rather than something to spend well.
- Forgetting the option pool, which is dilution that arrives without a cheque.
- Modelling one round at a time rather than the full path to exit.
- Holding pro rata rights and no reserves, which is the same as not having them.
