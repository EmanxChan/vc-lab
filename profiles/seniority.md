## Why it matters

Seniority decides the order of payment when there isn't enough to go around, which is exactly
the situation the preference exists for. Later rounds often negotiate seniority over earlier
ones, meaning the newest money gets paid first.

For an early investor that's the quiet risk in later rounds. A position that was first in line
at seed can be third by Series C without anyone renegotiating the original terms.

## What it looks like in practice

Two structures dominate. Stacked seniority pays the most recent series first, then works
backwards. Pari passu pays all series proportionally at the same time.

Stacked is more common in later rounds where the incoming investor has leverage. Which structure
applies is set in the charter and can be changed by a later financing if the protective
provisions allow it.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming pari passu without checking the charter.
- Ignoring that a later round can rewrite the seniority you agreed to.
- Modelling a waterfall without the payment order, which makes the model wrong.
- Reading seniority as only mattering in bankruptcy. It matters in every exit below the stack.
