## Why it matters

NRR measures what happens to revenue from existing customers over a year, including expansion,
contraction, and churn. Above 100% means the existing base grows without a single new logo.

That's the quiet engine behind most exceptional SaaS businesses. A company at 130% NRR grows
meaningfully every year even if sales stops entirely, which is a fundamentally different asset
from one at 85%.

## What it looks like in practice

It's calculated on a cohort of customers at the start of a period, comparing their revenue at
the end — so it captures upsell and downgrade as well as loss.

The pricing structure largely determines it. Per-seat and usage-based models produce natural
expansion; flat pricing cannot exceed 100% without a renegotiation.

## Where it goes wrong

- Including new customers in the calculation, which inflates it.
- Reporting NRR without gross retention alongside, which hides heavy churn masked by upsell.
- Comparing NRR across companies with different contract structures.
- Assuming a high NRR from a small number of expanding accounts is durable.
