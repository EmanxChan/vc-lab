## Why it matters

Advisor equity is cheap in isolation and expensive in aggregate. Eight advisors at 0.25% is 2%
of the company — more than the entire seed-stage option budget for early engineers, given to
people whose contribution is usually unmeasured.

It also clutters a cap table. A long list of small holders makes future rounds administratively
slower and signals a founder who traded equity for reassurance.

## What it looks like in practice

Typical grants run from about 0.1% for light involvement to around 1% for someone genuinely
operationally engaged, vesting monthly over one or two years with a short cliff. The FAST
agreement published by the Founder Institute is the common template and includes tiers by
involvement.

The test worth applying before granting: name the specific thing this person will do in the next
six months. If the answer is "credibility," it's a logo purchase, and logos rarely close rounds.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting equity for a name on a website with no defined commitment.
- No vesting, so an advisor who disengages after a month keeps the full grant.
- Accumulating advisors until the total exceeds what the early team holds.
- Granting to someone whose value is a single introduction that could have been a thank-you.
