## Why it matters

Activation is the highest-leverage number in most product-led businesses, because it sits
between acquisition and everything downstream. Doubling signups with a broken activation step
doubles the number of people who leave.

It's also the number founders skip. Signups are easy to count and feel like progress; activation
requires deciding what value actually means, which is a harder and more revealing question.

## What it looks like in practice

Defining it means finding the behaviour that separates users who stay from users who leave —
usually an action plus a timeframe. The classic examples are famous for a reason: the specific
number matters less than having one at all.

Once defined, the work is removing everything between signup and that moment. Most activation
gains come from deleting steps rather than adding onboarding.

## Where it goes wrong

- Defining activation as account creation, which measures nothing.
- Picking a metric that correlates with retention without asking whether it causes it.
- Optimising signups while activation is broken.
- Adding an onboarding tour instead of removing the friction the tour explains.
