## Why it matters

The hurdle is the minimum return LPs receive before the GP participates in profits. It exists
because LPs could have put the money in something safer, and the GP should only be paid for
beating that alternative.

In venture, hurdles are less universal than in private equity — many venture funds have none, on
the reasoning that venture risk is not comparable to a fixed return threshold. Where they exist,
8% is the conventional number.

## What it looks like in practice

The hurdle is usually expressed as an annual IRR on contributed capital, and whether it
compounds matters over a ten-year fund. It's calculated across the fund, not per deal, unless
the LPA says otherwise.

The interaction with the catch-up is what determines the economics. A hurdle with a full catch-
up behaves very differently from one without.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming a venture fund has a hurdle. Many don't.
- Ignoring whether the preferred return compounds, which matters a great deal over ten years.
- Modelling the hurdle without the catch-up tier that follows it.
- Confusing a hard hurdle with a soft one — they produce very different outcomes.
