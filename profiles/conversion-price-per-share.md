## Why it matters

The conversion price determines how many common shares each preferred share becomes. It starts
at the original issue price and moves down when anti-dilution provisions fire.

A lower conversion price means more common shares per preferred share, which is how anti-
dilution protection actually delivers — not by changing what was paid, but by changing what it
converts into.

## What it looks like in practice

The conversion ratio is the original issue price divided by the current conversion price.
Unadjusted, that's 1:1. After a down round with weighted-average anti-dilution, it might be
1:1.3.

The dilution lands on common. Those extra shares come from somewhere, and it's the founders and
employees who hold the shares being diluted.

## Where it goes wrong

- Confusing the conversion price with the liquidation preference basis, which stays at OIP.
- Forgetting that the conversion ratio changes the fully diluted count.
- Modelling conversion at 1:1 after a down round has triggered anti-dilution.
- Ignoring which anti-dilution formula applies, since they produce very different adjustments.
