## Why it matters

Return of capital is the first tier of the waterfall: LPs get their money back before anyone
shares in profits. It's the foundation of the alignment, and the definition of "their money" is
worth reading closely.

The question is whether it means capital invested in companies, or all capital contributed
including fees and expenses. The difference is roughly 20% of the fund over its life, which is
not a detail.

## What it looks like in practice

LP-favourable structures return all contributed capital — investments plus fees plus expenses —
before any carry is paid. GP-favourable ones return only invested capital, which lets carry
start earlier.

The distinction also interacts with the waterfall type. Under an American waterfall the return-
of-capital test may be applied deal by deal rather than across the whole fund.

## Where it goes wrong

- Not checking whether fees are included in the capital that must be returned.
- Assuming return of capital means LPs are whole. It usually precedes the preferred return.
- Modelling DPI of 1.0 as break-even for LPs when fees mean it isn't.
- Overlooking how the definition interacts with deal-by-deal distributions.
