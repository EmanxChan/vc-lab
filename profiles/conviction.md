## Why it matters

Conviction is the willingness to act on a judgment that others don't share — which is the only
way to generate returns in a competitive market. If everyone agrees, the price already reflects
it.

The difficulty is distinguishing conviction from stubbornness. Both feel identical from the
inside, and only the written reasoning separates them afterwards.

## What it looks like in practice

The discipline that helps is writing down what you believe and what would change your mind,
before committing. That converts conviction into something testable rather than something felt.

Conviction should also be proportionate. High conviction justifies a larger position; it doesn't
justify skipping diligence, and the two get conflated regularly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating enthusiasm as conviction. They feel the same and aren't.
- Not recording what would falsify the thesis, which makes updating impossible.
- Using conviction as a reason to shorten diligence.
- Maintaining conviction after the evidence changed, which is just commitment escalation.
