## Why it matters

Acquisition is how the large majority of venture-backed exits happen. IPOs get the attention;
M&A produces the returns.

The process is where all the terms negotiated years earlier come due at once — the preference
stack, the drag-along, the deemed liquidation definition, vesting acceleration. None of them
matter until they all matter simultaneously.

## What it looks like in practice

The sequence runs from approach through LOI with exclusivity, diligence, definitive agreements,
and close. Leverage peaks before the LOI is signed and declines steadily afterwards.

The most common failure is a retrade: diligence surfaces something, the price drops, and the
seller has no alternative because exclusivity removed it. Cleaning up diligence problems before
starting is the main defence.

## Where it goes wrong

- Signing a long exclusivity without a closing deadline.
- Starting a process without knowing where the disagreement range sits.
- Leaving known diligence problems to be discovered by the buyer.
- Running a process with one buyer, which removes every source of leverage.
