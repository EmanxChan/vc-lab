## Why it matters

Elimination by aspects is the decision heuristic of screening on one criterion at a time and
discarding anything that fails, rather than weighing everything together. It's fast and it's how
most screening actually works.

The cost is that it discards companies that are exceptional on one dimension and weak on the
criterion being applied first — which, in a power-law business, is exactly the profile of the
outlier.

## What it looks like in practice

The mitigation is ordering the criteria deliberately, applying the ones with the highest
information value first, and keeping a record of what was eliminated at each stage.

It's also worth periodically re-examining eliminations under a different order, which is one of
the more useful things the anti-portfolio can be used for.

## Where it goes wrong

- Applying criteria in whatever order they occur to you.
- Eliminating on a criterion that's weakly related to outcomes.
- Not recording eliminations, which makes the blind spots invisible.
- Using it at every stage, when later stages should weigh factors together.
