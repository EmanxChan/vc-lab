## Why it matters

Diligence at pre-seed is not the same exercise as at later stages. There's no revenue to audit
and no cohort data to analyse — the work is about the team, the market, and whether the claimed
evidence is real.

The most valuable diligence is usually reference checking done properly: former colleagues,
customers, and people who worked with the founders when things went badly.

## What it looks like in practice

The checkable items are legal hygiene (IP assignment, 83(b) filings, clean cap table), the
reality of claimed traction (are pilots paid, do customers actually use it), and references
taken off-list rather than from the founder's own set.

Speed is a real constraint. Competitive rounds close in days, and diligence that takes six weeks
means not investing. The answer is knowing in advance what you must check and what you can
accept.

## Where it goes wrong

- Running enterprise-grade diligence on a pre-seed company and losing the deal.
- Only taking references the founder provided.
- Accepting traction claims without asking what "customer" and "pilot" mean specifically.
- Skipping legal hygiene because it's boring. It's where the real problems hide.
