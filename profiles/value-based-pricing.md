## Why it matters

It's the difference between capturing a slice of the value you create and capturing almost none
of it. Cost-plus pricing is the single most common way early companies leave money on the table,
and the mistake compounds — every later price rise is a fight, and the first price anchors the
segment you attract.

It matters especially for AI products, where cost of delivery is falling and has almost no
relationship to the value of the output.

## What it looks like in practice

The work is quantifying the outcome in the customer's own numbers: hours saved times loaded
cost, error rate reduction times cost of an error, revenue recovered. Then you price at a
fraction of that — enough that the ROI is obvious and the customer keeps most of the gain.

Different segments value the same outcome differently, which is the honest argument for tiered
pricing. The enterprise isn't subsidising the startup; it's buying a bigger outcome.

## Where it goes wrong

- Pricing off your own cost base, which the customer neither knows nor cares about.
- Quantifying value with numbers you made up rather than ones the customer gave you.
- Setting one price for segments that get wildly different value.
- Confusing it with charging as much as possible — the customer has to keep the larger share.
