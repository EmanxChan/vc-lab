## Why it matters

Under an American waterfall carry is calculated on each investment as it exits, which means the
GP receives carry on early winners before the fund's overall outcome is known.

That's GP-favourable on timing and creates the risk the clawback exists to address: carry paid
on an early success may have to be returned if later losses mean LPs didn't actually clear their
preferred return across the fund.

## What it looks like in practice

Because of that risk, deal-by-deal structures come with protective machinery: interim clawback
tests, escrow accounts holding back a portion of carry, and personal guarantees from individual
partners.

The structure has become less common as LPs have pushed back, and ILPA's principles explicitly
favour whole-of-fund distribution.

## Where it goes wrong

- Spending carry received under a deal-by-deal waterfall before the clawback risk has passed.
- Agreeing to deal-by-deal without escrow or interim testing.
- Assuming the clawback is enforceable in practice. Recovering paid carry from individuals is
  hard.
- Modelling the fund's economics without accounting for the timing difference this creates.
