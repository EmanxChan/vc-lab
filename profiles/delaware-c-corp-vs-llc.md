## Why it matters

Structure decides whether an institutional round is straightforward or a project. Funds expect
Delaware C-Corp because the case law is deep and predictable, preferred stock works the way
everyone assumes, and the tax treatment doesn't create problems for their LPs.

It also gates QSBS, which only applies to C-Corp stock. For a founder, choosing an LLC to save
early tax can forfeit an exclusion worth millions later.

## What it looks like in practice

LLCs make sense for consulting firms, real-estate vehicles, and anything designed to distribute
profits rather than compound value toward an exit. C-Corps make sense for anything intending to
raise venture money and issue options.

Converting an LLC to a C-Corp is possible and routinely done, but it costs time and legal fees
at the worst moment, and it can reset the QSBS holding clock. Starting in the right structure is
cheaper than fixing it.

## Where it goes wrong

- Choosing an LLC for early tax simplicity when institutional funding is the plan.
- Assuming conversion is trivial. It is a real project with real cost.
- Incorporating in your home state to save a few hundred dollars, then converting to Delaware
  anyway.
- Forgetting that the QSBS clock starts at C-Corp stock issuance.
