## Why it matters

Tag-along is the mirror of drag-along, and it protects minority holders rather than compelling
them. If a major shareholder sells, everyone else gets to participate proportionally on the same
terms.

The problem it solves is a founder or large investor quietly selling their position at a good
price while everyone else stays in. Tag-along makes that impossible without bringing the others
along.

## What it looks like in practice

It usually applies to sales above a threshold and gives other holders a defined window to elect
to participate. In practice it makes large private secondary sales slower and more visible,
which is much of the point.

It pairs with the right of first refusal in the standard NVCA co-sale agreement — the company
and then investors get first refusal, and whatever isn't taken up is subject to tag-along.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming it applies to every sale rather than only above the threshold.
- Missing the election window, which is usually short.
- Not realising founder secondary sales may be exempted in the drafting.
- Treating tag-along and ROFR as the same right. They operate in sequence.
