## Why it matters

Pari passu means all series of preferred rank equally and get paid proportionally rather than in
order. When proceeds fall short, everyone takes the same percentage haircut.

It's the more founder-friendly and earlier-investor-friendly structure, because it prevents the
newest money from jumping the queue. It's also simpler to model, which matters more than it
sounds.

## What it looks like in practice

If preferences total $40M and proceeds are $20M, pari passu pays every series 50% of what it's
owed. Stacked seniority would pay the most recent series in full and leave earlier ones with
nothing.

The structure is usually set at the first priced round and defended in later ones. Whether it
survives depends on how much leverage the incoming investor has.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming pari passu is the default. In competitive later rounds it often isn't.
- Not checking whether a new round's terms change the ranking of existing series.
- Modelling proportional payment when the charter specifies an order.
- Reading pari passu as protection against the size of the stack. It only governs order.
