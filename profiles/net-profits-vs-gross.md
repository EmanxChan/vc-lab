## Why it matters

Gross returns are what the portfolio produced. Net returns are what LPs received after
management fees and carry. The gap is large and it's the number that actually matters to anyone
writing a cheque into a fund.

A gross 3x can become a net 2.2x or lower depending on fees and carry. Managers quote gross when
it flatters and net when it doesn't, which is why the basis should always be stated.

## What it looks like in practice

Over a ten-year fund a 2% annual fee consumes roughly 20% of committed capital, and carry takes
20% of profits above the return of capital. Both have to be modelled to get to a net number.

For an LP the comparison that matters is net-to-LP against public market equivalents, because
that's the actual alternative use of the money.

## Where it goes wrong

- Comparing one fund's gross return to another's net.
- Quoting a multiple without stating whether it's gross or net.
- Ignoring fee drag when modelling what the portfolio has to return.
- Assuming a strong gross portfolio automatically produces a strong net outcome.
