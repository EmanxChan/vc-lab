## Why it matters

Under a European waterfall no carry is paid until LPs have received all their contributed
capital plus the preferred return across the entire fund. It's the LP-favourable structure and
the standard in Europe, increasingly common everywhere.

The practical effect is that clawback risk mostly disappears, because the GP isn't paid until
the fund's overall outcome is known.

## What it looks like in practice

For the GP it means carry arrives late — often year seven or later — which is a real cash-flow
problem for a firm whose partners need to be paid in the meantime. That's part of why management
fees matter so much.

For LPs it removes the risk of paying carry on early winners and then watching later losses
reverse the gains without a reliable way to get the money back.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming European means no clawback provision is needed. One is still included.
- Modelling GP cash flows as though carry arrives with each exit.
- Treating the two waterfall types as economically equivalent over the fund's life. Timing has
  real value.
- Not checking which structure applies before modelling fund economics.
