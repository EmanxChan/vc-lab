## Why it matters

The option pool is how a company that can't pay market salaries competes for people, and it's
dilution that arrives without any cash coming in. Where it comes from in a round is one of the
more consequential negotiations founders don't realise they're having.

Created out of the pre-money, it's borne entirely by existing holders — which lowers the
effective valuation below the headline. Created post-money, it's shared with the incoming
investor.

## What it looks like in practice

Pools are typically sized at 10–20% of the post-round company, informed by a hiring plan through
to the next round. Investors usually push for enough to cover that plan so they aren't diluted
by a top-up later.

Strike price comes from the 409A, which is why the timing of the appraisal relative to grants
matters. Promising a strike price before the valuation refreshes is a common way to disappoint a
new hire.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing the pre-money before agreeing the pool size, which changes the effective price.
- Sizing the pool from a hiring plan nobody believes.
- Granting options against a stale 409A.
- Leaving unallocated pool out of the fully diluted count.
