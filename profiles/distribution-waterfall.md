## Why it matters

The waterfall is the mechanism that turns proceeds into payments, and its structure determines
when the GP gets paid and how much risk LPs carry. It's the single most important economic
section of the LPA.

The four tiers — return of capital, preferred return, GP catch-up, then the carry split — look
procedural but each one is negotiated, and the differences compound across a fund's life.

## What it looks like in practice

Money fills each tier completely before spilling into the next. LPs get their capital back, then
their preferred return, then the GP catches up to its carry percentage, then everything splits
at the agreed rate.

Whether the waterfall runs on the whole fund or deal by deal is the biggest single variable, and
it changes the timing of GP payments by years.

## Where it goes wrong

- Modelling carry as a simple percentage of gains without running the tiers.
- Ignoring which capital counts as returned — invested only, or invested plus fees.
- Not checking whether the preferred return compounds.
- Assuming the waterfall type is standard. It varies more than the fee and carry do.
