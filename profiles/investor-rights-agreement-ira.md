## Why it matters

The term sheet gets the attention and the IRA governs the relationship. Rights that seem minor
at signing — inspection rights, registration rights, pro rata — determine how much of the
founder's time investors can consume and how much of the next round they can take.

It's also where the difference between a well-run round and a messy one shows up later. Granting
broad rights to many small investors creates an administrative burden that compounds every
quarter.

## What it looks like in practice

Most rounds limit the heavier rights to major investors above a threshold, which keeps the
obligations manageable. Registration rights rarely get exercised but matter at IPO; pro rata
rights matter at every subsequent round.

For a small fund the pro rata right is the most valuable thing in the document, because it's
what allows concentration into winners without needing a new allocation.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting full information and inspection rights to every small holder.
- Ignoring registration rights until an IPO makes them suddenly relevant.
- Letting pro rata rights sit with investors who won't follow on, blocking allocation for those
  who will.
- Negotiating hard on the term sheet and signing the IRA without reading it.
