## Why it matters

The lead does the work everyone else free-rides on: the diligence, the price, the terms, and
usually the board seat. That's why rounds stall without one — nobody wants to set the price, and
everybody is willing to follow it.

For a small fund or an angel, the decision to lead or follow is strategic. Leading buys
ownership, information, and a relationship. Following buys access at someone else's price with
someone else's diligence.

## What it looks like in practice

A lead typically takes a substantial share of the round, negotiates the term sheet, and takes
the board seat. The commitment signals to the rest of the market that someone with real money at
stake did the work.

The quality of the lead matters beyond the cheque. A lead with a strong reputation makes the
rest of the round easier to fill, and a lead who negotiates aggressive terms leaves a structure
the company lives with for a decade.

## Where it goes wrong

- Following into a round without reading the terms the lead negotiated.
- Assuming a well-known lead did thorough diligence. Sometimes they didn't.
- Trying to lead without the capital to fill a meaningful share of the round.
- Choosing a lead on price alone, when the board relationship lasts far longer.
