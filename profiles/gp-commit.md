## Why it matters

The GP commit is the partners' own money in their own fund, and it's the clearest alignment
signal available to an LP. A manager with meaningful personal capital at risk makes different
decisions from one who only has upside.

The conventional benchmark is 1–2% of fund size, though LPs care more about what it represents
to the individual than what it represents to the fund. A large percentage from someone wealthy
signals less than a smaller one that matters.

## What it looks like in practice

For emerging managers the GP commit is a real constraint — 2% of a $20M fund is $400,000, paid
over the fund's life, by someone who has just given up a salary.

Some structures allow the commit to be funded by waiving management fees, which reduces the cash
burden. LPs vary on whether they consider that genuine alignment.

## Where it goes wrong

- Funding the entire commit through fee waivers and calling it skin in the game.
- Sizing a fund without accounting for the personal cash the commit requires.
- Assuming a percentage is what matters rather than what it represents to the individual.
- Forgetting the commit is called over time alongside LP capital, not paid up front.
