## Why it matters

Warrants are the sweetener that gets a deal done when price alone won't. They show up most often
in bridge rounds, venture debt, and situations where the investor is taking more risk than the
headline terms compensate for.

For the company they're deferred dilution, and because they sit outside the ordinary cap table
conversation they're easy to under-count. Fully diluted ownership includes them; a founder's
mental model often doesn't.

## What it looks like in practice

Coverage is quoted as a percentage of the investment, with a strike price usually set at the
current or next round price and a long exercise window. Venture debt lenders commonly take
warrants alongside interest, which is much of how that asset class makes its return.

In a bridge, warrant coverage is often the alternative to repricing — existing investors put in
more money on the old terms and take warrants to compensate for the added risk.

## Where it goes wrong

- Leaving warrants out of the fully diluted count, so ownership math is quietly wrong.
- Granting long-dated warrants at a low strike that overhang every later round.
- Using warrants to avoid a difficult price conversation, and paying more in dilution than a
  lower price would have cost.
- Forgetting they exist until an acquirer's diligence finds them.
