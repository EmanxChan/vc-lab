## Why it matters

The ICP is the single fastest read on whether a founder has done the work. A vague ICP means
every downstream number is guesswork — the market size, the CAC, the sales cycle, all of it. A
sharp ICP means the founder has talked to enough people to know who says yes quickly and who
wastes six weeks before saying no.

It also predicts burn. Selling to everyone means qualifying nobody, and a seed-stage company
cannot afford a sales motion that discovers the customer isn't a fit on call four.

## What it looks like in practice

Ask a founder who their customer is and listen for whether the answer contains a filter you
could apply to a database. Headcount, industry, the tool they use today, who signs, what breaks.
If the answer is a size band and a vertical, they are guessing.

The good version usually arrives with evidence attached: "these twelve closed in under three
weeks, these four took a quarter and two churned, and the difference is whether they have a
dedicated ops person." That is a founder who found the ICP rather than assumed it.

## Where it goes wrong

- Writing the ICP from the market you want rather than the customers who actually bought.
- Confusing it with a persona. The ICP is the account; the persona is the human inside it.
- Making it so broad it excludes nobody, which makes it useless as a filter.
- Never revising it. The first ICP is a hypothesis, and the first twenty customers are the test.
