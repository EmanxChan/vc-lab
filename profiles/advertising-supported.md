## Why it matters

Advertising removes all friction from adoption and puts the business in permanent tension with
its own users. Every decision that increases revenue — more ads, more tracking, more engagement
optimisation — makes the product slightly worse for the person using it.

It also demands scale that most startups never reach. At realistic ad rates, meaningful revenue
requires millions of engaged users. A company with fifty thousand users cannot fund itself on
ads, no matter how good the engagement.

## What it looks like in practice

The economics come down to how much attention you have and how well you can target it. Niche
audiences with clear purchase intent command far higher rates than large general ones — a
newsletter for procurement managers can out-earn a general-interest app with fifty times the
readers.

Privacy changes have made third-party targeting materially worse, which pushed value toward
first-party data and contextual placement. That favours products where the content itself
signals intent.

## Where it goes wrong

- Choosing ads for a product that will never reach advertising scale.
- Optimising engagement metrics that make the product worse and users leave slowly.
- Building on third-party tracking that platform policy can remove overnight.
- Treating users as the customer in product decisions and advertisers as the customer in the
  model.
