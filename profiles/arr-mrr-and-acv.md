## Why it matters

These are the numbers every investor asks for first, and the ones most often reported loosely.
Recurring means contractually repeating — not revenue that happened to recur, and not one-off
implementation fees annualised into a bigger-sounding number.

ACV is the one founders skip and investors care about, because it determines the go-to-market. A
$2,000 ACV cannot support a salesperson; a $200,000 ACV cannot be sold self-serve. The number
implies the whole cost structure.

## What it looks like in practice

ARR is normally MRR times twelve rather than trailing revenue, which makes it a run-rate — a
forward-looking snapshot, not a historical figure. That's fine as long as everyone knows which
is being quoted.

The variants matter in diligence: committed versus annualised, gross versus net of churn, and
whether services revenue is included. Founders quoting a big number without specifying which are
the ones to slow down.

## Where it goes wrong

- Annualising a single good month into ARR.
- Including one-off setup, services, or usage overage in recurring revenue.
- Counting signed pilots that haven't started billing.
- Reporting ARR growth without ACV, hiding whether growth came from more customers or bigger
  ones.
