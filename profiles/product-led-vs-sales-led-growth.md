## Why it matters

The choice has to match the contract size, and the arithmetic is unforgiving. A sales-led motion
costs real money per deal — salary, commission, time — which needs a contract large enough to
carry it. Product-led motions cost almost nothing per user but need volume and a product simple
enough to self-serve.

The mismatch is a common way to burn a seed round: a $3,000-a-year product sold with a $15,000
sales process, or a $200,000 enterprise deal expected to close itself.

## What it looks like in practice

Product-led requires a user who can adopt without permission, value visible before a purchase
decision, and no mandatory integration work. Sales-led is what you get when the buyer isn't the
user, the purchase needs procurement, or the deployment needs someone's help.

Most companies end up with both, aimed at different segments — self-serve for the small end,
sales for the enterprise. That's fine as long as they're deliberately separated rather than one
motion doing both badly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Choosing PLG because it sounds cheaper, on a product that needs onboarding to be useful.
- Running enterprise sales on a contract value that can't fund a salesperson.
- Adding self-serve to an enterprise product and starving it of the support that made it work.
- Measuring PLG on signups instead of activation.
