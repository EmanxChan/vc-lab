## Why it matters

A markup is an increase in the carried value of a position, usually because a new round priced
higher. It feels like performance and isn't — it's someone else's pricing decision recorded in
your books.

Markups are also how early-stage funds show progress before any distributions exist, which
creates an incentive to chase them. That incentive is worth being conscious of.

## What it looks like in practice

The convention is to mark to the most recent priced round. That makes marks sticky and lumpy —
nothing changes for two years, then everything changes at once.

A markup is genuine information about a company's trajectory, and it is not a realisation. The
honest framing is that it raises the probability of a good outcome without delivering one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting markups as returns.
- Optimising for markups rather than for exits, which are different objectives.
- Marking up on a round with heavy structure, where the headline price overstates value.
- Failing to mark down when conditions clearly changed.
