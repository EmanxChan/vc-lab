## Why it matters

A net-of-tax clawback limits the GP's repayment obligation to what it actually kept after paying
tax on the carry. The argument is straightforward: a GP who received $1M and paid $400,000 in
tax shouldn't have to return $1M.

For LPs it's a real reduction in recovery, and it's one of the terms where reasonable positions
genuinely conflict.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanics involve assumptions about the GP's tax rate, which are specified in the LPA rather
than calculated individually. Whether the GP can amend prior returns to recover the tax affects
how fair the provision is.

It's common enough to be considered market, but the specific assumed rate and whether it
accounts for actual tax recovery are worth reading.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming the clawback is gross when the LPA specifies net-of-tax.
- Modelling LP recovery at 100% of paid carry when the provision limits it.
- Not checking the assumed tax rate, which can be set favourably to the GP.
- Ignoring whether the GP can amend prior tax returns, which changes what's genuinely lost.
