## Why it matters

The 30-day 83(b) window is one of the few genuinely unforgivable deadlines in startup formation.
There is no extension, no cure, and no workaround — and the cost of missing it scales with how
well the company does.

For an investor this is a data-room hygiene check that occasionally uncovers something serious.
Missing 83(b) filings, founder stock never actually issued, or IP sitting with an individual
rather than the company are the kind of problems that are cheap to fix at pre-seed and expensive
at Series A.

## What it looks like in practice

The sequence is: incorporate, issue founder stock at a nominal price, put it on a vesting
schedule, file 83(b) with the IRS within 30 days of issuance, and assign all relevant IP to the
company. Standard formation packages handle all of it, and the cost of doing it properly is a
rounding error against the cost of not.

The election applies to stock subject to vesting. Options are a different instrument with
different timing, which is a common source of confusion when early employees ask.

## Where it goes wrong

- Missing the 30-day window. It cannot be fixed afterwards.
- Filing 83(b) but keeping no proof of mailing, which leaves you unable to demonstrate it.
- Issuing founder stock late, after the company already has value, so the nominal price no
  longer works.
- Leaving IP assigned to individuals — especially work done before incorporation.
