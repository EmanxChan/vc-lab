## Why it matters

Inbound flow arrives because of who you are; outbound flow happens because of what you do. Both
matter, and they select for different things.

Inbound is efficient and biased — it reflects your existing reputation and network, which means
it systematically misses founders outside it. Outbound is expensive and is the only way to reach
companies that would never have found you.

## What it looks like in practice

For an established firm inbound dominates, and the work is filtering. For a new manager outbound
is most of the job, and the leverage comes from being specific: a narrow thesis makes outbound
research tractable and makes the outreach credible.

The best outbound doesn't look like outreach. It looks like being genuinely useful in a
community where the founders you want already are.

## Where it goes wrong

- Waiting for inbound before a reputation exists to generate it.
- Treating outbound as a volume exercise rather than a research one.
- Not tracking which channel produced which investment.
- Assuming inbound quality reflects the market rather than your own network.
