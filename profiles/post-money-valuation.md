## Why it matters

Post-money is what ownership is calculated from, which makes it the number that matters to an
investor. It's also the number that sets the bar for the next round — a post-money that is too
high relative to progress makes the following raise structurally harder.

For a fund it's the input to every ownership calculation and every markup. Getting it wrong
propagates through the whole portfolio model.

## What it looks like in practice

Post-money equals pre-money plus the round. Ownership equals the cheque divided by post-money —
before accounting for any option pool created as part of the round, which dilutes everyone who
was there before.

Post-money SAFEs use the same word for a related but distinct idea: the SAFE's post-money
valuation fixes the holder's percentage at conversion, which is why the instrument behaves so
differently from its pre-money predecessor.

## Where it goes wrong

- Calculating ownership off pre-money, which overstates it.
- Ignoring the option pool when computing what you actually own after the round.
- Setting a post-money that the next round's expected progress can't justify.
- Reporting a markup based on a post-money that included a large pool top-up.
