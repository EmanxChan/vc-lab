## Why it matters

The fund is the vehicle that actually holds the investments — a limited partnership with a
defined life, a defined size, and a defined strategy. Everything else in the structure exists to
manage it.

Its finite life is the constraint that shapes venture behaviour. A ten-year fund cannot hold a
position indefinitely, which is why GPs push for exits on timelines that sometimes have nothing
to do with what's best for the company.

## What it looks like in practice

A typical structure is ten years with two one-year extensions, an investment period of three to
five years, and a defined strategy the LPA constrains the GP to follow.

Each fund is separate. A firm running Fund I, Fund II, and Fund III has three distinct pools
with three sets of LPs, three waterfalls, and three sets of obligations — which is where
conflicts between funds come from.

## Where it goes wrong

- Modelling a fund's return without accounting for its finite life.
- Cross-investing between funds without addressing the conflict properly.
- Assuming extensions are automatic. They usually require LP consent.
- Ignoring that the strategy in the LPA is a constraint, not a description.
