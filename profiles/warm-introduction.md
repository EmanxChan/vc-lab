## Why it matters

The introduction is a filter that costs the introducer something. Because their reputation is
attached, a warm intro carries information a cold email cannot, which is why response rates
differ by an order of magnitude.

The uncomfortable part is that it's also a network tax, and it falls hardest on founders without
existing access. For an investor, over-reliance on warm intros is the mechanism by which a
portfolio ends up looking exactly like the network that sourced it — which is a returns problem
before it's anything else.

## What it looks like in practice

The strongest introductions come from portfolio founders, because their incentive is clear and
their judgment is tested. Next best are other investors who have looked and passed for reasons
unrelated to quality — wrong stage, wrong sector, no allocation.

The forwardable email is the mechanic that makes it work: a short note the introducer can send
without writing anything themselves. Founders who make the introducer do the work get fewer
introductions.

## Where it goes wrong

- Asking for an intro before knowing whether the fund invests at your stage or in your sector.
- Making the introducer write the pitch. Send something forwardable.
- Treating intros as the only channel, and never building direct relationships.
- As an investor, relying on them so heavily that dealflow only reflects the existing network.
