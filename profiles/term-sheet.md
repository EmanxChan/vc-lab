## Why it matters

The term sheet is short, non-binding, and decides almost everything. Once it's signed, the
definitive documents are largely an exercise in writing down what it already says — which means
the leverage is all before signature, not after.

The parts founders focus on are price and amount. The parts that determine control and payout
are the preference, the protective provisions, the board, and the anti-dilution, and those are
the ones worth spending negotiating capital on.

## What it looks like in practice

The binding elements are usually confidentiality, exclusivity, and expenses — the no-shop in
particular, which stops the company from talking to anyone else while the deal is documented.

Working from NVCA-standard terms shortens the process enormously, because the negotiation
narrows to the things that actually differ rather than the whole document.

## Where it goes wrong

- Negotiating price hard and accepting everything else as standard.
- Signing with a long no-shop and no deadline for closing.
- Assuming non-binding means nothing is committed. Exclusivity is binding and it costs you
  leverage.
- Not modelling the exit waterfall the terms produce before signing them.
