## Why it matters

Realised gains are cash that exists. Unrealised gains are a valuation — usually the price of the
last round, which is an estimate produced by a negotiation between other people.

The gap between them is where most venture optimism lives. A fund reporting a 4x TVPI with a
0.3x DPI is reporting one fact and one hope, and the distinction matters enormously when markets
reprice.

## What it looks like in practice

Unrealised value is normally carried at the most recent round price, marked down when there's
evidence of impairment. That convention means marks are sticky — a company that raised at a high
valuation two years ago may still be carried there.

The discipline is to look at DPI alongside TVPI, and at the age of the marks. A portfolio whose
value rests on rounds priced in a very different market is carrying more risk than the number
suggests.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting TVPI without DPI alongside it.
- Carrying a position at a stale round price long after the market moved.
- Treating a markup as evidence of judgment. The next round's price isn't your decision.
- Comparing funds on unrealised value at different points in their lives.
