## Why it matters

Deals fail when a founder wins one role and assumes that's the sale. Enthusiastic users with no
budget produce months of pleasant meetings. A buyer with no internal champion signs and never
deploys.

It's also a diligence question worth asking directly: in your last five closed deals, who was
the champion, and what did they get out of it? Founders who can answer have actually run the
process.

## What it looks like in practice

The champion is the one to invest in, because they do the internal selling you can't. They need
ammunition — a business case in their language, numbers they can forward, and a reason this
makes them look good.

The risk is single-threading. If your champion leaves, the deal restarts from zero, and champion
turnover is one of the more common causes of a stalled enterprise pipeline. Multi-threading into
a second relationship is insurance.

## Where it goes wrong

- Selling to the user and never finding out who signs.
- Single-threading a large deal through one relationship.
- Giving the champion features when what they need is a business case.
- Assuming the loudest person in the room is the decision maker.
