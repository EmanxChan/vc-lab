## Why it matters

A soft hurdle delays the GP's carry; a hard hurdle permanently reduces it. Under a soft hurdle
with full catch-up, the GP eventually receives carry on all profits. Under a hard hurdle, the GP
only receives carry on profits above the threshold.

The difference is substantial and it's expressed in a single word in the LPA. Soft hurdles
dominate in practice, which is why they're often described simply as "a hurdle."

## What it looks like in practice

With a soft hurdle and an 8% preferred return, once LPs clear 8% the catch-up gives the GP 20%
of everything from the first dollar of profit. With a hard hurdle, the first 8% is permanently
excluded from the carry calculation.

On a fund returning 3x, the difference between the two structures runs to a significant share of
the total carry.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming a hurdle is hard when almost all are soft.
- Modelling the two identically, which produces materially wrong carry numbers.
- Negotiating the hurdle rate without establishing which type applies.
- Ignoring the interaction with the catch-up rate, which determines the effective outcome.
