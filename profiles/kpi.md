## Why it matters

KPIs are the small set of numbers a company manages by. The value is in the selection: measuring
everything is equivalent to measuring nothing, because attention is the scarce resource.

For an investor the KPIs a founder chooses are informative in themselves. A team tracking
signups and pageviews is thinking differently from one tracking activation and cohort retention.

## What it looks like in practice

Good KPIs are few, leading rather than lagging, and hard to move without genuinely improving the
business. Revenue is a lagging indicator; activation and retention predict it.

They should change as the company's stage changes. The metrics that matter while searching for
fit are not the ones that matter while scaling, and carrying the old set forward is a common
source of misdirected effort.

## Where it goes wrong

- Tracking so many metrics that none of them drives a decision.
- Choosing vanity metrics that only go up.
- Keeping the same KPIs across stages with very different questions.
- Reporting KPIs without the context of what would count as good.
