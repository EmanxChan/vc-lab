## Why it matters

Licensing is the highest-margin model available — the marginal cost of another licence is near
zero — and the least defensible position to occupy. Your licensees have every incentive to build
it themselves once volume justifies it, and they can see exactly what it's worth to them.

For AI companies this is the live question. Licensing a model or a capability into someone
else's distribution is fast revenue and a weak long-term position, because the layer you're
licensing is exactly the layer everyone is trying to commoditise.

## What it looks like in practice

It works best where the IP is genuinely hard to reproduce, protected, or improving faster than a
licensee could catch up. It works badly where the licensee's engineering team could rebuild it
in two quarters.

Deal structure matters as much as price: exclusivity limits your market, minimum commitments
protect you from a partner who signs and then deprioritises, and field-of-use restrictions keep
one licensee from foreclosing an entire industry.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting broad exclusivity early for a headline number.
- Licensing to a partner who is simultaneously building the same capability internally.
- Having no minimum commitments, so a signed deal produces no revenue.
- Assuming high margins mean low risk. Concentration is the risk.
