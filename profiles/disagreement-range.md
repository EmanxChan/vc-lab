## Why it matters

The disagreement range is the band of exit values where different shareholders want different
outcomes. Below the conversion point preferred prefers the preference; common wants a higher
price. Within that band the two groups are genuinely opposed.

This is where acquisitions get difficult, and it explains behaviour that otherwise looks
irrational — an investor blocking a deal founders want, or founders resisting a deal that pays
investors back.

## What it looks like in practice

The range is widest when the preference stack is large relative to the likely exit value, which
is the situation created by raising a lot at high valuations and then exiting modestly.

Understanding where it sits before entering an acquisition process tells you which conversations
will be hard and who needs to be brought along. It's also an argument for not stacking
preferences carelessly in the first place.

## Where it goes wrong

- Entering an acquisition process without modelling where interests diverge.
- Assuming everyone wants the highest price. Below the conversion point, not everyone does.
- Treating investor resistance as bad faith when it's the structure working as designed.
- Building a preference stack that makes the disagreement range cover the likely outcomes.
