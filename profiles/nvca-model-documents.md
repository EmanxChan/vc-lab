## Why it matters

Standardisation is the reason a priced round can close in weeks rather than months. When both
sides start from the same base, negotiation narrows to the handful of terms that actually differ
— price, board, preference — instead of re-litigating boilerplate.

Deviation from the model docs is itself a signal. It isn't automatically bad, but every
departure should have a reason someone can state out loud.

## What it looks like in practice

The set covers the term sheet, stock purchase agreement, certificate of incorporation, investor
rights agreement, voting agreement, and right of first refusal and co-sale agreement. They're
updated periodically and are free to download.

At pre-seed most rounds happen on SAFEs and never touch these. They become relevant at the first
priced round, which is why founders often encounter them for the first time under time pressure.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming model docs means no negotiation. The economics still have to be agreed.
- Accepting non-standard terms without asking why they were changed.
- Paying for bespoke drafting on a standard early round.
- Using an outdated version, which quietly reintroduces terms the market has moved past.
