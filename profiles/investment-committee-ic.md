## Why it matters

The IC is where the decision gets made and where individual enthusiasm meets collective
scrutiny. Its function is to catch the errors a single person committed to a deal won't see.

It works when it genuinely can say no. An IC that ratifies whatever the sponsoring partner
brings adds process without adding judgment.

## What it looks like in practice

Structures vary from consensus to unanimity to a sponsoring-partner model where others advise.
Each produces different behaviour: unanimity selects for uncontroversial deals, which in a
power-law business is a real cost.

For a solo GP the IC is a discipline rather than a body — a written memo, a mandatory case
against, and often an informal advisor who will push back honestly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Requiring unanimity, which systematically filters out contrarian bets.
- Running an IC that has never actually declined a deal.
- Bringing deals to IC without the memo circulated in advance.
- As a solo GP, skipping the discipline because there's no one to present to.
