## Why it matters

Reserves are the capital set aside for follow-ons. How much to reserve is one of the two or
three decisions that most determine a fund's outcome, and it's made before you know which
companies will need it.

Too little and you're diluted out of the winner. Too much and you've made fewer initial bets,
reducing the chance of catching an outlier at all.

## What it looks like in practice

Common ratios run from 1:1 to 1:2 between initial and reserve capital, varying with stage.
Earlier-stage funds typically reserve more because there are more rounds ahead.

The allocation discipline matters as much as the amount. Reserves deployed to keep struggling
companies alive are reserves not available for the one that works, and that trade is usually
made implicitly rather than deliberately.

## Where it goes wrong

- Setting a reserve ratio without modelling the dilution path to exit.
- Spreading reserves evenly rather than concentrating them.
- Deploying reserves into companies that need money rather than ones that deserve it.
- Reserving so heavily that the initial portfolio is too small to catch an outlier.
