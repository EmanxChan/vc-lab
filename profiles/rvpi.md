## Why it matters

RVPI is residual value to paid-in — what's still held, at current marks, divided by contributed
capital. It's the unrealised half of TVPI, and the half that can change without anything
happening.

It's the number most sensitive to valuation policy. A portfolio carried at stale round prices
from a hotter market has an RVPI that overstates what the positions would fetch.

## What it looks like in practice

RVPI declines over a fund's life as positions exit and value converts into DPI. A mature fund
with a high RVPI and a low DPI has positions it hasn't been able to realise.

The quality question is how the marks were set. Last-round pricing is standard, but a round
priced three years ago in different conditions is a weak basis for a current valuation.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reading RVPI as value that will be realised. Much of it won't be.
- Not checking how old the marks underlying it are.
- Comparing RVPI across managers with different valuation policies.
- Expecting RVPI to stay flat. It should convert to DPI or be written down.
