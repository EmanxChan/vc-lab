## Why it matters

The lock-up prevents insiders from selling for a set window after an IPO — typically 90 to 180
days. It exists so the market isn't flooded with insider selling immediately after listing.

For a fund it means an IPO is not liquidity. The position remains at market risk through the
lock-up, and the price at expiry may bear little relation to the offer price.

## What it looks like in practice

Expiry is a known, dated event, which means the market prices in the expected supply ahead of
it. Prices frequently soften into a lock-up expiry for exactly that reason.

Staggered releases and early-release provisions tied to price thresholds have become more
common, which spreads the supply and reduces the cliff effect.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating the IPO date as the realisation date.
- Marking the position at the offer price through the lock-up.
- Not planning the disposal strategy before expiry, when everyone else is selling.
- Assuming the lock-up terms are standard. Early-release provisions vary.
