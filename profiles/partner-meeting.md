## Why it matters

The dynamic that founders miss is that their champion is the one being evaluated as much as the
company. The sponsoring partner is spending internal credibility, and the questions in the room
are partly about whether they've done the work.

It explains a lot of otherwise confusing behaviour — why a partner asks for a specific extra
data point, why timelines slip a week, why enthusiastic meetings produce no term sheet. The
champion needs ammunition for a room the founder isn't in.

## What it looks like in practice

The useful preparation is to arm the champion: a clean one-pager, the three hardest objections
answered, and the specific numbers that would come up. Ask them directly what the pushback will
be — most will tell you.

Format varies by firm. Some vote, some require unanimity, some leave it to the sponsoring
partner with the others as advisors. Knowing which applies changes how you prepare.

## Where it goes wrong

- Pitching the champion and never asking what the partnership will object to.
- Assuming enthusiasm from one partner means the round is done.
- Bringing new material to the partner meeting that the champion hasn't seen.
- Reading a slow process as disinterest when it's usually an internal calendar.
