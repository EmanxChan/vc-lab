## Why it matters

The LOI is where leverage peaks and then starts to decline. Once signed with exclusivity, the
company can't shop the deal, and every diligence finding becomes a reason to renegotiate against
a seller with no alternative.

Price in an LOI is an opening position, not a commitment. Retrading after diligence is common
enough that experienced sellers negotiate the terms that constrain it before signing rather than
after.

## What it looks like in practice

The binding parts are usually exclusivity, confidentiality, and who pays costs. Everything about
price and structure is subject to diligence and definitive documents.

Shorter exclusivity is better for the seller, and specifying what can trigger a price adjustment
narrows the room for a retrade. Both are much easier to negotiate before signing than after.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting long exclusivity, which removes the only leverage the seller has.
- Treating the stated price as agreed.
- Signing before cleaning up diligence problems that will surface anyway.
- Letting the process run without a fallback plan if the deal dies.
