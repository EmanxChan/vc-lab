## Why it matters

Early sales is research wearing a sales hat. Nobody else can hear an objection and rewrite the
roadmap in the same week, and nobody else has the authority to make the promises that close a
first customer.

It's also the thing that has to end. A company that can't sell without the founder in the room
hasn't found a repeatable motion — it has found a charismatic one, and that's not fundable at
Series A.

## What it looks like in practice

The output of founder-led sales isn't just revenue, it's a documented pattern: who buys, what
objections come up, what makes deals stall, what the sequence is. That documentation is what
makes the first sales hire succeed rather than flounder.

The transition point is usually when the founder can predict the outcome of a call before making
it. That predictability is the motion; before it exists, hiring a salesperson just adds someone
with nothing to follow.

## Where it goes wrong

- Hiring a VP of Sales before a repeatable motion exists, and blaming them when it doesn't work.
- Doing founder-led sales without writing anything down, so the knowledge stays in one head.
- Closing deals with bespoke promises that make the product incoherent.
- Holding on too long, so sales capacity is capped by the founder's calendar.
