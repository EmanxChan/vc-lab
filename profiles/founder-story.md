## Why it matters

At pre-seed there is very little else to evaluate. The story is the evidence that the founder
understands the problem at a level a smart outsider couldn't reach in six months of research,
which is what makes them likely to make good decisions when the plan changes.

It also predicts persistence. Founders with a genuine connection to the problem stay through the
years where nothing works, and pre-seed companies spend a lot of time in that state.

## What it looks like in practice

The credible version is specific and slightly unflattering — the thing that went wrong, what it
cost, what was tried first. Polished origin stories read as constructed, because they usually
are.

It should also connect forward. The history explains the insight, the insight explains the
product, and the product explains why this team gets there first. A story that doesn't reach the
product is biography.

## Where it goes wrong

- Manufacturing a personal connection that doesn't exist. It shows.
- Telling the story and never connecting it to why it produces an advantage.
- Leading with credentials rather than with the specific experience.
- Making it so central that the business case never gets made.
