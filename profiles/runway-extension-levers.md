## Why it matters

Every lever gets more expensive the later you pull it. A cost reduction at nine months of runway
is a decision; the same reduction at three months is a crisis, and everyone — investors,
employees, customers — can tell the difference.

For an investor, which lever a founder reaches for first is informative. Founders who cut early
and communicate clearly tend to survive; founders who wait for the raise to solve it tend to run
out of options.

## What it looks like in practice

The levers in rough order of speed: discretionary spend, hiring freeze, moving customers to
annual prepayment, collections discipline, headcount reduction, then financing. Revenue growth
is the best lever and the slowest, which is why it rarely solves a runway problem on its own.

The rule that holds up is to cut once and deeply rather than three times shallowly. Repeated
small reductions destroy morale far more than a single decisive one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Waiting until under three months of runway, when every option is worse.
- Cutting in small increments repeatedly, which is worse for the team than cutting once.
- Assuming a bridge is available. It depends on existing investors' conviction, not need.
- Cutting the things that generate revenue to preserve the things that don't.
