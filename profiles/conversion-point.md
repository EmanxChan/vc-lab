## Why it matters

The conversion point is the exit value at which an investor does better converting to common
than taking the preference. Below it, they take the preference; above it, they convert.

It's the number that tells you who is aligned with whom in an acquisition negotiation. Below the
conversion point, preferred holders are indifferent to price increases within a range, and
common holders are not.

## What it looks like in practice

The calculation compares the preference amount to the investor's ownership percentage of the
exit value. With a $10M preference on 25% ownership, conversion beats preference above a $40M
exit.

Different series convert at different points because they have different preferences and
different ownership. In a complex cap table there isn't one conversion point, there are several
— which is why exit modelling needs the full waterfall.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming a single conversion point for a cap table with multiple series.
- Ignoring participation, which changes the calculation entirely.
- Modelling conversion without accounting for anti-dilution adjustments to the ratio.
- Not knowing where the conversion point sits when negotiating an acquisition price.
