## Why it matters

QSBS is one of the largest tax benefits available to startup founders, employees, and early
investors, and it's driven by decisions made at formation — entity type, when stock was issued,
and how long it's held.

It also shapes behaviour at exit. A sale that would cross the holding threshold in two months is
worth two months of patience, and founders who don't know the rule sometimes sign away a great
deal of money without realising there was a choice.

## What it looks like in practice

The conditions are specific: the issuer must be a domestic C-Corp, the stock acquired at
original issuance rather than bought from someone else, the company under a gross asset ceiling
at issuance, and the business in a qualifying trade — which excludes several service industries.

The thresholds and holding periods have been amended more than once, most recently in 2025, and
they apply differently depending on when the stock was acquired. This is a check-with-a-tax-
advisor area rather than a rule of thumb.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming it applies automatically. Every condition has to be met.
- Selling shortly before the holding period is satisfied.
- Operating as an LLC, which makes the stock ineligible.
- Losing the documentation proving original issuance and the company's asset level at the time.
