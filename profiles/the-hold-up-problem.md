## Why it matters

The hold-up problem is what happens when a small holder has a veto and uses it for leverage.
Their block isn't about the merits — it's about being paid to remove the block.

It's the reason drag-along rights and single-class conversion votes exist. Both are mechanisms
for preventing a minority from extracting value at the moment everyone else needs a transaction
to close.

## What it looks like in practice

The structural defences are set years earlier: drag-along thresholds that don't require
unanimity, conversion votes across all preferred as a single class, and protective provisions
that aren't granted series by series.

Once a transaction is live the leverage sits with whoever can block it, and the options are to
pay them or to abandon the deal. That's why this is a drafting problem rather than a negotiating
one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting series-by-series vetoes, which multiply the number of parties who can hold up a deal.
- Setting drag-along thresholds near unanimity.
- Assuming small holders will act reasonably when they have leverage and a real economic
  interest.
- Discovering the veto structure during an acquisition rather than at the financing.
