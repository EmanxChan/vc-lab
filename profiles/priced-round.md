## Why it matters

A priced round is where the company actually gets valued, preferred stock gets issued, and all
the deferred questions from every SAFE get settled at once. It's also where governance arrives —
a board, protective provisions, and a set of rights that persist.

The reason it takes longer and costs more is that it's the real thing. Everything the SAFE
deferred has to be decided, documented, and agreed by everyone.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanics: a pre-money valuation is agreed, new preferred shares are issued, all outstanding
SAFEs and notes convert, and the option pool is usually topped up — typically out of the pre-
money, which means founders bear it.

The option pool shuffle is the part that surprises people. Agreeing a pre-money valuation and
then agreeing a 15% post-round pool means the effective price is lower than the headline,
because the pool comes out of the existing holders.

## Where it goes wrong

- Negotiating pre-money without settling the option pool, which changes the effective price.
- Not modelling how the SAFE stack converts before agreeing the round price.
- Underestimating the legal cost and time relative to a SAFE round.
- Treating the governance terms as boilerplate because the price was the hard part.
