## Why it matters

The funnel is the arithmetic of the process: how many companies seen produce how many
investments. The ratios are brutal and worth knowing, because they determine how much sourcing
is needed to deploy a fund.

It also localises problems. Poor conversion from screen to diligence is a filtering problem;
poor conversion from term sheet to close is a competitiveness problem.

## What it looks like in practice

Typical funnels are steep — hundreds of companies seen for a handful of investments. The exact
ratios vary enormously by stage and strategy, which is why your own numbers matter more than
benchmarks.

Working backwards is the useful exercise: to make eight investments a year at your observed
conversion rates, how many companies do you need to see, and is that achievable with current
sourcing?

## Where it goes wrong

- Not measuring the funnel, so sourcing needs are guessed at.
- Comparing your ratios to another firm's without adjusting for stage and strategy.
- Reading a steep funnel as a problem. It's normal.
- Optimising conversion by lowering standards rather than by improving sourcing.
