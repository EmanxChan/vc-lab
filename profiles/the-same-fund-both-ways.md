## Why it matters

Running one set of portfolio outcomes through both waterfall structures is the fastest way to
see that the total carry can be identical while the timing — and therefore the risk — is
completely different.

The comparison also shows where the clawback risk comes from, and why LPs care about the
structure even when the headline carry percentage is the same.

## What it looks like in practice

The pattern that emerges: with an early winner and later losses, the American waterfall pays
carry that the European one would never have paid. Whether that money comes back depends
entirely on whether the clawback works.

Where all the outcomes are positive, the two structures converge on the same total and differ
only in when the GP receives it — which still has real value given the time involved.

## Where it goes wrong

- Concluding the structures are equivalent because total carry can match. Timing and risk
  differ.
- Modelling only the good scenario, where the difference is smallest.
- Ignoring the float — the value to the GP of receiving money years earlier.
- Assuming clawback fully neutralises the difference. In practice recovery is imperfect.
