## Why it matters

The ownership target is what connects a fund's strategy to its arithmetic. Under the power law
one company produces most of the return, so the question is whether the position in that company
is large enough to return the fund.

It's the number that should drive cheque size, round selection, and reserve policy — and it's
the one most often set by what was available rather than by what the model requires.

## What it looks like in practice

The calculation runs backwards: what exit value is realistic, what ownership at exit would
return the fund, and therefore what ownership at entry survives the expected dilution. That last
step is where most targets are set too low.

A small fund can hit a meaningful target because the fund is small. A large fund needs either a
large position or a much larger outcome, which is why fund size constrains strategy so tightly.

## Where it goes wrong

- Setting the target at entry without modelling dilution through to exit.
- Taking whatever allocation is offered rather than what the model needs.
- Ignoring that reserves are what defend the target.
- Applying the same target across stages with very different dilution paths.
