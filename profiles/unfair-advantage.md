## Why it matters

At pre-seed there's no product traction to evaluate, so the unfair advantage is a large share of
what the investment decision actually rests on. It's the answer to "why this team," and it's the
reason to believe they'll get to the moat before someone else does.

Most claimed advantages fail the test of being unfair. Working hard, caring more, and moving
fast are available to everyone.

## What it looks like in practice

The durable kinds are earned rather than chosen: domain expertise from years inside the problem,
distribution access others don't have, a dataset that can't be reassembled, or a founding team
that has already worked together through something hard.

The useful diligence question is simply: what would it take for a competent, well-funded
competitor to get this? If the answer is money and six months, it isn't unfair.

## Where it goes wrong

- Listing effort, passion, or speed. Everyone has access to those.
- Claiming proprietary data that's actually publicly available or purchasable.
- Naming an advisor relationship as if it were a distribution channel.
- Having a genuine advantage and building something that doesn't use it.
