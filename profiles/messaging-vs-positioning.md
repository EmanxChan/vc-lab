## Why it matters

Teams try to fix a positioning problem with better copy, and it never works. If a product is
positioned against the wrong alternative, no headline rescues it — the buyer is still comparing
you to something you lose against.

The diagnostic value is in the order: get positioning right first, then messaging is mostly
execution. Do it backwards and you rewrite the website every quarter without improving
conversion.

## What it looks like in practice

One positioning statement should generate many pieces of messaging — a homepage headline, an
outbound email, a conference talk, the first slide of a deck — all pointing at the same claim in
different registers.

If the messages contradict each other, that's usually a positioning problem showing through
rather than a copywriting one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Rewriting the website when the actual problem is who you're selling to.
- Having positioning that only lives in one person's head, so messaging drifts.
- Testing headlines without a stable position underneath them.
- Treating positioning as a marketing deliverable rather than a company decision.
