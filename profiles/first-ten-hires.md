## Why it matters

At ten people every hire is 10% of the company, and the compounding is cultural as well as
operational — early employees set the hiring bar for everyone after them. A weak fifth hire
produces a weak fifteenth.

For an investor this is where a lot of post-investment risk lives. A founder who can attract
people better than themselves into a risky, underpaid role is demonstrating something that no
metric captures.

## What it looks like in practice

The pattern that works is generalists early, specialists as the shape of the work becomes clear.
The first hires need to be comfortable with undefined roles, because the roles genuinely are
undefined and will change twice in the first year.

Compensation is the visible constraint and rarely the binding one. Early employees join for the
problem, the people, and the ownership. The equity has to be meaningful enough to matter, which
at ten people means whole percentage points for the first few.

## Where it goes wrong

- Hiring specialists before knowing what the job actually is.
- Lowering the bar to fill a role quickly, which resets the bar permanently.
- Under-granting equity to the first hires, then trying to correct it two years later.
- Hiring people who need structure into a company that doesn't have any yet.
