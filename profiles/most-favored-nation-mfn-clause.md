term: Most Favored Nation (MFN) clause

## Why it matters

MFN solves the pricing problem for an investor going first when nobody knows what the company is
worth. Rather than guess, they take whatever the market later decides — which is why it's common
in the earliest, least-priced money.

The cost to the company is a loss of flexibility. Every subsequent concession propagates
backwards, which makes it hard to give a strategically important investor a special deal without
giving it to everyone.

## What it looks like in practice

It's most common on uncapped SAFEs and in side letters. The mechanics matter: what counts as a
better term, whether it applies only to instruments of the same type, and whether it survives
into the priced round or expires at conversion.

Stacked MFNs across many small investors are the failure mode. One concession to one party can
cascade through a dozen agreements at once.

## Where it goes wrong

- Granting MFN broadly, so no later investor can be given a bespoke term.
- Leaving the definition of "better terms" vague, which produces disputes at conversion.
- Not tracking who holds MFN, then discovering the cascade during a round.
- Assuming it expires at the priced round when the drafting says otherwise.
