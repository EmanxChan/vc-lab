## Why it matters

Deal flow is the top of the funnel, and it constrains everything below it. You cannot invest in
a company you never saw, which makes sourcing the one part of the job that has no substitute.

Quality matters more than volume past a point. A thousand inbound decks that don't match the
thesis is worse than fifty that do, because screening has a real cost in attention.

## What it looks like in practice

Sources divide into inbound (reputation, content, referrals) and outbound (research, cold
outreach, communities). The best flow tends to come from being known for something specific
rather than from being generally visible.

For a new manager the honest constraint is that dealflow is downstream of reputation, which
takes years. Operating experience, a public point of view, and genuine helpfulness are the
levers available before a track record exists.

## Where it goes wrong

- Optimising for volume when the constraint is relevance.
- Relying entirely on warm intros, which reproduces the existing network's biases.
- Not tracking where deals came from, so you can't tell which sources work.
- Assuming good dealflow arrives automatically once a fund is announced.
