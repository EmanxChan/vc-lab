## Why it matters

Founders collapse these two and then wonder why a deal that should have closed stalled. Segment
tells you where to aim the go-to-market. Persona tells you what the message has to say. Get the
segment right and the persona wrong and you generate meetings that never convert.

For an investor it's a diligence shortcut: a founder who can name both, and describe how the
message changes between them, has run a real sales process. One who only has a segment has run a
market-sizing exercise.

## What it looks like in practice

In B2B the split shows up as an org chart. The segment defines the account; inside it there is
someone with the problem, someone with the budget, and someone who has to change how they work.
A pitch aimed at the budget-holder talks about cost and risk. The same product pitched to the
daily user talks about the twenty minutes they get back.

In consumer the segment is often demographic and the persona is behavioural, and the behavioural
one matters more — two people the same age with the same income can have completely different
reasons to buy.

## Where it goes wrong

- Building elaborate personas with stock photos and names before talking to anyone real.
- Targeting a segment but writing all the copy for one persona inside it.
- Assuming the user and the buyer are the same person. In B2B they usually aren't.
- Treating persona work as a marketing exercise rather than a sales one.
