## Why it matters

Stacked preferences are the cumulative amount owed to all preferred before common receives
anything, and they grow with every round. A company that has raised $60M across four rounds has
a $60M hurdle before founders and employees see a dollar.

This is the arithmetic that makes a $50M exit on a company that raised $60M pay nothing to
common, regardless of what the cap table says anyone owns.

## What it looks like in practice

The stack grows with each round and can grow faster if any round carries a multiple. Two rounds
at 1x plus one at 2x on a $30M raise produces a stack well above the cash raised.

The number worth tracking continuously is the exit value at which common starts to receive
proceeds. It moves up with every financing, and it's the honest measure of whether employee
equity is worth anything.

## Where it goes wrong

- Tracking the amount raised rather than the preference stack, which can be larger.
- Ignoring accrued dividends, which add to the stack over time.
- Not recalculating the break-even exit value after each round.
- Granting employee options without knowing whether the stack makes them worthless.
