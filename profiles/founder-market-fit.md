## Why it matters

Founder-market fit is whether this particular team has an unusual advantage in this particular
market — domain knowledge, distribution access, or experience that can't be acquired quickly.

At pre-seed it carries most of the weight, because there's little else to evaluate. It's the
reason to believe this team will reach the insight before a better-funded competitor.

## What it looks like in practice

The evidence is specific rather than general: years inside the problem, a network of the right
people, a prior attempt that failed instructively, or access to data or customers others can't
reach.

The counterexample is worth holding: plenty of outsiders have built great companies precisely
because they didn't know what was supposed to be impossible. Fit is a strong prior, not a rule.

## Where it goes wrong

- Accepting general enthusiasm for a market as founder-market fit.
- Treating credentials as equivalent to operating experience in the problem.
- Applying it so strictly that outsider founders are systematically filtered out.
- Assuming fit in an adjacent market transfers. It often doesn't.
