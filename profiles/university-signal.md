## Why it matters

Founders from a handful of universities appear disproportionately in successful companies, and
the research quantifies it. The question is what the signal actually measures.

Most of it is selection and network rather than education. Selective institutions admit people
who were already likely to do well, and then connect them to each other and to capital.

## What it looks like in practice

Used as a sourcing input it's reasonable — these networks are dense and worth being part of.
Used as a filter it's a way to systematically miss founders whose advantage came from somewhere
else.

The stronger version of the same information is what the person did, not where they were. A
specific project, a shipped product, a research result — all of those carry more information
than the institution.

## Where it goes wrong

- Using it as a screening criterion rather than as one weak input.
- Confusing the network effect with the education.
- Ignoring base rates — most founders from any university don't succeed.
- Letting it substitute for evaluating what the founder has actually built.
