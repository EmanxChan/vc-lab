## Why it matters

A defined process is what stops investing from being a series of ad hoc reactions. It makes
decisions comparable, makes the reasons reviewable, and makes it possible to learn from what you
passed on.

It also protects against the failure mode of enthusiasm: without a process, the company you
happened to meet on a good day gets more attention than the better one you met when busy.

## What it looks like in practice

The sequence is broadly sourcing, first-level screen, second-level review, diligence, memo,
decision. Each stage should have an explicit reason to advance or stop, written down.

The written reason is the part that compounds. A pipeline with a recorded rationale for every
pass becomes a dataset about your own judgment, which is the only way to find out whether it's
any good.

## Where it goes wrong

- Running the process only for deals you're excited about.
- Not recording pass reasons, which makes the anti-portfolio useless as a learning tool.
- Letting stages blur, so diligence starts before a decision to diligence was made.
- Making the process so heavy it can't move at the speed a competitive round requires.
