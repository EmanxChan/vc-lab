## Why it matters

Freemium replaces a sales force with a product, which is why it produces such good margins when
it works. It also means the free tier is a permanent cost centre, and it only pays for itself if
free users either convert or bring in people who do.

For AI-native products the arithmetic is harsher than it was for classic SaaS: free users
consume inference, so every free account has real marginal cost. Freemium models designed in a
zero-marginal-cost world don't transfer cleanly.

## What it looks like in practice

It works when the free tier delivers real value but hits a natural, honest limit tied to success
— more usage, more collaborators, more data. It fails when the limit feels artificial, because
then the free tier teaches people to resent you.

Conversion rates are lower than founders expect. Low single digits is normal. The model only
works at volume, which means it needs a product people find without being sold to.

## Where it goes wrong

- Making the free tier so generous nobody needs to upgrade.
- Making it so thin nobody sticks around long enough to want to.
- Ignoring the serving cost of free users, especially where inference is involved.
- Bolting freemium onto a product that requires onboarding help to be useful at all.
