## Why it matters

Net burn is the number that determines survival, but gross burn is the number that determines
exposure. A company with high gross burn and high revenue looks healthy until revenue moves, and
then the cost base is revealed at full size.

The distinction matters most for businesses with concentrated or volatile revenue. Losing one
large customer converts a comfortable net burn into an emergency without any change in spending.

## What it looks like in practice

Track both, and understand runway under a scenario where revenue falls. The gap between the two
is a measure of how dependent survival is on revenue continuing exactly as forecast.

Non-recurring items distort both. A one-off legal bill or an annual prepayment can make a month
look alarming or reassuring, which is why a three-month average is the usual basis for runway.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting only net burn, hiding the size of the fixed cost base.
- Calculating runway from a single unusually good month.
- Ignoring what happens to runway if the largest customer leaves.
- Treating collections as revenue — cash timing and recognition are different things.
