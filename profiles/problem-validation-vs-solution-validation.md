## Why it matters

Doing these in the wrong order is the most expensive common mistake in early-stage building.
Problem validation is cheap — conversations, a landing page, a manual pilot. Solution validation
costs engineering months. Teams routinely skip the cheap one and pay for the expensive one.

It also explains a specific failure mode investors see often: a technically excellent product
with real users and no revenue. The solution works. The problem was never worth money.

## What it looks like in practice

Problem validation looks like evidence that the pain already costs something — people paying for
a bad alternative, hiring around it, or building internal tooling. Solution validation looks
like usage: they tried yours, they kept using it, they stopped using the old thing.

The strongest early signal collapses both: someone pays before the product is finished. That's a
validated problem and a credible solution in one transaction.

## Where it goes wrong

- Building first and validating the problem afterwards, when the sunk cost makes honesty hard.
- Reading "this is a real problem" as "people will pay to solve it." Those are different claims.
- Validating the problem with one segment and the solution with another.
- Treating a working product as proof the problem mattered.
