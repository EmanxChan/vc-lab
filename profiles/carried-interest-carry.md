## Why it matters

Carry is the GP's share of profits and the entire economic reason to run a fund rather than take
a salary. It's also where the alignment is: carry only pays if LPs make money first.

The size of the number is often misunderstood. Twenty percent of the profits on a fund that does
well is a lot of money — but it arrives years after the work, only if the fund performs, and
it's shared across the partnership.

## What it looks like in practice

Carry is calculated through the waterfall, which determines when and how much is paid. The
details — hurdle, catch-up, European versus American — change the timing and sometimes the total
substantially.

Tax treatment is the perennial policy fight. Section 1061 requires a three-year holding period
for carry to receive long-term capital gains treatment, which for venture is usually satisfied
comfortably.

## Where it goes wrong

- Modelling carry without the waterfall. The structure determines when it's actually received.
- Assuming carry is received as profits accrue. Under a European waterfall it comes at the end.
- Forgetting carry is subject to clawback if later losses reverse earlier gains.
- Ignoring vesting, which determines what a departing partner keeps.
