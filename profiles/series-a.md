## Why it matters

Series A is where the question changes from whether it works to whether it scales, and the
evidence required changes with it. Investors are underwriting a repeatable motion — acquisition
that works without the founder, retention that holds, and unit economics that survive being
multiplied.

It is also where governance arrives properly: a priced round, a real board, protective
provisions, and a set of rights that will persist for the life of the company.

## What it looks like in practice

The bar is usually expressed in revenue but is really about consistency. Growth that comes from
one channel, repeatably, with retention behind it, beats a larger number assembled from
unrelated deals.

The gap between seed and Series A is where most companies die, and the cause is usually a
mismatch between what the seed round funded and what the A round expects. That gap has widened
as A-round bars rose.

## Where it goes wrong

- Optimising for the revenue number and ignoring retention, which is what actually gets
  diligenced.
- Raising on a single large customer that makes the growth look repeatable when it isn't.
- Assuming the Series A bar is what it was when the seed was raised.
- Treating the board that arrives with the round as a formality.
