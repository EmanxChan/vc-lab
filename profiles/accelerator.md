## Why it matters

Accelerators function as a filter and a signal. The selective ones have seen enough companies
that acceptance carries information, which is why the strongest programmes have such
disproportionate outcomes — the filter is doing much of the work.

For an investor they are a dealflow channel with known economics and known timing. The cost is
that everyone else sees the same companies at the same demo day, on the same terms, which
compresses the advantage of being there.

## What it looks like in practice

The terms are usually a small cheque for a fixed percentage, plus mentorship and a demo day. The
value varies enormously by programme: at the top the network and signal are worth far more than
the money; further down the equity is expensive for what arrives.

The useful question about any programme is what its alumni do next — whether they raise, from
whom, and at what rate. That number says more than the curriculum does.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating acceptance into any accelerator as equivalent signal. The distribution is extremely
  wide.
- Sourcing only at demo day, where the price is set and competition is highest.
- Ignoring how much equity a programme takes relative to what it provides.
- Assuming the mentorship is the value. Usually it's the filter and the network.
