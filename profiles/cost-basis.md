## Why it matters

Cost basis is what was actually paid for a position, and it's the denominator for MOIC and the
reference point for gains and losses. Simple in principle, and easy to get wrong when follow-ons
are involved.

A position built across three rounds at different prices has one blended cost basis, and that
blend determines whether the position is profitable — not the price of any single cheque.

## What it looks like in practice

Every follow-on increases cost basis, which means defending a position that ultimately fails
increases the loss. That's the real cost of reserves deployed badly.

Conversely, follow-ons into a winner at a higher price dilute the multiple even as they increase
absolute return. A 20x on the seed cheque becomes a 6x blended if enough capital went in at
Series C prices.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reporting the multiple on the initial cheque rather than on total invested.
- Ignoring how follow-ons blend down a headline multiple.
- Forgetting that fees paid are part of what LPs contributed, even if not part of cost basis.
- Modelling reserves without accounting for their effect on the position's return profile.
