## Why it matters

Pilots are the most inflated number in early-stage fundraising, and the inflation is usually not
deliberate — founders genuinely feel like a pilot is traction. Sometimes it is. A paid pilot
with a named conversion trigger is real. An unpaid one with no end date is a long meeting.

The conversion rate from pilot to contract is the number that matters, and it's the one least
often volunteered.

## What it looks like in practice

The questions that resolve it: is money changing hands, who signed, what specifically has to be
true at the end for it to convert, and what date is that decision. A pilot with a defined
success metric and a budget owner attached converts. One without either drifts.

Pilot purgatory is the failure mode — a company with eight pilots running, all consuming founder
time, none converting, and a fundraise built on the count.

## Where it goes wrong

- Counting unpaid pilots as traction in a deck.
- Running pilots with no defined success criteria, which makes the conversion conversation
  subjective and losable.
- Letting pilots run past their end date because ending one feels like losing a logo.
- Optimising for pilot count instead of conversion rate.
