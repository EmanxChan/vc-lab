## Why it matters

Pre-seed is the stage where the underwriting is almost entirely about the team and the timing,
because there is no traction to analyse. It is also the stage where ownership is cheapest, which
is the entire argument for being there.

The stage has become a real institutional category rather than a gap. Seed rounds grew large
enough that a distinct earlier round was needed, and pre-seed filled it — which means the
vocabulary of a seed round from a decade ago now describes a pre-seed one.

## What it looks like in practice

A typical pre-seed funds twelve to eighteen months of building toward the evidence a seed round
wants: a working product, early users, and a defensible story about who the customer is. Almost
always on a SAFE rather than a priced round, because the legal cost of pricing a company this
early is not worth paying.

The judgment is about the founder's rate of learning, not their current state. What matters is
whether they have gone from nothing to something quickly, and whether their answers changed when
the evidence did.

## Where it goes wrong

- Raising a pre-seed large enough that the seed round has to clear an unreasonable bar.
- Expecting pre-seed metrics to predict anything. At this stage they mostly don't.
- Treating the milestone plan as fixed, when the point of the stage is to find out what's true.
- Underestimating how long the next raise takes, and running the plan to the last month.
