## Why it matters

Marketplaces have the best endgame and the worst beginning. Once liquidity exists, the network
defends itself and margins are structurally good. Before it exists, you're running two customer-
acquisition problems simultaneously and neither side has a reason to be there.

That chicken-and-egg period is where nearly all marketplaces die, and it's the part a pre-seed
investor is actually underwriting.

## What it looks like in practice

The solutions are all variations on faking one side until the other arrives — subsidising
supply, hand-recruiting it, being the supply yourself, or launching in a geography so narrow
that a small absolute number creates real density. Liquidity is local, not global; a marketplace
with a thousand sellers spread across a country has none.

The number that matters is match rate: of the people who show up wanting something, what share
get it. Everything else is vanity until that works in at least one place.

## Where it goes wrong

- Measuring total users on both sides instead of liquidity in a single market.
- Expanding geographically before density works anywhere.
- Ignoring disintermediation — once two sides meet, they have every reason to transact off-
  platform.
- Setting the take rate before knowing which side is harder to acquire.
