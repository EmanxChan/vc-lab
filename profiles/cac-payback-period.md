## Why it matters

Payback is the cash-flow view of unit economics, and it's the one that actually constrains
growth. A business with excellent lifetime value and a 30-month payback needs enormous capital
to grow, because every new customer consumes cash for two and a half years before returning any.

It's a better early metric than LTV/CAC because it depends on far fewer assumptions. You need
CAC and gross margin, both measurable now, rather than a retention curve you're guessing at.

## What it looks like in practice

Under 12 months is strong for B2B SaaS, 12–18 is workable, and beyond 24 the business is
capital-hungry regardless of how good the lifetime value looks. Consumer businesses generally
need much faster payback because retention is shorter and less certain.

Use gross profit rather than revenue. Paying back on revenue ignores the cost of serving the
customer and overstates the speed by whatever your margin isn't.

## Where it goes wrong

- Calculating on revenue instead of gross profit.
- Ignoring that payback lengthens as you move to more expensive channels.
- Accepting a long payback because LTV/CAC looks good — the constraint is cash, not ratio.
- Averaging across segments with very different payback profiles.
