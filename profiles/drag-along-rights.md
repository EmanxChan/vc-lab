## Why it matters

Drag-along solves the holdout problem. Without it, a small shareholder can block an acquisition
the overwhelming majority wants, purely for leverage. With it, once the required majority
approves, everyone is compelled to sell on the same terms.

For founders and small holders it means the decision to sell may not be theirs. The threshold
that triggers it is therefore one of the more important numbers in the voting agreement.

## What it looks like in practice

Typically triggered by approval from the board plus a majority of preferred and sometimes a
majority of common. Where those thresholds sit determines who effectively controls an exit.

The protections worth negotiating are that dragged holders get the same price and terms as
everyone else, and that they aren't forced into open-ended indemnity obligations beyond their
share of the proceeds.

## Where it goes wrong

- Not knowing what threshold triggers it until an acquisition is on the table.
- Agreeing to a drag that can be triggered by preferred alone.
- Accepting unlimited indemnity exposure as a dragged shareholder.
- Assuming founders control the sale decision because they hold a majority of common.
