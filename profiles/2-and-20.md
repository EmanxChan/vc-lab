## Why it matters

Two percent management fee and twenty percent carry is the industry default, and defaults are
powerful — most funds land near it because deviating requires justification on both sides.

It's less universal than it sounds. Large funds often charge less than 2% because the absolute
fee is enormous; top-performing managers sometimes charge 25–30% carry because they can.

## What it looks like in practice

The variations that matter: fee percentage and the base it's charged on, carry percentage,
whether there's a hurdle, and which waterfall applies. Two funds both described as "2 and 20"
can have materially different economics.

For emerging managers the fee is often the binding constraint. A first fund small enough to
raise generates a fee too small to operate on, which is why many first-time GPs are effectively
subsidising their own firm.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming two funds quoting the same headline terms have the same economics.
- Ignoring the fee base, which changes the absolute number substantially.
- Negotiating carry percentage while ignoring the hurdle and waterfall structure.
- Treating the standard as immutable when the market varies by fund size and track record.
