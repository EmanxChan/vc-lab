## Why it matters

It's the easiest sale in enterprise software because it removes the buyer's risk entirely, which
matters enormously when you're a two-year-old company asking a large organisation to trust you.
It also inverts the risk onto you.

This model is having a moment because of AI: when software can actually do the work rather than
help someone do it, charging for the work becomes possible, and it captures far more value than
a per-seat licence ever could.

## What it looks like in practice

It requires an outcome both sides can measure and attribute without arguing. Recovered dollars,
closed tickets, filled shifts — countable things with a clear baseline. Where attribution is
contestable, the model produces disputes instead of revenue.

Cash flow is the hidden problem: you carry the delivery cost and get paid after the result
lands, sometimes much later. That's a working-capital business wearing a software business's
clothes.

## Where it goes wrong

- Picking an outcome you can't measure cleanly, so every invoice becomes a negotiation.
- Failing to agree the baseline before starting — without it, you can't prove you caused
  anything.
- Ignoring the cash gap between doing the work and being paid for it.
- Capping the upside so tightly that a great result earns the same as a mediocre one.
