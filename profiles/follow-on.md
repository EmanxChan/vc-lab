## Why it matters

Follow-on investments are how a fund concentrates into what's working. Under the power law the
initial cheque is rarely enough — the position in the company that returns the fund has to be
large enough to matter at exit.

It's also where the hardest discipline lies, because the psychological pull is to support the
struggling company rather than to pay a higher price for the successful one.

## What it looks like in practice

The decision should be made as though it were a new investment at the current price, not as a
defence of the existing position. Sunk cost is the dominant bias here and it's strong.

The arithmetic to watch is blended cost basis. Following on heavily at later prices reduces the
multiple even as it increases absolute return, and whether that trade is right depends on the
fund's size and stage.

## Where it goes wrong

- Following on to protect a position rather than because the current price is attractive.
- Reserving equally across the portfolio rather than concentrating on the winners.
- Ignoring the effect on blended cost basis and reported multiple.
- Having pro rata rights and no capital reserved to exercise them.
