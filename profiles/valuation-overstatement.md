## Why it matters

Headline valuations systematically overstate what companies are worth, because the price
reflects the most recent share class with the best terms — not the whole company. Research on
this has found the gap to be substantial and widely distributed.

It matters for marks. A portfolio carried at post-money valuations is carrying a number that
assumes every share is worth what the newest preferred share is worth, which is structurally
untrue.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanism is the preference stack plus protective terms. A round with a 2x preference, a
ratchet, and seniority is worth more per share than common — so multiplying that price by total
shares overstates the enterprise.

The correction is to model the waterfall rather than the headline. That's more work and it's the
only way to know what a position is actually worth.

## Where it goes wrong

- Marking positions at post-money without examining the structure behind the price.
- Comparing valuations across companies with very different terms.
- Reading a valuation increase as a value increase when structure was the concession.
- Reporting portfolio value on headline marks without noting the assumption.
