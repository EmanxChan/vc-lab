## Why it matters

Seed is where a company stops asking whether anyone wants this and starts asking whether the
wanting can be turned into a business. The money buys the search for product-market fit, and the
round is sized to give that search enough time.

It is also the stage where the evidence bar becomes real. A pre-seed can be funded on a story; a
seed usually needs something that happened — users, revenue, retention, or a named set of design
partners.

## What it looks like in practice

Seed rounds are large enough now that they often behave like the Series A of a decade ago: a
real lead, real diligence, sometimes a priced round with a board seat. The structure varies more
than at pre-seed, which makes it worth being explicit about what is being bought.

The milestone that matters is whatever unlocks a Series A, and that is usually evidence of
repeatability — the same motion producing the same result more than once, without the founder
doing all of it personally.

## Where it goes wrong

- Raising a seed against a Series A bar that has moved since the plan was written.
- Spending the round scaling before fit exists, which is the classic way to die with money in
  the bank.
- Treating early revenue as fit without checking whether it retains.
- Sizing the round to eighteen months when the milestone realistically takes twenty-four.
