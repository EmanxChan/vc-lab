## Why it matters

Pro rata is what allows an early investor to maintain ownership as the company raises more.
Without it, a position that started meaningful is diluted through every subsequent round until
it doesn't move the fund's outcome.

Under the power law this is close to the whole game for a small fund. The one company that works
has to still be a meaningful position by the time it exits, and pro rata plus reserves is the
only way that happens.

## What it looks like in practice

The right is usually granted in the investor rights agreement, often limited to major investors
above a threshold. It's a right to participate, not an obligation, and exercising it requires
capital that has to have been reserved for the purpose.

In competitive later rounds pro rata gets squeezed — new investors want allocation, and existing
holders are sometimes asked to waive. That's the moment the right proves whether it was real.

## Where it goes wrong

- Holding pro rata rights with no reserves to exercise them.
- Accepting a threshold that excludes you from the right at the next round.
- Waiving pro rata in the company's best interests without modelling what it costs the fund.
- Assuming the right survives every round. Check whether it does.
