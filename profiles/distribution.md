## Why it matters

A distribution is the only moment an LP actually receives money. Everything before it — markups,
TVPI, paper gains — is an estimate. This is why DPI is the metric experienced LPs trust and TVPI
is the one they discount.

It's also the event that triggers the waterfall, and therefore the point at which the carry
arrangement stops being theoretical.

## What it looks like in practice

Distributions happen when a portfolio company exits or when the fund sells a position. The
proceeds run through the waterfall in the LPA, which determines how much goes to LPs and how
much to the GP as carry.

Timing is a GP decision within limits. Distributing quickly is good for LP IRR; holding creates
option value but extends the fund's life and delays the return.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reading a markup as equivalent to a distribution. One is cash; the other is an opinion.
- Distributing before the waterfall is properly calculated, creating clawback exposure.
- Holding a liquid position for optionality past the point where LPs need the return.
- Not modelling the tax consequences for LPs of distribution timing.
