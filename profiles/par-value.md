## Why it matters

Par value is a legal artifact, usually set at $0.0001, and it has almost nothing to do with what
stock is worth. Its only real function is establishing a nominal floor below which shares can't
be issued.

It matters in one practical place: founder stock issued at par at incorporation is what makes
the 83(b) election cheap. Eight million shares at $0.0001 is $800 of taxable value.

## What it looks like in practice

Delaware franchise tax can be calculated using the authorised shares method or the assumed par
value capital method, and the difference between them can be thousands of dollars a year. Most
startups use the second.

Beyond that, par value shows up in the charter and is otherwise ignored. It is not a valuation
of anything.

## Where it goes wrong

- Confusing par value with fair market value or the 409A price.
- Setting par value high, which raises the minimum issuance price for founder stock.
- Using the wrong Delaware franchise tax calculation method and overpaying substantially.
- Issuing founder stock below par, which isn't permitted.
