## Why it matters

A first fund is usually too small and too early to be judged on returns, because the returns are
not in yet. What it can be judged on is whether its investors want to do it again. Re-up rate is
the one metric about Fund I that is available while Fund II is being raised, which makes it the
real product of the first fund.

It also changes what LP relationship management is for. The cadence of updates, dinners and
annual meetings is not courtesy; it is the work that decides whether the next raise starts from
half-done or from zero.

## What it looks like in practice

The signals arrive long before the decision. Fast replies, prompt capital calls, attendance at
events and unprompted interest in the next vehicle all point one way. Slowing replies, dragging
capital calls, questions turning into scrutiny, and back-channelling for information rather than
asking point one the other.

Back-channelling is the sharpest of those, because it is not about performance at all. An LP who
goes around the official update has concluded the official update is incomplete.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating communication as a reporting obligation rather than as the retention mechanism.
- Going quiet in the quarters that are hardest to write about, which are the ones being graded.
- Surprising LPs with news they should have had early, exits included.
- Reading an LP's silence as contentment.
- Optimising a first fund entirely for paper marks, when the measurable outcome is who comes back.
