## Why it matters

Founders build competitive analysis around companies that look like them and miss where the
losses actually come from. For most early products the dominant competitor is inertia, and
inertia is free, already deployed, and has no sales cycle.

It changes the pitch, too. Competing against a startup means arguing features. Competing against
a spreadsheet means arguing that the problem is worth solving at all — a completely different
conversation.

## What it looks like in practice

The way to find the real competitive set is to ask what customers did before, and what the ones
who said no went on to do. That list is usually dominated by manual workarounds and internal
tools nobody counts.

The jobs-to-be-done framing helps here: define the competition by what the customer hires to
make progress, not by which companies share your category label.

## Where it goes wrong

- Building the competitive slide entirely from funded startups.
- Dismissing "do nothing" as a competitor when it's the most common outcome.
- Ignoring internal builds, which are the default substitute in large organisations.
- Positioning against a startup the buyer has never heard of.
