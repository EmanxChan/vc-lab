## Why it matters

Founders answer the business-model question with a revenue model and think they've answered it.
They haven't — and the missing half is exactly where the thing usually breaks. You can charge a
subscription and still lose money on every customer.

For an investor the distinction is a diligence tool. Anyone can say "marketplace, 15% take
rate." Fewer can explain what it costs to bring both sides, how long they stay, and at what
volume the margin turns positive.

## What it looks like in practice

The full answer connects four things: who pays, what they get, what delivering it costs, and
what acquiring them costs. If any of the four is missing the model isn't described yet — it's
just priced.

The same revenue model can be a great business or a terrible one depending on the other three.
Subscription with 85% gross margin and organic acquisition is a different company from
subscription with 30% margin and paid acquisition, even at identical revenue.

## Where it goes wrong

- Answering "what's the business model?" with a single word.
- Copying a revenue model from a company with a completely different cost structure.
- Leaving out cost of delivery, which is where AI-heavy products often get into trouble.
- Assuming the model that works at ten customers works at ten thousand.
