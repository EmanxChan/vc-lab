## Why it matters

Channels are the difference between a good product nobody finds and a good product with
distribution. Most early companies underinvest here because building is more comfortable than
partnering, and then discover that the incumbent's advantage was never the product.

Each channel also carries a hidden cost beyond the margin — partner enablement, co-selling,
support, and a customer relationship you no longer fully own.

## What it looks like in practice

Early on, one channel done properly beats four done partially. The right first channel is
wherever your specific buyer already goes to solve this class of problem — which might be a
marketplace, an industry association, a systems integrator, or nothing at all but direct
outreach.

Partner channels take longer to produce revenue than founders plan for. Six to twelve months
from signed agreement to first meaningful deal is normal, and a partner who signs but never
sells is the standard outcome without dedicated attention.

## Where it goes wrong

- Signing many partners and enabling none of them.
- Treating a partner agreement as distribution. It's permission to try.
- Ignoring channel conflict — direct sales undercutting the partner who sourced the lead.
- Building a channel motion before the direct motion works, so nobody knows what to teach the
  partner.
