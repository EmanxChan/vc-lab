## Why it matters

In the US, paying someone does not by itself transfer copyright in what they made. Absent a
written assignment, a contractor generally owns their work. For a company whose entire value is
its technology, that's not a paperwork problem — it's a question of whether the company owns the
thing it's selling.

It's one of the most common diligence findings at seed and Series A, and one of the most
expensive to fix late, because the leverage sits entirely with the person who didn't sign.

## What it looks like in practice

Everyone who touches the product signs before they start — founders, employees, contractors,
advisors, the designer who did the logo. For work predating incorporation, founders sign an
assignment covering it retroactively as part of formation.

The edge cases matter: work done on a previous employer's laptop or time can be claimed by that
employer, and university affiliations often carry their own IP terms. Both are worth asking
about directly in diligence.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming payment transfers ownership. It doesn't.
- Missing contractors and freelancers, who are the most common gap.
- Ignoring work done before incorporation or at a prior employer.
- Discovering the gap during diligence, when the fix costs whatever the holder decides.
