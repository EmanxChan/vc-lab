## Why it matters

Pre-money is the number that gets negotiated, and post-money is the number that determines
ownership. Confusing them is the most common arithmetic error in a fundraise, and it always
favours whoever understood it better.

The option pool makes it worse. A pool created out of the pre-money reduces the effective
valuation without changing the headline, which is why the same nominal price can mean materially
different ownership.

## What it looks like in practice

The arithmetic is simply pre-money plus the amount raised equals post-money, and the investor's
ownership is the amount raised divided by post-money. A $2M round on an $8M pre-money is 20% of
the company, not 25%.

The negotiation that actually matters is pre-money *and* pool together. Agreeing one without the
other leaves the most consequential number undecided.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing a pre-money valuation without agreeing the option pool at the same time.
- Quoting a valuation without saying which one it is.
- Comparing your round's pre-money to another company's post-money.
- Optimising for the headline number at the cost of the terms attached to it.
