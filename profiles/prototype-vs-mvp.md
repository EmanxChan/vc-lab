## Why it matters

The difference is whether anything is at stake for the user. Prototypes collect opinions; MVPs
collect behaviour. Opinions are systematically generous and behaviour isn't, which is why an
enthusiastic prototype review and a dead MVP are a common sequence.

For an investor it's a diligence distinction worth making explicit. "We have a working product"
can mean either, and the two are very different stages of evidence.

## What it looks like in practice

Prototypes are the right tool for narrow questions — is this flow comprehensible, does this
concept land, can we get alignment internally. They're fast, cheap, and honest about what they
are.

The mistake is stopping there. A prototype that tests well tells you the idea is communicable,
not that it's wanted. Only usage under real conditions, with real consequences, tells you that.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating positive prototype feedback as validation.
- Describing a prototype as a product in a deck or a data room.
- Building a polished prototype when a rough working version would have answered more.
- Skipping the prototype entirely and building the wrong thing beautifully.
