## Why it matters

Teams optimise what they measure, and most companies measure things that can be improved without
improving the product. Picking one number that can't be gamed is a coordination device more than
a measurement one.

The failure it prevents is a common one: marketing optimising signups, product optimising
engagement, sales optimising bookings, and the company as a whole getting worse while every
dashboard is green.

## What it looks like in practice

A good north star measures delivered value, moves before revenue does, and is influenceable by
the team. Revenue itself is usually a bad choice because it lags too far behind the work and can
be moved by pricing rather than product.

It's a focusing device, not the only number worth watching. Retention, burn, and margin still
need attention — the north star just decides what gets prioritised when they conflict.

## Where it goes wrong

- Choosing a metric that can be improved without the product getting better.
- Picking revenue, which lags and hides what caused it.
- Changing it every quarter, which defeats the purpose of having one.
- Treating it as the only metric, and letting margin or burn drift.
