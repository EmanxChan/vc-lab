## Why it matters

The catch-up is the tier that restores the GP to its full carry percentage after LPs have
received their preferred return. Without it, a hurdle would permanently reduce the GP's share of
total profits rather than just delaying it.

With a 100% catch-up, once the hurdle is met the GP receives all distributions until it has
received 20% of total profits — then everything splits 80/20 as intended.

## What it looks like in practice

The catch-up rate is negotiated. A 100% catch-up gets the GP to its full share quickly; a 50%
catch-up splits distributions during the catch-up tier and takes longer.

This combination — hurdle plus full catch-up — is what makes a soft hurdle soft. The GP ends up
with the same total percentage as if there were no hurdle, just later.

## Where it goes wrong

- Modelling a hurdle without the catch-up, which overstates the LP's share of total profits.
- Assuming the catch-up rate is always 100%.
- Not checking whether the catch-up applies to profits or to distributions.
- Treating hurdle and catch-up as separate negotiations. They only make sense together.
