## Why it matters

Escrow holds back a portion of carry distributions so there's money available if a clawback is
later triggered. It converts an unenforceable promise into a funded one.

It also reduces the float, which is precisely why GPs resist it and LPs want it. The percentage
held back is a direct negotiation over how much timing benefit the GP keeps.

## What it looks like in practice

Typical holdbacks run 20–30% of carry distributions, released as the fund's outcome becomes
clearer or at wind-down. The escrow is usually held by a third party rather than the GP.

It works alongside interim testing: the test determines whether there's a shortfall, and the
escrow is where the money to cover it actually sits.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing to a clawback with no escrow behind it.
- Releasing escrow early because the fund looks like it will perform.
- Holding escrow with the GP rather than a third party.
- Setting the holdback percentage too low to cover a realistic shortfall.
