## Why it matters

An interim clawback test calculates, at defined points during the fund's life, whether the GP
would owe money if the fund were wound up that day. It surfaces the problem early rather than at
the end.

That's valuable because clawback recovery gets harder over time. A shortfall identified in year
six can be addressed by withholding future carry; one identified in year eleven requires clawing
back money that's been spent.

## What it looks like in practice

Tests are typically run annually or at defined intervals, and a shortfall triggers either an
escrow top-up or suspension of further carry distributions.

It's one of the ILPA-recommended protections for deal-by-deal structures, and its presence is a
reasonable proxy for how LP-friendly the rest of the LPA is.

## Where it goes wrong

- Agreeing to deal-by-deal carry with no interim testing.
- Running the test only at wind-down, when it's too late to be useful.
- Ignoring a shortfall on the assumption later gains will cover it.
- Not tying the test to a concrete remedy, which makes it informational rather than protective.
