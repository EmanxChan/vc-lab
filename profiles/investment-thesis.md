## Why it matters

A thesis is what every other decision gets judged against. Without one, a portfolio is whatever
happened to be in front of you at the moment you had money, and there's no way to tell a good
pass from a lazy one.

It also does work that effort can't substitute for. Founders and other investors route deals to
people whose interests they can state in a sentence. A thesis you can't say out loud is a thesis
nobody can send you deals against.

## What it looks like in practice

Three variables, each answered with a decision rather than a preference: **sector** (what the
company does), **stage** (when you come in), **geography** (where it operates). A fourth —
vehicle — says how the check gets written: personal angel money, a syndicate, an SPV, a fund.

The test of specificity is whether someone else could apply it without you. "AI" is not a sector
answer. "AI rebuilding human trust — bridges, community bonds, belonging" is, because it names
what the company is *for* and therefore what it leaves out.

A thesis is written to be revised. The honest version carries its open question with it — the
thing that, if it turned out otherwise, would mean the thesis was wrong.

## Where it goes wrong

- Picking a sector broad enough that nothing is excluded, which makes the thesis decorative.
- Writing it from what's fashionable rather than from where you actually have access.
- Stating it once and never testing it against the companies you actually screen.
- Confusing the thesis with the pitch. The thesis is a filter you obey, not a story you tell.
