## Why it matters

Fundraising dynamics are almost entirely about parallelism. A term sheet with a deadline is only
leverage if other funds are far enough along to respond to it. Sequential raising gives you a
series of conversations with no competitive tension in any of them.

It also protects the company. An open-ended raise consumes founder attention indefinitely and
shows up in the metrics, which then makes the raise harder — the failure mode compounds.

## What it looks like in practice

The mechanics: build the full target list first, sort into tiers, take a few second-tier
meetings early to find the weak points in the pitch, then run the priority list in a tight
window. Track everything, because a process with thirty funds in it is not something to run from
memory.

Momentum is real and visible. Funds ask who else is looking, and the honest answer either helps
or hurts. That's an argument for starting when the story is strongest, not when the runway
demands it.

## Where it goes wrong

- Starting with the top choice, before the pitch has been tested.
- Sequential meetings, which remove any competitive dynamic.
- Beginning with under six months of runway, which is visible and prices into the terms.
- Not tracking the process, so follow-ups slip and interest goes cold.
