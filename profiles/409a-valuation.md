## Why it matters

Issuing options below fair market value creates a tax problem for the employee — immediate
taxation plus penalties under Section 409A — which turns a benefit into a liability. The
appraisal exists to establish a defensible price and create safe-harbour protection.

The gap between preferred price and common price is the part founders find surprising and is
entirely rational: common stock sits behind the whole preference stack and carries none of the
rights, so it's genuinely worth less.

## What it looks like in practice

409As are typically refreshed annually, or after any material event — a priced round, a large
acquisition offer, a significant change in performance. Using a stale one after a round has
closed forfeits the safe harbour.

There's a natural tension: a low 409A is good for employees because it lowers the strike price,
but it has to be defensible. Appraisers who deliver aggressively low numbers create audit risk
that lands years later.

## Where it goes wrong

- Issuing options against a 409A that predates the last financing.
- Assuming common stock is worth the preferred price, or vice versa.
- Choosing an appraiser for the answer rather than the rigour.
- Promising a strike price to a hire before the valuation is refreshed.
