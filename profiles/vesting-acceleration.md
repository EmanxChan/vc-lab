## Why it matters

Acceleration decides who gets paid what in an acquisition, and it's negotiated years before
anyone knows whether there will be one. Single trigger is founder-friendly and acquirer-hostile:
it means the team's equity fully vests on close, removing the retention the acquirer is partly
paying for.

That's why single trigger can reduce the price or kill a deal outright. Double trigger is the
market standard because it protects the individual against being acquired and discarded, without
removing the acquirer's retention.

## What it looks like in practice

Double trigger typically requires the acquisition plus termination without cause, or resignation
for good reason, within a defined window — commonly twelve months after close. "Good reason"
usually covers a material demotion, a pay cut, or being made to relocate.

Partial acceleration is a common compromise, especially for executives: some fraction vests on
the change of control, the rest on the second trigger.

## Where it goes wrong

- Pushing for single trigger and finding it reduces the acquisition price.
- Leaving "good reason" undefined, which makes the protection unenforceable in practice.
- Assuming acceleration is standard for employees. Usually it isn't beyond the senior team.
- Negotiating it at the acquisition rather than at grant, when there is no leverage.
