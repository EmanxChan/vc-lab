## Why it matters

The clawback requires the GP to return carry that turns out not to have been earned once the
fund's full outcome is known. It's the safety net under a deal-by-deal waterfall.

The problem is enforcement. By the time a clawback is triggered the money may have been
distributed to individual partners, spent, and taxed — and recovering it from people who have
left the firm is genuinely difficult.

## What it looks like in practice

The protections that make it work in practice are escrow accounts holding back a share of carry,
interim testing during the fund's life, and personal guarantees from individual partners.

Net-of-tax clawbacks limit the obligation to what the GP received after tax, which is reasonable
from the GP's side and reduces the LP's recovery.

## Where it goes wrong

- Relying on a clawback provision with no escrow or guarantee behind it.
- Assuming departed partners will return carry. Often the obligation is practically
  unenforceable.
- Not testing the clawback position during the fund's life, so it only surfaces at the end.
- Distributing carry to individuals without holding back for the potential obligation.
