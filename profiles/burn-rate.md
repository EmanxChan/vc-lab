## Why it matters

Burn is the rate at which a company consumes cash, and it's the number that sets the clock on
everything else. Every strategic decision at an early company is ultimately constrained by how
many months remain.

The distinction between gross and net burn matters more than founders usually treat it. Net burn
determines survival; gross burn determines how quickly things deteriorate if revenue moves.

## What it looks like in practice

The convention is a three-month trailing average, because single months are distorted by annual
payments, one-off legal costs, and timing of collections.

Burn multiple — net burn divided by net new ARR — is the efficiency version of the same idea and
is increasingly what growth investors look at. It answers how much cash is consumed per dollar
of new recurring revenue.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting a single unusually good month.
- Reporting net burn without disclosing the gross figure.
- Treating burn as a fixed number when it usually rises with headcount already committed.
- Not knowing burn precisely, which is a real signal about operational discipline.
