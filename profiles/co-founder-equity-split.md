## Why it matters

This is the decision most likely to destroy a company from the inside, and it gets made early,
fast, and emotionally. Resentment about the split compounds silently for years and surfaces at
the worst possible moment — usually during a fundraise or an acquisition.

For an investor it's a real diligence item. A split that doesn't reflect contribution, or a
large stake held by someone no longer involved, is a structural problem that gets harder to fix
with every round.

## What it looks like in practice

The split matters far less than the vesting attached to it. An imperfect split with four-year
vesting self-corrects, because whoever leaves early keeps proportionally little. A perfect split
with no vesting is a live grenade.

Equal splits are common and often right, particularly where founders are genuinely co-equal.
What makes them dangerous is using equality to avoid a difficult conversation about roles,
commitment, and what happens if someone wants out.

## Where it goes wrong

- Splitting equity without vesting, which makes every other mistake permanent.
- Weighting heavily toward whoever had the idea. Execution is where the value is created.
- Avoiding the conversation because it's uncomfortable, and letting resentment accumulate
  instead.
- Giving a large stake to someone part-time who never becomes full-time.
