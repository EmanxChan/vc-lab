## Why it matters

This is the single most consequential piece of small print in early-stage fundraising, and the
one founders most often get wrong. A post-money SAFE fixes the investor's percentage of the
company; a pre-money one doesn't.

The practical effect: with post-money SAFEs, every additional SAFE you sell dilutes you and not
the existing SAFE holders. Sell enough of them and the founder's stake at conversion is
materially smaller than the arithmetic they did at the time.

## What it looks like in practice

Y Combinator moved to post-money in 2018 specifically to make the investor's ownership certain
and calculable. That certainty is real and it comes directly out of the founder's share.

The discipline is to maintain a running conversion model — all instruments, all caps, against a
realistic priced round — and update it every time another SAFE is signed. The number to watch is
founder ownership post-conversion, not the cap on any individual note.

## Where it goes wrong

- Not knowing which version you're signing. They look almost identical.
- Assuming later SAFEs dilute earlier ones. Under post-money they don't.
- Modelling each SAFE in isolation rather than the whole stack at once.
- Discovering the combined effect at the priced round, when nothing can be changed.
