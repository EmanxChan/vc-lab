## Why it matters

The OIP is the price a series was originally sold at, and it's the anchor for almost everything
else — the liquidation preference is a multiple of it, and the conversion price starts equal to
it.

It's a fixed historical fact, which is what makes it useful as a reference point. The conversion
price moves with anti-dilution adjustments; the OIP doesn't.

## What it looks like in practice

A 1x preference means 1x the OIP per share. Anti-dilution adjusts the conversion price below the
OIP, which increases the number of common shares each preferred share converts into — but the
preference stays anchored to the original price.

That separation is the part people find confusing, and it's why the two numbers diverge after a
down round.

## Where it goes wrong

- Conflating OIP with the current conversion price after an anti-dilution adjustment.
- Calculating the preference off the adjusted conversion price rather than the OIP.
- Forgetting that each series has its own OIP.
- Assuming OIP equals the post-money divided by shares. It's the actual price paid per share.
