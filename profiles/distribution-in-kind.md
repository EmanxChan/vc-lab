## Why it matters

A distribution in kind gives LPs securities rather than cash — usually shares in a company that
has gone public. It transfers the decision about when to sell from the GP to the LP.

That can be helpful, since LPs have different tax positions and different views. It can also be
unwelcome, since some LPs can't hold public equities and are forced to sell immediately,
sometimes into a falling price.

## What it looks like in practice

Most common after an IPO, once a lock-up expires. The LPA usually specifies how the securities
are valued for the purpose of calculating carry, which matters because the price moves.

Coordinated distributions can move a stock if the position is large relative to trading volume,
which is a real consideration for both the GP's reported return and the LPs' realised one.

## Where it goes wrong

- Distributing in kind to LPs who are unable to hold the securities.
- Not specifying the valuation date, which determines the carry calculation.
- Ignoring the market impact of a large coordinated distribution.
- Treating the distribution date value as the return LPs actually achieved.
