## Why it matters

The LPAC is where LPs get a voice without taking management control — which they must not do,
because managing the fund would jeopardise their limited liability. It advises and consents on
specific matters rather than directing the GP.

Its most important function is conflicts. When a GP wants to do something the LPA doesn't
clearly permit — cross-fund investments, valuation methodology changes, related-party
transactions — the LPAC is who approves it.

## What it looks like in practice

Membership is typically the largest LPs plus a few chosen for perspective. It meets a few times
a year, reviews valuations, and handles consent requests.

For an emerging manager the LPAC is also an early-warning system. LPs who are uncomfortable
usually say so there first, and a GP who treats it as a formality misses the signal.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating the LPAC as a rubber stamp for conflicts.
- Populating it only with the friendliest LPs, which removes its usefulness.
- Letting it drift into management decisions, which risks LP limited liability.
- Bringing conflicts to it after the fact rather than before.
