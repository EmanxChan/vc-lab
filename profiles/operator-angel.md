## Why it matters

An operator-angel invests their own money and brings operating experience — someone who has
actually built the thing they're now funding. The value is credibility with founders and
genuinely useful judgment on execution.

It's also a real sourcing advantage. Founders talk to people who have done the job, which means
operator-angels see companies before institutional investors do.

## What it looks like in practice

The advantage is domain-specific and narrower than it feels. Experience scaling a marketplace
transfers well to another marketplace and poorly to enterprise software.

The failure mode is treating operating experience as a substitute for investing discipline.
Knowing how to run a company is not the same skill as knowing which companies to back, and the
two are often confused.

## Where it goes wrong

- Assuming operating success transfers to investing judgment.
- Advising outside your actual domain because founders defer to you.
- Investing in what you'd have built rather than what the market wants.
- Concentrating too heavily because domain confidence feels like conviction.
