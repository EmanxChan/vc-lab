## Why it matters

Internal explanations for losses are systematically wrong in one direction: price. It's the
least uncomfortable reason for the person who lost the deal, so it's the reason that gets
recorded. The actual causes are usually fixable and much less flattering.

Wins matter as much as losses and get analysed far less. Knowing precisely why you won tells you
what to repeat, and it's often not what the team thinks.

## What it looks like in practice

The signal comes from the buyer, and preferably not from the person who ran the deal — people
are polite to whoever sold to them. Short structured conversations a few weeks after the
decision, asking what the alternatives were, what mattered, and what almost changed their mind.

Patterns emerge fast. Ten interviews usually surface two or three recurring causes, and those
tend to be operational — response time, a missing integration, a reference they couldn't get —
rather than strategic.

## Where it goes wrong

- Recording the reason from the salesperson's notes.
- Only analysing losses, so you never learn what's working.
- Accepting "price" without asking what they compared it to and what would have justified it.
- Doing it once rather than continuously, so you learn about a market that has since moved.
