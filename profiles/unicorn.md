## Why it matters

A unicorn is a private company valued at $1B or more. The term mattered when it was rare and
means less now that there are many — including a substantial number whose valuations reflect
structure rather than value.

For a fund the relevant fact is that a unicorn valuation is a private mark, not a realisation.
Plenty of companies have crossed the threshold and later exited well below it.

## What it looks like in practice

Research on unicorn formation is consistent on one point: they take multiple rounds and many
years. The average US unicorn raises more than six rounds before reaching the threshold, which
is the practical case for reserves.

The valuation itself frequently overstates the economics. A high headline price supported by a
heavy liquidation preference or a ratchet is not equivalent to a company genuinely worth that
number.

## Where it goes wrong

- Reading a unicorn valuation as an achieved outcome.
- Ignoring the structure that supports a headline valuation.
- Assuming the path is fast. It usually takes many rounds over many years.
- Using unicorn count as a measure of fund performance rather than DPI.
