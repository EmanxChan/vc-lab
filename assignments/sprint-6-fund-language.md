## Part 2 — Same terms, with why it matters

1. **Thesis:** The specific slice of the market a fund invests in — sector, stage, and geography.
   *Why it matters:* It's the first question every LP asks, and a vague answer reads as an unformed fund.
2. **Pre-seed:** The first institutional money in, usually before meaningful revenue.
   *Why it matters:* It sets what diligence is even possible — at this stage you're underwriting the founder, not the numbers.
3. **SAFE:** A simple agreement that converts to equity at the next priced round.
   *Why it matters:* Most pre-seed checks go in on SAFEs, so if you can't discuss one fluently you can't discuss the deal.
4. **Post-money valuation cap:** The ceiling price at which a SAFE converts into equity.
   *Why it matters:* It's the number that actually determines your ownership, and founders often quote it loosely.
5. **Check size:** The dollar amount a fund invests in a single company.
   *Why it matters:* Founders use it to decide whether you can lead or only follow.
6. **Ownership target:** The percentage of a company a fund aims to hold after its investment.
   *Why it matters:* It's the discipline that connects your check size to your fund's math — without it you're guessing.
7. **Reserves:** Capital held back at the start to invest again in companies that are working.
   *Why it matters:* No reserves means you get diluted out of your winner exactly when it's proving itself.
8. **Pro rata:** The right to invest in later rounds to maintain your ownership percentage.
   *Why it matters:* It's the mechanism that lets a small early check keep mattering in a breakout.
9. **Power Law:** The pattern where one investment returns more than all the others combined.
   *Why it matters:* It explains nearly every investor behavior founders find confusing, including pressure to go bigger.
10. **Fund size:** Total capital committed by LPs, which determines what exit size actually matters.
    *Why it matters:* A $40M exit is a win for a $10M fund and a rounding error for a $200M one.
11. **2 and 20:** A 2% annual management fee to run the firm, plus 20% of profits as carried interest.
    *Why it matters:* Fees are paid regardless of performance, so pointing to carry signals you're serious about returns.
12. **DPI:** Distributed to Paid-In — the cash actually returned to investors, not paper value.
    *Why it matters:* Anyone can show a good TVPI from markups; DPI is the number that can't be argued with.

## Part 3 — Six practice prompts

1. *(founder)* "What are you optimizing for on this round — the cap, or getting the right people on the cap table?"
2. *(founder)* "What does the next 18 months buy you, and what has to be true for a Series A to be obvious?"
3. *(founder)* "Are you raising on a SAFE or pricing this round? What cap are you anchoring to and why that number?"
4. *(LP)* "When you look at emerging managers, are you weighting DPI from prior angel investments, or is it mostly thesis and access?"
5. *(LP)* "How do you think about fund size for a pre-seed manager — what's the ceiling where the math stops working for you?"
6. *(either)* "How much of the round is reserved for follow-on, and who's expected to lead the next one?"

## Part 4 — My venture vocabulary in plain English

I'm early in venture and I'd rather be precise than perform fluency. Here's how I think about it in
plain terms: a fund is a pool of money from investors called LPs, and my job is to decide which
startups it goes into and to make sure enough of it comes back. Venture returns follow a power law —
one company usually returns more than everything else combined — so the discipline is less about
avoiding bad companies than about making sure I own enough of the one that works, which is why
things like ownership targets and reserves matter more than they sound like they should. I focus on
AI and humanity at pre-seed to seed in the US, which means I'm usually underwriting a founder rather
than a spreadsheet. I'm learning the mechanics quickly and in public — I keep a working glossary and
notes at https://vc-lab.vercel.app — and I'd rather ask a direct question than nod along to a term
I only half understand.
