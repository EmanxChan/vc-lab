## Step researched: Distributing Proceeds

The step I was least familiar with is the final one — distributing proceeds. I understood how
a fund raises money and writes checks, but not how cash actually gets back to LPs. What I
learned is that it runs through a "distribution waterfall," a strict order of payment. First,
LPs get their invested capital returned. Second, they receive a preferred return (a hurdle
rate, industry standard around 8%). Only after both are satisfied does the GP earn carried
interest — typically a 20% share of profits, with the remaining 80% going to LPs. The big
structural choice is European vs. American waterfall: a European waterfall pays carry only
after the *entire fund* has returned all LP capital, while an American waterfall pays carry
deal-by-deal as each exit lands. European is LP-friendly and is the norm for emerging
managers; American gets the GP paid sooner but risks clawback if later deals fail. I also
learned the operational side matters more than I assumed — distributions require calculating
each partner's account, netting out fees, checking side letters, and communicating clearly
with LPs on a set timeline. Doing that cleanly is a visible signal of whether a GP is
professional or amateur, and it directly shapes whether LPs re-up in Fund II.
