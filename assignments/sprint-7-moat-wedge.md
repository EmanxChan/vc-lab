# Assignment — Your Moat Wedge

**Submitted:** July 2026
**Prompt:** Write 5 venture wedge statements for assistant-AI / AI co-working use cases, name the
exact founder harm each prevents, pick the top 2 and identify the buyer, then write "Wedge Ready."

> Reading note: I've taken "founder harm" to mean the concrete damage done to the operator or
> founder who *buys* the product — the person whose time, cash, risk, or team is on the line.

---

## Part 1 — Five wedge statements

1. We back **AI co-workers that run back-office operations for small clinics and practices** at
   pre-seed in the US to prevent owner-operators from spending their highest-value hours on
   insurance paperwork instead of patients.

2. We back **AI agents that capture and preserve institutional knowledge as people leave** at
   pre-seed in the US to prevent founders from re-buying knowledge they already paid for every time
   someone quits.

3. We back **AI co-working tools for deskless and frontline teams** at pre-seed in the US to prevent
   operators from losing their best frontline staff to preventable scheduling and communication chaos.

4. We back **AI that drafts and files regulated documentation** at pre-seed in the US to prevent
   founders from betting the company on a compliance filing a junior hire prepared at midnight.

5. We back **AI operations partners for solo and sub-ten-person teams** at pre-seed in the US to
   prevent founders from hiring a role they can't afford eight months before they can afford it.

## Part 2 — The exact harm, as a founder consequence

| # | Wedge | Harm prevented |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clinic back-office | The owner personally works 12–15 hours a week on claims and prior authorizations — time that isn't billable and isn't growth. Denied claims sit uncontested, so real revenue is written off. |
| 2 | Knowledge capture | A senior departure costs 3–6 months of a replacement's ramp, during which existing staff absorb the gap, get overloaded, and become the next people to quit. |
| 3 | Frontline co-working | Shift confusion and missed messages drive turnover in roles that cost real money to replace, and the churn lands hardest on the managers who then also leave. |
| 4 | Regulated documentation | One bad filing means fines, remediation cost, and a stalled deal or delayed launch — a cash and existential risk event, not an inconvenience. |
| 5 | AI ops partner for small teams | The founder either hires too early and burns runway on a salary the revenue can't support, or doesn't hire and personally becomes the bottleneck on every process. |

## Part 3 — Top two wedges: buyer and urgency

**Wedge 5 — AI operations partner for small teams**
*Who buys:* The founder or first ops hire at a 3–10 person company that has revenue but no
operational headcount. *Why they pay early:* Because the alternative purchase is a $90K salary they
can't justify yet — a few hundred dollars a month against that comparison is an easy yes, and the
pain is felt personally by the person holding the credit card.

**Wedge 1 — AI back-office for small clinics and practices**
*Who buys:* The owner-operator of an independent practice, or their practice manager.
*Why they pay early:* Because the ROI is arithmetic rather than aspiration — recovered claims and
reclaimed clinical hours are countable in the first month, and this buyer is already accustomed to
paying per-seat for billing software.

## Part 4 — Wedge Ready

- **Top wedge:** AI operations partners for solo and sub-ten-person teams — software that absorbs
  the operational work a company can't yet justify hiring for.
- **Target geo:** United States
- **Stage:** Pre-seed to Seed
- **Founder harm prevented:** Founders either burn runway on an operations hire the revenue can't
  support, or skip the hire and personally become the bottleneck on every process in the company.
  Both outcomes cost months and cash a company at this stage does not have.
