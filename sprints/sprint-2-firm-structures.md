## 2.1 — Venture Capital Structures
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/02/venture-capital-structures/ (`vcl.to/VI2-1`)

"A fund" is not one shape. There are four, and they trade control against diversification
against how much machinery you have to run.

| Structure | What it is | The trade |
|---|---|---|
| **Traditional fund** | Pooled LP capital across a portfolio | Diversified and credible; slow, expensive, 10-year commitment |
| **Venture builder** | A studio that creates companies rather than backing them | Maximum control and ownership; you are now an operator, not an investor |
| **Outsourced fund** | A platform (AngelList and similar) handles admin and back office | Low overhead, fast to start; less control, platform-shaped |
| **SPV** | One vehicle, one deal | Cheap, targeted, easy to explain; zero diversification |

The article's own example of a traditional fund: raise $200M, put $20M into each of 10 companies,
and one IPO exit returns $100M in profit.

**What stuck:** the SPV is the bridge I had not seen clearly. It sits between writing a personal
check and running a fund — one deal, its own vehicle, other people's money alongside mine, and no
ten-year obligation. If someone asks to come in on a deal I am already doing, that is the
structure for it.

**Relevance to my thesis:** I am an angel now and a fund later, so the useful question is what
occupies the middle. Answer from this session: SPVs first, then a small traditional fund once
there is a track record to point at. Venture builder is the wrong shape for me entirely — I want
to back founders, not become one again.

## 2.2 — Venture Firm Entities
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/02/venture-capital-entities/ (`vcl.to/VI2-2`)

Three entities, three jobs. The thing people call "a VC firm" is actually all three:

| Entity | Job | Gets paid by |
|---|---|---|
| **Management Company (ManCo)** | Owns brand & IP, employs staff, runs vendors and ops | Management fee |
| **General Partner (GP)** | Makes investment decisions, oversees the fund(s) | Carried interest |
| **The Fund** (an LP) | Collects LP capital, holds securities, calls capital | — it's the vehicle |

All three are typically **pass-through entities** — no tax at the entity level, income flows to
individual returns. Avoids double taxation on investment profits.

**What stuck:** the separation exists so that fee income, carry, and investor capital never sit
in the same pocket. It's structural discipline, not paperwork.

## 2.3 — Venture Capital Processes
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/02/venture-capital-processes/ (`vcl.to/VI2-3`)

Six steps across three processes:

**Create the fund**
1. **Write the thesis** — geography, sectors, strategy, stage, target size, name (4–6 weeks)
2. **Form the team** — partners you've known 5+ years; test commitment before offers
3. **Pitch LPs** — define LP archetypes, secure PACT commitment letters
4. **Complete fund formation** — at 10% or $1M committed: counsel, domicile, vendors, entities, capital calls

**Make an investment**

5. **Close a deal** — source to thesis, build conviction, IC, diligence, negotiate, sign, wire, set up updates

**Return the money**

6. **Distribute proceeds** — collect exit proceeds, determine carry status, calculate per
   partner accounts, net fees, check side letters, communicate, distribute on schedule
