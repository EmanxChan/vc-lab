## 3.1 — The Power Law
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/the-power-law-in-vc/ (`vcl.to/VI3-1`)

One investment returns more than all the others combined — often by orders of magnitude.
Rooted in Pareto's distribution work.

Consequences:
- Portfolios are built **expecting** most companies to fail or flatline
- The job is not avoiding losers, it's guaranteeing exposure to the one winner
- Drives the hunt for "Blue Ocean Deals" — untapped markets, strong early adoption
- Fund size and check size must be set so a single outlier can move the whole fund
- It's why access to hot deals is fought over so hard

**The reframe:** VC is not a diversification model wearing a risk costume. It's a concentrated
bet on identifying transformational companies, with diversification used only to buy more
lottery tickets in the same lottery.

## 3.2 — Venture Fund Economics
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/venture-fund-economics/ (`vcl.to/VI3-2`)

- **Management fee** — ~2%/yr of LP commitments. A $50M fund → $1M/yr. Covers salaries, rent,
  insurance, diligence, travel.
- **Carried interest** — 20% of profits. $20M profit → $4M GP / $16M LP.
- **Hurdle rate** — carry applies only above a preferred return (~8%), so LPs are made whole first.
- **Origin** — post-WWII, standardized by figures like Tom Perkins.

## 3.3 — Venture Fund Metrics
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/venture-fund-metrics (`vcl.to/VI3-3`)

| Metric | What it answers | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **MOIC** | What multiple on money in? | Ignores time entirely |
| **TVPI** | Total value (realized + paper) / paid in | Blends real and imaginary |
| **DPI** | How much cash actually came back? | The honest one |
| **RVPI** | How much is still in the ground? | Paper marks, self-reported |
| **IRR** | Annualized, time-weighted return | Flattered by early markups; gameable by timing |

**Rule of thumb:** TVPI shows the size of the outcome, DPI shows how much of it is real, IRR
shows how fast you got there. Never read IRR alone.
