## 1.3 — The Investment Process and Timeline
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-investment-process/ (`vcl.to/VI1-3`)

The process is a funnel, not a pipeline. Roughly **500 leads → 1–2 investments.**

1. **Sourcing** — networking, events, platforms, accelerators, incubators
2. **Due diligence** — business model, market, competition, financials, founding team
3. **Term sheet** — non-binding; amount, valuation, ownership, rights, preferences
4. **Documentation & close** — investment agreements, shareholder contracts, wire
5. **Post-investment support** — board seat, network access, help scaling
6. **Follow-on & exit** — later rounds, then IPO / M&A / secondary

**What stuck:** the filtering ratio. Conviction isn't the hard part — volume at the top of the
funnel is what makes conviction possible.

## 1.4 — Stages of VC Funding
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-funding-stages/ (`vcl.to/VI1-4`)

| Phase | Stages | What the money buys |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Angel, Pre-Seed, Seed | Prototype, first team, business plan, market research, early product |
| Growth | Series A, Series B | Team expansion, operational scale, new markets, sales & marketing infra |
| Late | Series C, D+ | Market leadership, international expansion, exit prep |
| Exit | M&A, IPO, secondary | Liquidity |

Series A runs "a few million to tens of millions"; Series C can reach hundreds of millions.

**Relevance to my thesis:** I'm at pre-seed/seed — the stage where the check funds *whether the
thing works at all*, not whether it scales. Diligence is mostly about the founder, because
there isn't much else yet.
