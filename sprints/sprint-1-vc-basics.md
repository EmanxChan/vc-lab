## 1.1 — Overview of the VC Industry
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/what-is-venture-capital/ (`vcl.to/VI1-1`)

Venture capital is a specialized form of private equity: capital from a fund is invested into
companies with significant growth potential. The money moves in three stages — **raise** from
investors, **deploy** into equity stakes, **exit** through an acquisition or IPO — and the firm
earns on the way through with a ~2% annual management fee and ~20% of profits as carry, with the
other 80% going back to LPs.

The number that reframes the job: **over 70% of venture deals never produce a substantial
return**, so the ones that work have to target 3–5x the whole fund to cover everything else.

VC is also explicitly not a money-only business. The firm is supposed to bring industry
expertise, network access, recruiting help, customer introductions, and someone to call when
things break. And it deliberately avoids "lifestyle businesses" — profitable, steady, no
exponential ceiling. Those are good companies; they are just not this asset class.

The article is unusually blunt about the industry's own record: aggressive tactics, a diversity
gap, thin diligence, inflated valuations, and a power imbalance with founders. VC Lab's whole
framing is a response to that.

**What stuck:** the 70% failure rate is not an accident of bad picking — it is the design. Every
other mechanic in this program (ownership targets, reserves, fund size) exists to survive it.

**Relevance to my thesis:** as an angel writing personal checks, I get the failure rate without
the fee income that cushions it. What I have instead is the non-capital half — the network,
introductions, and being someone a founder calls at 11pm. That is the part I can actually deliver
today, before I have a fund.

## 1.2 — Role of VC in Funding Startups
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/impacts-of-venture-capital/ (`vcl.to/VI1-2`)

Four levels of impact, from widest to narrowest:

- **Societal** — unicorns (companies valued over $1B) create jobs, wealth, and economic movement
- **Ecosystem** — capital pulls in talent, wins build reputation, reputation pulls in more of both
- **Innovation** — funding lets entrepreneurs attack problems incumbents have gotten comfortable ignoring
- **Startup** — capital plus guidance lets a company take a bigger swing than it could self-funded

The worked example is ride-hailing: five funded startups, the largest absorbs its competitors,
and the surviving company expands into carpooling, delivery, scooters, and prescriptions —
changing traffic and access along the way.

**What stuck:** the ecosystem loop is the one that matters to me, because it is the only one I
can stand inside. Talent follows wins, and wins follow talent. That is exactly what an alumni
network is — a compounding loop rather than a credential.

**Relevance to my thesis:** the article treats "impact" as a downstream side effect of returns.
My thesis puts it upstream — trust, bridges, and belonging are the filter, not the byproduct. The
honest tension is whether an upstream filter narrows the outlier set. That is the open question
in [thesis.md](../thesis.md), and this session is where it first shows up.

## 1.3 — The Investment Process and Timeline
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-investment-process/ (`vcl.to/VI1-3`)

The process is a funnel, not a pipeline. Roughly **500 leads → 1–2 investments.**

1. **Sourcing** — networking, events, platforms, accelerators, incubators
2. **Due diligence** — business model, market, competition, financials, founding team
3. **Term sheet** — non-binding; amount, valuation, ownership, rights, preferences
4. **Documentation & close** — investment agreements, shareholder contracts, wire
5. **Post-investment support** — board seat, network access, help scaling
6. **Follow-on & exit** — later rounds, then IPO / M&A / secondary

**What stuck:** the filtering ratio. Conviction isn't the hard part — volume at the top of the
funnel is what makes conviction possible.

## 1.4 — Stages of VC Funding
Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-funding-stages/ (`vcl.to/VI1-4`)

| Phase | Stages | What the money buys |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Angel, Pre-Seed, Seed | Prototype, first team, business plan, market research, early product |
| Growth | Series A, Series B | Team expansion, operational scale, new markets, sales & marketing infra |
| Late | Series C, D+ | Market leadership, international expansion, exit prep |
| Exit | M&A, IPO, secondary | Liquidity |

Series A runs "a few million to tens of millions"; Series C can reach hundreds of millions.

**Relevance to my thesis:** I'm at pre-seed/seed — the stage where the check funds *whether the
thing works at all*, not whether it scales. Diligence is mostly about the founder, because
there isn't much else yet.
