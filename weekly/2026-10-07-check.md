## 5. Draft — the ten-companies test

This is thesis.md's first pressure-test item and its own stated honest check. Every valuation below
is from reporting on the round itself, with the date, and I have flagged every place I am not
confident. Do not paste this anywhere before you have read the flags.

**First, the thing that breaks the August answer.** "The last five years," run from today, means
since October 2021. The five companies Set 2 found in August — Nextdoor, Discord, Patreon, Hive,
Sift — were all 2021 marks, and four of them have **aged out of the window**: Discord's $15B was
September 2021, Patreon's $4B and Hive's $2B were April 2021, Sift's was spring 2021. Only
Nextdoor is inside, by four weeks, and Nextdoor no longer holds its number — it is down roughly
75% from the $10 SPAC price, trading in the mid-$2s, with a market cap under $1B and a move from
the NYSE to the Nasdaq Capital Market this month. So the August answer does not survive its own
test anymore, and not because the thesis got worse.

**Second, what replaces it.** Eight companies that are inside the window, AI-native, US, and that
pass your own test — does this change how two people regard each other, or only how one party
regards a machine:

- **OpenEvidence** — $12B, Series D, January 2026 ($250M, co-led by Thrive and DST). Gives a
  clinician cited medical evidence at the point of care. The citation is a receipt a patient or a
  second opinion could actually check, which is exactly the line you drew for Glacis.
- **Abridge** — $5.3B, Series E, June 2025 ($300M, a16z). Turns the doctor–patient conversation
  into the clinical note. What it hands back is attention: the clinician looks at the person
  instead of the screen. This is the purest fit on the list.
- **Hippocratic AI** — $3.5B, Series C, November 2025 ($126M, Avenir Growth). Patient-facing AI
  that reaches out between visits. **This is the one I would argue with you about.** It may be
  exclusion 4 — a machine standing in for a human relationship rather than strengthening one. I
  put it in because the alternative for most of those patients is no contact at all, but it is a
  judgment call and it is yours.
- **Spring Health** — $3.3B, Series E, July 2024 ($100M, Generation Investment Management).
  Assesses someone and routes them to the right human clinician. The AI is the bridge, not the
  destination.
- **Headway** — $2.3B, Series D, July 2024 ($100M, Spark Capital). Matches patients to therapists
  who actually take their insurance. The receipt lands in the patient's hands as coverage.
  Secondary trackers disagree about a later round; the Series D number is the one corroborated by
  the firms on the deal, so it is the one I used.
- **Persona** — $2B, Series D, April 2025 ($200M, co-led by Founders Fund and Ribbit). Verifies
  that whoever is on the other end is a real, specific human. On an internet filling with agents,
  that is the precondition for two strangers trusting each other at all.
- **Ambience Healthcare** — $1.25B, Series C, July 2025 ($243M, Oak HC/FT and a16z). Same shape as
  Abridge, one rung down.
- **Incode** — $1.25B, Series B, December 2021 ($220M, General Atlantic and SoftBank). Identity
  verification. Inside the window by eight weeks. Flag: it has strong Mexico roots even though it
  is San Francisco-headquartered, so if you want the US filter applied strictly, cut it and the
  count is seven.

Two more that get to ten only with an asterisk, which I am naming rather than hiding: **Substack**
at $1.1B (July 2025, BOND and The Chernin Group) fits "community bonds" better than anything else
on this page and is not an AI company at all, so it fails the sector filter rather than the
anti-thesis. **Nextdoor** reached $4.3B in November 2021 and has given it all back.

**So: does the test pass?** Yes — eight clean, ten if you accept the two asterisks. The filter is
not too tight to support a fund. But the finding that matters is not the count.

**Five of the eight are healthcare.** Run honestly against the last five years, your thesis points
at the clinical encounter, not at community and social. That is uncomfortable for two reasons, and
both are worth sitting with. First, "bridges, community bonds, belonging" is the language of
`thesis.md`, and not one of the eight is a community product — the companies that actually cleared
$1B+ by changing how two people regard each other did it between a patient and a clinician.
Second, the 2021 community cohort is the group that de-rated: Discord from $15B to roughly $8.5B
on secondaries, Nextdoor down 75%. The AI-native "receipt in a human's hands" cohort is the group
that went up, and most of it went up in the last eighteen months.

**This also answers your open question**, the one about whether a trust-and-belonging filter
selects for or against outlier outcomes. In August the honest read was that it selects for a
slower shape of outcome, because the AI-native examples topped out around $1B to $4B. That is no
longer true. OpenEvidence went from $1B to $12B in eleven months. The ceiling you were worried
about was a 2021 ceiling.

**What I would not do with this.** I would not rewrite `thesis.md` to say "AI rebuilding human
trust in healthcare." That is me doing your thinking, it is a much narrower thesis than the one
you believe, and the five-year window is a backward-looking instrument — it tells you where the
outcomes landed, not where the next ones will. But the gap between what your thesis says and what
its own test returns is the most interesting thing in this notebook right now, and you should
either close it deliberately or write down why you are leaving it open.
