## 5. The nine Washington companies, screened

The 2026-09-02 run pulled nine WA companies with open pre-seed rounds out of SEC Form D filings and
stopped there, deliberately, because Form D does not say what a company does and guessing would have
been worse than silence. This week I went and found out. Sources are company sites, Crunchbase,
PitchBook, LinkedIn and press; I have marked what I could not confirm.

Then I ran each through your own test: **does this change how two people regard each other, or only
how one party regards a machine?**

### One survivor

**Senior Year, Inc. — CozyText.** Bellevue, incorporated 2024, $500,000 offered, $35,000 sold,
**$465,000 still open.** Diane Lansinger, sole officer, four-time founder, previously at Pioneer
Square Labs and SEEVA. She describes the work as AgeTech crossed with FamTech crossed with CareTech
— a texting product that keeps older adults and the people who love them in contact, in the medium
the family already uses rather than one the older person has to be taught.

That is not near your thesis, it is your thesis. Two people regard each other differently because
the product exists: a grandparent stays a participant in a family instead of becoming a logistics
problem, and an adult child gets a relationship back instead of a caregiving task. The AI is not the
substance — belonging is — which is exactly the shape your anti-thesis says to look for. It is
pre-seed, it is in the United States, it is thirty minutes from you, and the round has 93% of its
room still open.

She is also the sharpest possible test of the resurfaced sentence at the top of this report. A
four-time Seattle founder out of Pioneer Square Labs is somebody your networks should already know.

### Out on exclusion 1 — trust in machines, not between people

**Kumio, Inc.** Seattle, inc. 2025, $500,000 offered, $200,000 sold. Jinhee Kim (CEO, previously
Snapette and UYU) and Vangelis Stykas (CTO, the security researcher who backdoored a North Korean
operation and presented it at Black Hat in August). Agentic cybersecurity for AI data centres —
security agents covering planning, construction, commissioning and operations. Genuinely impressive
founders. It protects the machine; no human relationship changes. This is the Aira Security shape you
already rejected once, which is the good news: the filter gave the same answer twice without you
having to think about it.

**Opgate, Inc.** Richland, inc. 2026, $500,000 offered, $60,000 sold. Adam and Sarah Avenir, both
officers and directors. Sarah Avenir's public headline reads "Building runtime security for AI
agents," which puts this in the same bucket as Kumio. **Confidence flag:** I could not confirm that
the Form D entity and that description are the same product. `opgate.ai` is a procurement engine
built in Vilnius, Lithuania, and is almost certainly an unrelated namesake — I did not treat it as
evidence, and neither should you.

### Out on exclusion 2 — AI as method, not as substance

**Tursio, Inc.** Sammamish, inc. 2023, $500,000 offered, $50,000 sold. Alekh Jindal, ex-Microsoft
Gray Systems Lab, ex-Keebo CTO. Enterprise search and generative AI over existing databases. Swap
the AI for a better query planner and the company is unchanged in kind. **It also fails on stage
before it fails on thesis** — $1.47M already raised, Speciale Invest and Z5 Capital on the cap table,
26 employees. Not a pre-seed.

**Civarro, Inc.** Kirkland, inc. 2025, $500,000 offered, $50,000 sold. Salmeron Barnes and Patrick
Santos. Data centre energy management, still in stealth. Infrastructure, and the receipt never lands
in a human's hands.

### Not screened — I could not establish what they do

**Adaptive Logic, Inc.** Olympia, inc. 2026, $250,000 offered, $30,000 sold. Jordan Hughes and

**Vellora, Inc.** Seattle, inc. 2026, $1,300,000 offered, $78,000 sold, **$1,222,000 open** — the

### The two that test the boundary — your call, not mine

These are the interesting ones, and I would rather hand them to you undecided than force a verdict.

**MedServ Marketplace, Inc.** Sammamish, inc. 2025, $250,000 offered, $75,000 sold, **$175,000
open.** Viraj Shah, solo founder. A two-sided marketplace matching biomedical equipment technicians
and field service engineers with hospitals and manufacturers. It is human-to-human by construction —
it is literally a matching product for people — but the outcome metric a buyer would judge it on is
equipment uptime, which is exclusion 3 territory. My read is that it matches labour rather than
building a bond, so it is out. But it is close enough that how you argue it is worth more to your
thesis than the answer.

**NAMSOFT, Inc., trading as Plutus.** Kirkland, inc. 2025, $1,000,000 offered, $200,000 sold,
**$800,000 open.** Shashank Chiranewala, solo. Retail investors browse thematic portfolios from
independent research providers and mirror one automatically into their own brokerage account. This is
a trust intermediary between a person and a stranger whose judgment they are choosing to follow —
which is nearer your thesis than anything else on the list except CozyText. What makes me hesitate is
that the trust runs one way and is priced, which reads more like a marketplace for reputation than a
bond between people. If you disagree, say so, because that disagreement would sharpen exclusion 1
more than another rejection would.
