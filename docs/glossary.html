vc glossary

Venture capital vocabulary, defined plainly, added as I encounter it. Kept separate from my engineering glossary — different vocabulary, different job.

Deal Terms

2x Liquidation Preference

When the company is sold, investors get paid back two times their money first — before founders and employees see anything. A $5M investment with a 2x preference takes $10M off the top. Whatever's left is split among everyone else.

Above 1x is considered aggressive, and usually signals either a hot deal the investor wants downside protection on, or a weak negotiating position for the founder.

Ratchet (Full Ratchet Anti-Dilution)

Protects investors if the company later raises at a lower price. Their old shares are re-priced down to the new cheaper price, so they end up holding more shares. It preserves their ownership but dilutes founders and employees hard.

The softer, more common alternative is weighted-average anti-dilution, which adjusts partially rather than all the way down.

Term Sheet

The non-binding document outlining a proposed investment — amount, valuation, ownership percentage, rights, and preferences. Not the deal, but the blueprint the legal documents get built from.

Pro Rata Rights

The right to invest again in later rounds to maintain your ownership percentage. The main way early investors keep a meaningful stake in a winner.

Fund Structure

General Partner (GP)

The entity that makes investment decisions and oversees one or more funds. Receives carried interest. Composed of Managing Partners, General Partners, and Venture Partners.

Limited Partner (LP)

The investor in the fund — family offices, funds of funds, exited founders, tech executives, high-net-worth individuals. They commit capital but don't make investment decisions.

Management Company (ManCo)

The operating entity. Owns the brand and IP, employs staff, handles vendors, and receives the management fee. Distinct from both the GP entity and the fund itself.

The Fund

A limited partnership that collects capital from LPs and invests it, holding securities on behalf of investors. Typically a pass-through entity for tax purposes, as are the GP and ManCo — which avoids double taxation on investment profits.

PACT

A non-binding commitment letter used in VC Lab methodology. Signing it "hard circles" an LP as a serious prospective investor and moves them out of exploratory conversation.

Capital Call

The mechanism by which a fund draws down committed capital from LPs over time, rather than taking it all upfront. Governed by the terms of the LPA (Limited Partnership Agreement).

Economics

2 and 20

The standard compensation model: a 2% annual management fee on committed capital, plus 20% carried interest on profits. The fee runs the firm — salaries, diligence, travel. The carry is the upside.

The tension: fees are paid regardless of performance, so a large fund can pay its GPs well even if it never returns capital. Which is why LPs scrutinize fund size.

Carried Interest ("Carry")

The GP's share of fund profits, typically 20%. On $20M of profit, the GP takes $4M and LPs keep $16M. This is the line item that makes a fund manager wealthy — not the fee.

Hurdle Rate / Preferred Return

The minimum annual return LPs must receive before the GP earns any carry. Industry standard is around 8%, compounded.

Distribution Waterfall

The order in which money flows back out of a fund: LP capital returned first, then preferred return, then the carry split.

Two flavors — European (carry only after the whole fund returns all LP capital; LP-friendly, standard for emerging managers) and American (carry paid deal-by-deal; GP gets paid sooner, with clawback risk).

Performance Metrics

MOIC — Multiple on Invested Capital

The raw multiple on money invested. Simple, and completely ignores time.

TVPI — Total Value to Paid-In

Distributions plus remaining portfolio value, divided by capital paid in. The headline "how is this fund doing" number. Splits into DPI and RVPI.

DPI — Distributed to Paid-In

Cash actually returned to LPs, divided by capital paid in. The realized, no-arguing number.

RVPI — Residual Value to Paid-In

Remaining portfolio value divided by paid-in capital. Paper gains — what's still in the ground.

IRR — Internal Rate of Return

The annualized, time-weighted return. Lets LPs compare a VC fund against other asset classes. Flattered by early markups and sensitive to timing, so it's read alongside DPI — never alone.

The Power Law

The observation that one investment typically returns more than all others combined. Not a quirk — it's the organizing principle of the asset class. It dictates portfolio construction, fund size, check size, and what counts as a "good" company.
