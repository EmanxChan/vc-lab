1.3 — The Investment Process and Timeline

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-investment-process/ (vcl.to/VI1-3)

The process is a funnel, not a pipeline. Roughly 500 leads → 1–2 investments.

  1. Sourcing — networking, events, platforms, accelerators, incubators
  2. Due diligence — business model, market, competition, financials, founding team
  3. Term sheet — non-binding; amount, valuation, ownership, rights, preferences
  4. Documentation & close — investment agreements, shareholder contracts, wire
  5. Post-investment support — board seat, network access, help scaling
  6. Follow-on & exit — later rounds, then IPO / M&A / secondary

What stuck: the filtering ratio. Conviction isn't the hard part — volume at the top of the funnel is what makes conviction possible.

1.4 — Stages of VC Funding

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/06/14/vc-funding-stages/ (vcl.to/VI1-4)

PhaseStagesWhat the money buys
EarlyAngel, Pre-Seed, SeedPrototype, first team, business plan, market research, early product
GrowthSeries A, Series BTeam expansion, operational scale, new markets, sales & marketing infra
LateSeries C, D+Market leadership, international expansion, exit prep
ExitM&A, IPO, secondaryLiquidity

Series A runs "a few million to tens of millions"; Series C can reach hundreds of millions.

Relevance to my thesis: I'm at pre-seed/seed — the stage where the check funds whether the thing works at all, not whether it scales. Diligence is mostly about the founder, because there isn't much else yet.

2.2 — Venture Firm Entities

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/02/venture-capital-entities/ (vcl.to/VI2-2)

Three entities, three jobs. The thing people call "a VC firm" is actually all three:

EntityJobGets paid by
Management Company (ManCo)Owns brand & IP, employs staff, runs vendors and opsManagement fee
General Partner (GP)Makes investment decisions, oversees the fund(s)Carried interest
The Fund (an LP)Collects LP capital, holds securities, calls capital— it's the vehicle

All three are typically pass-through entities — no tax at the entity level, income flows to individual returns. Avoids double taxation on investment profits.

What stuck: the separation exists so that fee income, carry, and investor capital never sit in the same pocket. It's structural discipline, not paperwork.

2.3 — Venture Capital Processes

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/02/venture-capital-processes/ (vcl.to/VI2-3)

Six steps across three processes:

Create the fund

  1. Write the thesis — geography, sectors, strategy, stage, target size, name (4–6 weeks)
  2. Form the team — partners you've known 5+ years; test commitment before offers
  3. Pitch LPs — define LP archetypes, secure PACT commitment letters
  4. Complete fund formation — at 10% or $1M committed: counsel, domicile, vendors, entities, capital calls

Make an investment

  1. Close a deal — source to thesis, build conviction, IC, diligence, negotiate, sign, wire, set up updates

Return the money

  1. Distribute proceeds — collect exit proceeds, determine carry status, calculate per partner accounts, net fees, check side letters, communicate, distribute on schedule

Sessions: 3.1 The Power Law in VC · 3.2 Venture Fund Economics · 3.3 Venture Fund Metrics · 3.4 Venture Allocation Strategies

3.1 — The Power Law

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/the-power-law-in-vc/ (vcl.to/VI3-1)

One investment returns more than all the others combined — often by orders of magnitude. Rooted in Pareto's distribution work.

Consequences:

  • Portfolios are built expecting most companies to fail or flatline
  • The job is not avoiding losers, it's guaranteeing exposure to the one winner
  • Drives the hunt for "Blue Ocean Deals" — untapped markets, strong early adoption
  • Fund size and check size must be set so a single outlier can move the whole fund
  • It's why access to hot deals is fought over so hard

The reframe: VC is not a diversification model wearing a risk costume. It's a concentrated bet on identifying transformational companies, with diversification used only to buy more lottery tickets in the same lottery.

3.2 — Venture Fund Economics

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/venture-fund-economics/ (vcl.to/VI3-2)

  • Management fee — ~2%/yr of LP commitments. A $50M fund → $1M/yr. Covers salaries, rent, insurance, diligence, travel.
  • Carried interest — 20% of profits. $20M profit → $4M GP / $16M LP.
  • Hurdle rate — carry applies only above a preferred return (~8%), so LPs are made whole first.
  • Origin — post-WWII, standardized by figures like Tom Perkins.

3.3 — Venture Fund Metrics

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/09/venture-fund-metrics (vcl.to/VI3-3)

MetricWhat it answersWatch out for
MOICWhat multiple on money in?Ignores time entirely
TVPITotal value (realized + paper) / paid inBlends real and imaginary
DPIHow much cash actually came back?The honest one
RVPIHow much is still in the ground?Paper marks, self-reported
IRRAnnualized, time-weighted returnFlattered by early markups; gameable by timing

Rule of thumb: TVPI shows the size of the outcome, DPI shows how much of it is real, IRR shows how fast you got there. Never read IRR alone.

4.3 — Sourcing and Closing LPs

Source: https://govclab.com/2023/08/16/sourcing-and-closing-limited-partners/ (vcl.to/VI4-3)

The LP construction model (example: $10M fund)

Check sizeCommitments neededTypical archetype
$1M2Family offices, funds of funds, corporate VC
$500K6Exited founders, tech executives
$250K12HNWIs, bankers, larger VC firms
$100K20Friends, alumni networks, angels

Initial close typically captures 10–25% of target fund size.

The funnel

Source → Connector intro → initial meeting → pitch → materials → PACT signed → diligence → legal review → agreement signed → capital call. Eleven steps total.

The PACT is the checkpoint that matters: a non-binding commitment letter that hard-circles an LP as serious. Ask for it the moment genuine interest appears.

Note to self

"Identifying real LPs requires searching with a combination of less obvious keywords" — the obvious searches surface the same over-solicited names everyone else found.

Questions to bring to the live sessions

  • ○What's the realistic first-close number for a first-time manager in the US?
  • ○How early is too early to ask for a PACT?

Sprint 5 — Venture Dealsprep only

Sessions: Decile Hub Dealflow · Sourcing Deals · Creating Deal Memos · Market Analysis

Not yet covered in the live curriculum. Prep notes and my own motion below.

My agent-assisted motion

Three agents, one for each stage of the deal lifecycle. Written during Sprint 1 (Thesis Wedge Selection) and kept here because this is where they actually get built.

1. Sourcing — an agent that watches alumni activity across Plug and Play, Grid110, and Material Change to flag trust-focused founders before they start fundraising. Why it works: this is my edge made mechanical. The alumni signal exists whether or not I'm watching it; the agent means I don't miss it.

2. Diligence — an agent that adversarially tests a startup's misinformation or trust product. If a company claims to rebuild trust, the fastest read is to try to break it.

3. Support — an agent that routes portfolio founders to alumni mentors and tracks user trust signals after investment, like churn or complaint spikes. Why it works: trust erosion shows up in support tickets before it shows up in revenue.

What this sprint should give me

  • A repeatable sourcing motion for pre-seed AI founders in the US
  • A deal memo template I can write in under 90 minutes
  • A market analysis approach that works when there's no revenue to analyze

Deal memo skeleton (draft — refine during the sprint)

  1. The one-liner — what they do, in the founder's words
  2. Why now — what changed in the world to make this possible
  3. Team — why these people, specifically
  4. Market — size, and more importantly, shape
  5. Thesis fit — does it actually rebuild trust, or am I stretching?
  6. The bet — the single thing that has to be true
  7. Terms & ownership — entry price, check size
  8. Anti-memo — the strongest case against, written honestly

Open questions

  • ○How do deal memos differ at pre-seed vs. Series A?
  • ○As an angel rather than a GP, what does my diligence process realistically look like?
  • ○Which of the three agents is worth building first?
