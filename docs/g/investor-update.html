Investor update

The regular written update to existing investors — usually monthly or quarterly — covering metrics, progress, problems, and asks.

Picture this: a founder sends a short update on the fifth of every month for two years. When they raise the next round, the investors already know the story, and two of them make introductions without being asked.

Why it matters

The update is the cheapest fundraising tool a founder has, and the most consistently skipped. Investors who have watched the numbers move for eighteen months make decisions faster than ones being told the story for the first time.

It's also a discipline. Writing down what was supposed to happen and what did, every month, is how a founder notices a trend that a dashboard hides. The updates that only appear when money is needed are the ones that tell an investor exactly what they need to know about the founder.

What it looks like in practice

The good ones are short and honest: the numbers that matter, what happened, what went wrong, and one or two specific asks. Specific asks are the part that generates help — "introductions welcome" produces nothing; "we need a head of clinical ops, ideally from a multi-site group" produces candidates.

Consistency beats polish. Monthly for two years builds a track record; sporadic beautiful updates build nothing.

Where it goes wrong

  • Only writing when things are going well, which makes silence a signal.
  • Burying bad news, which destroys trust exactly when you need help.
  • Vague asks that nobody can act on.
  • Making it so long that nobody reads it.
