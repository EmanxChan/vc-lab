deal memo

Eight sections, writable in ninety minutes, including the case against.

How to use this. Run vc memo "Company Name" to scaffold a memo in deals/. Write it in under 90 minutes. If it takes longer, the thinking isn't clear yet — that's information too.

The eight sections

1. The one-liner

What they do, in the founder's own words. If you can't write it in one sentence, you don't understand it yet.

2. Why now

What changed in the world that makes this possible today and not three years ago. A company with no "why now" is usually a feature waiting for a platform shift that already happened.

3. Team

Why these people. Do they have founder-market fit, or did they pick this market? Have they worked together before — 68.6% of multi-founder unicorns had co-founders who previously worked or studied together.

4. Market

Size, and more importantly shape. Who is the buyer, what do they currently spend on this, and how hard is it to reach them.

5. Thesis fit

Does this actually rebuild human trust — bridges, community bonds, belonging — or am I stretching because I like the founder? Be strict. This is the section that stops the portfolio drifting.

6. The bet

The single thing that has to be true for this to work. One sentence. If there are three, the company hasn't focused yet.

7. Terms and ownership

Instrument, cap or price, check size, resulting ownership, and whether pro rata is available.

8. The anti-memo

The strongest possible case against, written honestly, as if I were paid to kill it. What would a smart skeptic say? What would make me wrong? If I can't write this, I have enthusiasm rather than conviction.

The anti-memothe section people skip

Write the strongest possible case against the investment, honestly, as if you were being paid to kill it. Three reasons it matters:

  • Investors read pitches by elimination by aspects — hunting for the fatal flaw. If you haven't found it, someone else will, and later.
  • It's the difference between conviction and enthusiasm. If you can't articulate the bear case, you don't understand the company yet.
  • When a deal goes wrong, the anti-memo tells you whether you were unlucky or wrong. Only one of those is fixable.
