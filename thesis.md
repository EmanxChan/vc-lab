# Thesis

**AI that rebuilds human trust · Pre-seed · United States · Angel now, fund later**

## The thesis

I write pre-seed angel checks into US AI startups rebuilding trust. In a world where
information, connection, and trust keep getting more volatile, human connection and technology
built well can be the antidote — AI that builds bridges, strengthens community bonds, and
fosters belonging rather than eroding them.

## The path

**Angel first, fund second.** I'm writing personal checks now to build a real track record and
learn the craft from the inside. Launching a fund comes after there's evidence — deals I
actually got into, founders who'd vouch for me, and a point of view that's been tested rather
than asserted.

That order matters for how I evaluate everything else in this program: fund mechanics are
things I'm learning *toward*, not operating today.

## My edge

I'm an alum of **Plug and Play**, **Grid110**, and **Material Change** — three ecosystems where
founders building trust and community technology already know me. That means I see pre-seed
deals in this space before they're shopped widely.

The access compounds, because alumni networks route dealflow to people who stay engaged after
the program ends, not just to people who once participated.

## The three variables

| Variable | Position |
|---|---|
| **Sector** | AI rebuilding human trust — bridges, community bonds, belonging |
| **Stage** | Pre-seed |
| **Geography** | United States |
| **Vehicle** | Personal angel checks today; a fund once there's a track record |

## The anti-thesis

A thesis that can't say no isn't a filter, it's a preference. This is what looks like a fit and
isn't — derived from the 29 companies I actually screened, not invented at a desk.

### 1. Trust in machines, not between people

The most common near-miss. Plenty of good companies make AI systems safer, auditable, or harder to
attack. That is trust in a *system*, and it is not the same as trust *between people*.

The line I draw: does the product make a machine's behaviour legible and accountable to the humans
it affects, or does it just protect the machine? Aira Security stops attacks on AI agents — no
human relationship changes, so it's out. Glacis produces a receipt an auditor, a patient, or a
rejected applicant could actually rely on — the person on the receiving end gains something, so
it stays in.

That boundary is narrow and I should expect to defend it in every direction.

### 2. AI as method, not as substance

If you could swap the AI for a better algorithm and the company is unchanged in kind, the AI was a
tool, not the thesis. Every biotech in the batch failed here — Pauling, Sift, Clockwork, Sigma,
Motley, Polybiomics, ImYoo. Good science, and the trust question never enters.

Same for hardware and industrial: Aerovy, FlyX, Spacedock, Reflection Robotics, Nexxa.

### 3. Efficiency dressed as connection

If the outcome metric in the pitch is time or cost saved, it is an efficiency company, and
efficiency is orthogonal to trust. Ally AI, Nitecapp, Codemod, CodeChimp, Expanso, Lod, ElastixAI.
Some touch humans; none change how humans regard each other.

### 4. AI that spends trust rather than builds it

The exclusion that proves the filter works, because these are the hardest to reject — AI, US,
pre-seed, human-facing, and still wrong.

**InfinityLoop** uses generative AI to negotiate harder against your suppliers. **Vicino** generates
on-brand enterprise content at scale. Both are competent companies. Both make the world slightly
more adversarial and slightly more synthetic, which is the condition the thesis exists to answer.

If I can't say no to these two, the thesis means nothing.

### Two nos that are not about the thesis

Worth separating, because they are "right company, wrong fit for me" rather than "wrong company."

- **Stage.** Past a priced seed, I'm not the right money. ElastixAI at $18M, Nexxa at $14M,
  Shyld at $14M, Myriad at $7.8M, Casium at $5M.
- **Geography.** US only. Cygeniq sells into APAC and the Middle East.

### The test

> Does this change how two people regard each other, or only how one party regards a machine?

Two people is thesis. One party and a machine is infrastructure — and infrastructure is only in
scope when the receipt lands in a human's hands.

### What would make this wrong

If this anti-thesis is right and I still can't name ten US companies from the last five years that
fit it and reached $1B+, then the filter is too tight to support a fund, and one of the two has to
give. That test is the first item below, and it is the honest check on everything written here.

## The open question

Does a trust-and-belonging filter select for or against outlier outcomes? Trust infrastructure
can be slower-compounding and harder to price than pure tooling. As an angel this matters less —
I'm not solving for fund math yet. It becomes the central question the moment I size Fund I.
